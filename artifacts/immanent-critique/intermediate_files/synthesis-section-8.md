## Conclusion

Part I traced a genealogy that Finlayson (2014) warns against reading as a single line. The review has kept that warning in view, and the evidence supports it. Hegel's criterion-testing already contains two senses of the term (Becker 2018). Marx's inheritance divides into categorial, social-historical and moral readings (Postone 1993; Geras 2020). The first generation turns determinate negation on reason itself and so generates the normative problem that Habermas pressed. The post-1970 theorists then answer that problem in incompatible ways. What persists is a family of commitments: standards drawn from the object, a role for contradiction, and an orientation toward transformation. Procyshyn (2019) draws the corresponding conclusion that immanent critique is not a method in any strict sense, since each of these commitments admits more than one interpretation.

The four axes show where the positions part company. On the source of standards (a), the candidates are norms embedded in practice (Honneth, Walzer), the presuppositions of communication (Habermas), the failures of a form of life (Jaeggi), the form of the critical practice (Särkelä 2017), social contradiction (Fraser, von Samson) and experienced wrongness (Freyenhagen). On contradiction (b), it is central for Marx, Adorno, Fraser and von Samson (2025), though it means class antagonism, non-identity, crisis tendency and clarified contradiction respectively. Honneth's reconstruction replaces it with a "surplus of validity" (Repa 2023), and the realist ideology critics give it no evident role. On the reconstructive/transformative axis (c), Habermas and Honneth are reconstructive, while Adorno, Jaeggi, Kompridis and Allen are transformative or disclosive. Repa's defence of Honneth shows that the line is itself contested. On progress (d), the objection is raised most sharply against the reconstructive variants (Kim 2022; Solinas 2025; Agatensi 2025). The problem-solving responses face a dilemma between objectivism and progressivism (Wiersma 2026), and Chen (2025) disputes their scientific analogy.

Several problems remain open. The first is the dilemma Fraser (2023) and von Samson (2025) formulate: if embedded norms carry authority, critique is conservative, and if they do not, it loses its normative bite. No response has dissolved it. Relocating immanence to contradiction, practice or experience, accepting hybrids, and rejecting the demand for foundations (Evans 2022; Han-Pile 2016) each leave a cost. The second is whose context counts. Radhakrishnan (2025) and Stahl (2024) argue that the practices supplying the standards may already be shaped by exclusion, which puts the choice of standards on a political footing. The third is isolation. The analytic literatures on internal reasons and reflective equilibrium (Williams 1981; Daniels 1996) and on fact-sensitivity (Cohen 2008) share the structure of the dilemma, but they make little contact with the Frankfurt debates. Outside philosophy, the term often travels without a definition. A limit of this review is that it reaches several core texts only through secondary literature: Allen's *The End of Progress*, Horkheimer's essay on traditional and critical theory, and Adorno's major works. The German-language literature is also absent from the bibliography.

The literature suggests three directions. One is hybrid external-immanent critique (Bodde 2020; Cozzaglio 2020), which begins from the immanent side and adds external elements, in contrast to Forst's (2011) context-transcending ground. Another is pluralism about methods, which Radhakrishnan (2025) recommends and which fits Carleheden's (2020) comparison of reconstruction, construction and genealogy. The third is to treat critique as a practice of agents and not a procedure of theorists, as in Celikates (2018) and Särkelä (2017). These proposals also bear on the unsettled definition: if immanence is a matter of the form of practice and not the location of a standard, the questions of conservatism and progress would take a different shape.
