# Literature Review Plan: Immanent Critique from Hegel to the Present

## Research Idea Summary

A two-part review of immanent critique as idea and method. Part I gives a chronological genealogy from Hegel's determinate negation through Marx, the first-generation Frankfurt School, Habermas, and Honneth, to Jaeggi, Stahl and others. Part II maps the main contemporary debates: the sources of critical normativity, the conservatism objection, contradiction, ideology critique, context-transcendence, progress and decolonial/feminist challenges, and uses beyond philosophy.

## Key Research Questions

1. How did immanent critique develop historically, and which continuities and ruptures mark the line from Hegel to the present?
2. How should immanent critique be distinguished from internal and external critique, and is the distinction stable?
3. Where does immanent critique get its normative standards, and can it escape the conventionalism/conservatism objection?
4. What role do contradiction, crisis and determinate negation play, and is critique reconstructive, transformative, or both?
5. How does immanent critique relate to ideology critique, to Adorno's "wrong life" problem and to context-transcendence?
6. Can immanent critique survive the critique of progress and Eurocentrism (Allen, decolonial, feminist)? Can it be used outside philosophy?

## Literature Review Domains

Domains 1-3 form the chronological overview (Part I). Domains 4-7 cover the contemporary debates (Part II). Domains will be researched sequentially, so each is scoped to stand alone and to avoid overlap.

### Domain 1: Hegelian and Left-Hegelian Origins (Hegel, Young Hegelians, Marx)

**Focus**: Determinate negation, the Phenomenology's "immanent" method (the Introduction's criterion-testing, the experience of consciousness), Hegel's critique of Kant's external critique and of the Logic's immanent development. Then the Left-Hegelian reception: Feuerbach, the early Marx ("ruthless criticism of everything existing"), the critique of political economy, Capital's method, and ideology as a Marxian theme.

**Key Questions**:
- What exactly is "immanent" in Hegel's method? Do the criteria arise from consciousness itself (the Phenomenology), or from conceptual self-development (the Logic)?
- Is determinate negation a logical or a historical-social operation?
- Is Marx's critique of political economy properly immanent? How do the readings of Capital divide (Postone, Heinrich, Reichelt, Backhaus, Arthur, Ollman, Cohen, Wood's "external" reading)?
- Does Marx rely on an external moral standard (the Wood/Husami/Geras debate on Marx and justice)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Hegel; Hegel's Dialectics; Marx; Karl Marx on justice/exploitation; Ideology; Critical Theory), IEP; PhilPapers categories on Hegel's Phenomenology, Marx's Capital, Marx on morality.
- Key terms: "determinate negation", "Hegel immanent critique", "Phenomenology of Spirit method", "criterion", "Hegel Kant critique external", "Marx immanent critique", "critique of political economy method", "Marx justice exploitation", "new Marx-reading / Neue Marx-Lektüre", "Left Hegelians critique", "Marx ideology critique".
- Authors to anchor: Hegel; Pippin; Forster; Pinkard; Taylor; Maker; Redding; Stern; Fulda; Rosen; Postone; Reichelt; Heinrich; Wood; Cohen; Geras; Lukes; Sayers; Arthur; Brudney; Leopold.
- Expected papers: 15-20.

**Relevance**: Establishes the conceptual DNA (determinate negation, standards generated from the object, contradiction) that all later versions inherit or revise.

### Domain 2: First-Generation Critical Theory (Lukacs, Horkheimer, Adorno, Marcuse, Benjamin)

**Focus**: The programmatic distinction between traditional and critical theory; Lukacs's reification and totality as precursors; Adorno's negative dialectics, constellations, "immanent critique" in "Cultural Criticism and Society", the Dialectic of Enlightenment and the self-undermining of reason; Marcuse's immanent standards and one-dimensionality; Benjamin's constellational and redemptive critique. Also the Frankfurt School's use of immanent critique in aesthetics and culture critique.

**Key Questions**:
- What does Adorno mean by immanent critique, and how does it relate to negative dialectics and non-identity?
- How should we read "wrong life cannot be lived rightly" (Minima Moralia)? Does it undermine the possibility of any standpoint, or does it leave a negative normative basis?
- Does the Dialectic of Enlightenment's totalizing diagnosis (the performative contradiction charged by Habermas) leave room for immanent critique?
- How do the first generation's accounts of ideology and of immanent transcendence differ (Adorno, Marcuse, Horkheimer)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Critical Theory; Adorno; Frankfurt School; Walter Benjamin; Herbert Marcuse; Georg Lukacs), IEP; PhilPapers categories on Adorno and on Critical Theory.
- Key terms: "Adorno immanent critique", "negative dialectics", "non-identity", "Adorno ideology critique", "wrong life", "Minima Moralia ethics", "Dialectic of Enlightenment performative contradiction", "Horkheimer traditional and critical theory", "Marcuse one-dimensional immanent", "Benjamin critique constellation", "Lukacs reification".
- Authors to anchor: Adorno; Horkheimer; Marcuse; Benjamin; Lukacs; Jay; Bernstein (J. M.); Finlayson; Freyenhagen; Cook; Honneth; Wellmer; Menke; O'Connor; Hammer; Rush; Geuss; Bernstein; Jarvis; Gordon; Buck-Morss; Schweppenhauser.
- Expected papers: 15-20.

**Relevance**: Provides the main twentieth-century articulation, the "wrong life" problem and the totalization worry that structure later debates.

### Domain 3: From Habermas to Contemporary Frankfurt Reconstructive Critique (Habermas, Honneth, Benhabib, Fraser, Walzer)

**Focus**: Habermas's paradigm shift to communicative action and rational reconstruction (the Theory of Communicative Action, Knowledge and Human Interests, the "critique of ideology" as depth hermeneutics, the "unfinished project" of modernity); Benhabib's "critique, norm, and utopia" and "anticipatory-utopian" critique; Honneth's recognition-based normative reconstruction (Disrespect, Struggle for Recognition, Freedom's Right, Reification) and the Fraser-Honneth debate; Fraser's critique of recognition and her "critical theory of crisis" (Capitalism: A Conversation); Walzer's connected criticism as the parallel communitarian/hermeneutic strand.

**Key Questions**:
- How do Habermas and Honneth ground immanent critique, in the pragmatic presuppositions of speech and in institutionalized recognition respectively, and does this ground suffice?
- What separates "reconstruction" (Honneth) from "critique" in the Hegelian sense, and what is the status of the "normative reconstruction" in Freedom's Right?
- Does Walzer's connected criticism differ substantively from Habermasian reconstruction (the Walzer-Habermas and Walzer-Rawls contrasts)?
- How do Fraser and Benhabib respond (the "perspectival dualism", the "surplus of validity", "transcendence from within")?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Habermas; Axel Honneth; Critical Theory; Feminist Political Philosophy; Recognition; Social Criticism if available); PhilPapers categories on Habermas, Honneth, Social and Political Philosophy; NDPR reviews of Honneth, Habermas, Fraser, Benhabib.
- Key terms: "Habermas rational reconstruction", "Habermas critique ideology", "Honneth normative reconstruction", "Freedom's Right critique", "Honneth Fraser redistribution recognition", "Benhabib critique norm utopia", "Walzer connected critic", "Walzer interpretation social criticism", "immanent transcendence", "social criticism internal".
- Authors to anchor: Habermas; Honneth; Benhabib; Fraser; Walzer; McCarthy; Bohman; Forst; Deranty; Zurn; Schaub; Thompson; Hartmann; Laitinen; Bernstein (R.); Kompridis; Browne; Cooke; Rush; Celikates.
- Expected papers: 12-18.

**Relevance**: Completes the genealogical overview and links directly to the normativity and "context-transcendence" debates.

### Domain 4: Contemporary Theories of Immanent Critique (Jaeggi, Stahl, Celikates, and the taxonomy of critique)

**Focus**: The analytical re-articulation since about 2000: Jaeggi's "Rethinking Ideology" and Kritik von Lebensformen (problem-solving, crises, "immanent critique" of forms of life), Stahl's Immanente Kritik (Elemente einer Theorie sozialer Praktiken) and his work on the three types (internal, immanent, external) and the "social practice" account of immanent critique; Celikates's critique of critique and "critical social theory" (Kritik als soziale Praxis); Sabia, Rosa, Sorg, Jaeggi and Celikates's Sozialphilosophie introduction; Ng, Antonio, Buchwalter, Boltanski (the sociology of critique), Boltanski and Thevenot, Lukes, Geuss's "Idea of a Critical Theory". Includes the taxonomy: immanent vs. internal vs. external/transcendent critique (Stahl, Jaeggi, Cooke, Rush, Sabia, Hammer) and "critique as problem-solving" (Jaeggi) versus "negation" (Adorno).

**Key Questions**:
- How can the three-way distinction (immanent / internal / external) be defined precisely, and are the boundaries stable or only gradual (see Stahl's "What Is Immanent Critique?", Jaeggi, Sabia, Ng)?
- Are there strong (revolutionary, "transformative") and weak (reconstructive, "rehabilitative") versions? Is Jaeggi's crisis/problem-solving model sufficiently immanent?
- What is the distinctive immanent critique of social practices, rules and norms (Stahl on constitutive norms, Brandom-style pragmatism, Wittgensteinian rule-following)?
- How does Celikates's emphasis on reflexive social practice, and on critical theory without a hegemonic observer, alter the account of critique?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: PhilPapers (Immanent critique; Critical Theory; Social Philosophy); Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex searches for the core articles (Stahl 2013 "Was ist Immanente Kritik?", Stahl 2013 Immanente Kritik, Jaeggi 2009/2014 Kritik von Lebensformen, Jaeggi 2009 "Rethinking Ideology", Celikates 2009 and 2018, Ng 2015 "Immanent Critique and..." and Ng's Critique and Politics, Sabia 2010 "Defending Immanent Critique", Cooke, Antonio 1981 "Immanent Critique as the Core of Critical Theory", Buchwalter ed. Hegel and Critical Theory).
- Key terms: "immanent critique", "internal critique", "external critique", "immanent vs internal critique", "Stahl immanent critique", "Jaeggi critique of forms of life", "Celikates critique as social practice", "Boltanski sociology of critique", "social criticism method", "critical theory methodology", "Sozialphilosophie".
- Use search_sep.py and s2_search.py with "--recent" flag for work since 2018.
- Expected papers: 15-20.

**Relevance**: Core of the user's second request; supplies the conceptual apparatus for the later debates and covers the most recent literature.

### Domain 5: The Normativity, Justification, and Conservatism Debate; Internal Critique in Analytic Philosophy

**Focus**: Objections that immanent critique lacks independent normative authority, merely reproduces existing norms (conservatism/conventionalism), or smuggles in external standards covertly; responses (the "ideal vs. actual" reading, immanent transcendence, "surplus of validity", constitutive norms, the "normative truth" of practices). Linked analytic literature: internal and external reasons (Williams), internal criticism in Wittgensteinian/Rortyan thought, Walzer vs. Dworkin vs. MacIntyre, Raz's "internal critique", Rorty on contingency, Brandom's expressive rationality, Geuss, "justificatory strategy" debates, Forst's justification-based critique, Habermas vs. Foucault, Cohen's "Facts and Principles", Rawls's reflective equilibrium and the "method of internal critique", Cohen vs. Williams on "realism".

**Key Questions**:
- Is the conservatism objection decisive (Habermas, Honneth and Fraser on Adorno; Geuss; Sabia; Finlayson; Ng)?
- Can the immanence requirement be combined with universalist or transcendental justification (Forst, Benhabib)? Is there a "dilemma" of internal vs. external standards?
- How does the debate relate to analytic discussions of internal reasons, reflective equilibrium, constructive interpretation and disagreement?
- Is "genuine" immanence possible when a social formation contains competing or ideologically distorted norms (the "which norms?" and "norm selection" problem)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Reflective Equilibrium; Reasons for Action: Internal vs. External; Justification; Political Realism; Normative Ethics Methodology), PhilPapers categories on social criticism and metaethics of critique; search for critiques by Geuss, Finlayson ("Habermas and Rorty"/"Contexts of Justification"), Sabia, Rosen, Honneth.
- Key terms: "conservatism objection immanent critique", "conventionalism social criticism", "internal criticism", "justification critique normativity", "Walzer MacIntyre criticism", "Rorty ethnocentrism", "Williams internal reasons critical theory", "immanent critique justification problem", "normative foundations critical theory", "political realism legitimacy critique", "Rawls reflective equilibrium internal critique".
- Authors to anchor: Walzer; Raz; Williams; Geuss; Forst; Rosen; Dworkin; Rorty; Brandom; Sabia; Finlayson; Allen; Cohen; Celikates; Stahl; Ng; Jaeggi; Ypi; Sleat; Fine.
- Expected papers: 12-18.

**Relevance**: Addresses the central objection; deliberately supplies the strongest sceptical and external-critique positions.

### Domain 6: Contradiction, Ideology Critique, and Immanent Transcendence

**Focus**: How immanent critique operates: the status of contradiction (logical vs. real/social, antagonism vs. crisis), determinate negation as method in contemporary social theory, the link to ideology critique (Jaeggi, Haslanger, Shelby, Celikates, Geuss, Eagleton, Zizek, Stanley's "How Propaganda Works"; "ideological contradiction" as immanent critique in Stahl's accounts), the transformative vs. reconstructive character, crisis theory (Fraser, Jaeggi, Rosa, Postone), immanent transcendence (Bloch, Marcuse, Benhabib) and utopia; Adorno's "wrong life" and "damaged life" in relation to ethics.

**Key Questions**:
- Is ideology critique a species of immanent critique, or are they distinct (Stahl, Jaeggi, Haslanger)? How do contemporary social-epistemological accounts of ideology (Haslanger, Celikates, Shelby, Mills) bear on it?
- What does "contradiction" mean in social theory (real contradictions, performative contradictions, practical-normative contradictions), and does immanent critique require a dialectical logic?
- How can critique both be immanent and transcend its context ("immanent transcendence")? How does it avoid the voluntarist (external) alternative and the fatalist (determinist) one?
- Does the "wrong life" thesis (Adorno; Freyenhagen's "Adorno's Practical Philosophy"; Gordon; Bernstein; Finlayson) show limits of immanent critique or ground a negativist ethics?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Ideology; Critical Theory; Adorno; Dialectical Thinking), PhilPapers categories for Ideology, Adorno's ethics, Hegelian Marxism; Semantic Scholar searches for Haslanger, Jaeggi, Celikates, Freyenhagen.
- Key terms: "ideology critique immanent", "ideological contradiction", "real contradiction Marx Hegel", "performative contradiction", "crisis theory critical theory", "immanent transcendence", "Adorno wrong life", "Adorno negative ethics", "Freyenhagen Adorno", "Haslanger ideology critique", "Shelby ideology", "damaged life".
- Expected papers: 12-18.

**Relevance**: Connects the formal account to the substantive tasks (diagnosing domination, ideology) and treats Adorno's problem directly.

### Domain 7: Progress, Decolonial and Feminist Challenges, and Applications Beyond Philosophy

**Focus**: (a) The critique of progress and Eurocentrism: Amy Allen's The End of Progress (and Allen's Critique on the Couch, reception and replies from Honneth, Habermas, Jaeggi, Forst; Chakrabarty, Said, Mignolo, Spivak, Fanon, Quijano; "provincializing" critical theory; Dussel; Mbembe; Mills; Gordon; Celikates and Bhambra; the "colonial unconscious" and Foucauldian genealogy as alternatives); feminist and queer critiques (Fraser, Butler, Benhabib, Haslanger, Cooke; "Hegelian feminism"); (b) uses of immanent critique outside philosophy: critical social science (Boltanski, Bourdieu, critical realism), critical legal studies (Unger, Koskenniemi, Kennedy, Cover; "internal critique" in jurisprudence; Dworkin vs. Critical Legal Studies), education (critical pedagogy, Freire, Biesta), organization studies, critical management studies, STS, political theory/international relations (Cox, Linklater); cultural critique; AI/technology critique as most recent application.

**Key Questions**:
- Does the Hegelian-Marxian line presuppose a developmental or progressive philosophy of history, and can immanent critique be decoupled from progress (Allen, Honneth, Jaeggi's "retrospective learning processes", Forst's "normative constructivism")?
- What do decolonial and feminist critics see as the limits of immanence (the problem of whose context, the marginalized or "constitutive outside")? Can immanent critique be provincialized or pluralized?
- How have other disciplines adapted or applied immanent critique (law, education, social science, IR), and do the adaptations clarify or distort its logic?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: PhilPapers (Critical Theory and Postcolonialism; Decolonial theory; Feminist Critical Theory; Critical Legal Studies), NDPR reviews of The End of Progress, Critique on the Couch, Allen and Mendieta (eds.), Celikates; Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and CORE for cross-disciplinary uses, with "--recent" for 2018-2026.
- Key terms: "Amy Allen End of Progress", "critical theory progress colonialism", "decolonizing critical theory", "Eurocentrism Frankfurt School", "Habermas Honneth postcolonial critique", "feminist immanent critique", "immanent critique law", "immanent critique education", "immanent critique sociology", "critical management studies immanent critique", "immanent critique international relations", "immanent critique artificial intelligence".
- Authors to anchor: Allen; Bhambra; Chakrabarty; Said; Mignolo; Dussel; Spivak; Mills; Mendieta; Fraser; Butler; Cooke; Boltanski; Bourdieu; Unger; Koskenniemi; Biesta; Freire; Linklater; Cox; Zurn; Stahl.
- Quality note: for application papers, prefer those that explicitly define and theorize "immanent critique" rather than using the phrase loosely; flag loose uses.
- Expected papers: 12-18.

**Relevance**: Covers the final two items in the user's list and tests whether the method's presuppositions (progress, a shared context) survive.

## Coverage Rationale

Domains 1-3 supply the chronological overview in historical order (Hegel/Marx; first-generation Critical Theory; Habermas/Honneth and associated authors). Domains 4-7 take up the debates in a logical sequence: taxonomy and method (4), justification (5), mechanics and ideology (6), and limits and extensions (7). Domain 5 is where critical and external views are concentrated, which keeps the review from being merely confirmatory. Domain 5 also includes the analytic internal-critique literature requested. Boundaries: Adorno's "wrong life" is introduced historically in Domain 2 and examined as a debate in Domain 6; Walzer appears in Domain 3 (as a parallel project) and recurs in Domain 5 (as an object of conservatism objections). Authors recurring across domains should be cited at the domain where their contribution is primary.

## Expected Gaps

- Few systematic treatments distinguish immanent from internal critique; the terminology is used inconsistently across German and Anglophone literatures (Stahl and Jaeggi use it differently from Walzer and the analytic literature).
- Limited sustained engagement between the Frankfurt literature and the analytic internal-reasons literature.
- Very thin engagement between immanent critique and decolonial/Indigenous thought beyond Allen; the question whether the "immanent" context may itself be colonial is underexplored.
- Applications in law, education and social science often use "immanent critique" loosely without engaging the philosophical debate.
- Language bias: key texts (Stahl, Jaeggi, Celikates, Adorno, Honneth) are in German; translations and English reception lag. Researchers should note German-language sources and available translations.
- Possible new work on AI/technology critique and on climate/ecological critique using immanent critique.
