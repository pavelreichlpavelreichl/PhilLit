## Introduction

**Purpose**: Frame Plekhanov as the first systematic philosophical expositor of Marxism and establish the review's focus and stakes.

**Content**:
- Plekhanov (1856–1918) as the "father of Russian Marxism": the figure who gave historical materialism its first systematic philosophical (not merely economic/political) form and who is credited with coining "dialectical materialism" and foregrounding Marx's Hegelian roots (white2018plekhanov).
- The central object: his philosophy of history — the "five-fold formula," the freedom/necessity dialectic, the theory of ideology and "social psychology," and the polemic against Narodnik subjectivism.
- Why it matters: this schema became, via Lenin, the template of Soviet "diamat," yet Plekhanov ended a Menshevik opponent of October — a paradox that structures both his reception and the scholarly debates.
- The absence of a dedicated SEP/IEP entry and the unevenness, and Cold War datedness, of Anglophone scholarship — motivating a synthesis that maps positions and tensions rather than settling them (jameswhite2023jacobin).
- Scope/structure preview: history → foundations → aesthetics → politics → influence/verdict.

**Key Papers**: white2018plekhanov, walicki1979history, baron1963plekhanov, jameswhite2023jacobin, plekhanov1940essays
**Word Target**: 500–600 words

## Section 1: The Philosophy of History (ANALYTICAL CORE)

**Section Purpose**: Reconstruct and critically assess Plekhanov's philosophy of history — the review's primary focus — and expose its internal tension between strong determinism and individual/geographic mediation.

**Main Claims**:
1. Plekhanov built a monist, multi-tiered causal schema meant to block both economic reductionism and subjectivist voluntarism.
2. His compatibilist solution to freedom/necessity is philosophically unstable at exactly the point where individuals matter — a tension pressed by both analytic (Shaw) and historical (Hook) critics.
3. His anti-Narodnik polemic partly caricatures its target and, in doing so, exposes the question-begging character of his own determinism.

**Subsection 1.1: The five-fold formula and the "social psychology" mediating level**
**Papers**: plekhanov1947monist, brazevich2021genesis, hu2026reconstruction, soboleva2021marxism
**Content**: Reconstruct the five-term chain (productive forces → economic relations → socio-political order → psychology of social man → ideologies); emphasize that each term sets limits/tendencies rather than dictating content, and that "social psychology" is an irreducible layer explaining why ideas cannot be read off economics. Note the scholarly gap: no rigorous dedicated study of the formula (distributed across Baron, Walicki, Brazevich).
**Connection**: Establishes the architecture that Sections 2–3 fill in (its ontology; its ideology level).

**Subsection 1.2: Freedom, necessity, and the role of the individual**
**Papers**: plekhanov1940essays, shaw1988plekhanov, hook2023heroinhistory, mayer1993dictatorship
**Content**: Plekhanov's Spinozist–Hegelian "freedom is the recognition of necessity"; individuals as accelerants/retardants of a fixed process. Shaw's critique: the contingencies Plekhanov invokes (e.g., absence of equally talented rivals) threaten to collapse the determinism or render the individual's role arbitrary. Hook's parallel historical test — October 1917 as contingent on Lenin — reaching similar skepticism by a different route.
**Connection**: The pivot of the whole review; recurs in Section 4 (political voluntarism) and Section 5 (the assessors' verdicts).

**Subsection 1.3: The critique of Narodnik subjectivist sociology**
**Papers**: teslya2021mikhailovsky, walicki1979history, plekhanov1947monist
**Content**: Plekhanov versus Mikhailovsky/Lavrov's "subjective method" and "critically thinking individuals." Teslya's reconstruction shows Mikhailovsky's non-teleological, non-determinist theory was more considered than the polemic allows; Walicki situates the Populism→Marxism transition as both continuity and rupture. Assess whether the critique is fair or question-begging given Plekhanov's own determinism.
**Connection**: Shows the polemical origin of the determinism and links to Section 4's revolutionary strategy.

**Subsection 1.4: How deterministic was it? Stagism, geography, and mediation**
**Papers**: vanree2013plekhanov, bassin1992geodeterminism, peet1993critique, hu2026reconstruction
**Content**: The determinism operationalized as Russian "stagism" (van Ree: a novel departure from Marx, not mere application). The Bassin–Peet exchange on geographical/environmental determinism (Ratzel) as a live, adjudicated dispute over whether Plekhanov's materialism is narrowly economic or a more mediated "social organism" (Hu) — complicating the "vulgar determinist" caricature.
**Connection**: Directly sets up the Section 5 historiographical debate on "vulgar materialism."

**Section Summary**: A coherent multi-level schema whose determinism is more qualified than caricatures allow, but whose treatment of individual agency remains unresolved — the fault line the rest of the review tracks.
**Word Target**: 1600–2000 words

## Section 2: Philosophical Foundations — Monism, Epistemology, and Fidelity

**Section Purpose**: Establish the ontological and epistemological substructure of the philosophy of history and the debates over its coherence and faithfulness to Marx.

**Subsection 2.1: Monism via Spinoza**
**Papers**: steila2021spinoza, steinberg2024russianmarxism, soboleva2021marxismspinozism
**Content**: Plekhanov's reading of Spinozist substance-monism ("Marxism is a variety of Spinozism") as the backbone of a non-reductive materialism; Steinberg's non-partisan verdict that Plekhanov's reading is the most defensible among Russian Marxists; Bogdanov's hermeneutic and systematic objections (Soboleva).
**Connection**: Supplies the "monist" content behind "monist view of history."

**Subsection 2.2: The hieroglyph theory of knowledge and its critics**
**Papers**: wagner1993genesisknowledge, rybas2023receptionskant, rumyantseva2025plekhanovkant, seriot2022hieroglyphics, pavlov2017caesar
**Content**: The Helmholtzian sign/"hieroglyph" theory of sensation; Engels's objection that it flirts with Kantian agnosticism; the empiriocritic (Bogdanov) and Leninist responses; the thing-in-itself and Plekhanov's contested "defence" of Kant; the doctrine's long Soviet afterlife.
**Connection**: The epistemological warrant for the "objectivity" claimed by the five-fold schema; anticipates Lenin's reflection theory in Section 5.

**Subsection 2.3: Fidelity to Marx, Engels, and Hegel**
**Papers**: white1996karlmarx, moore1971origindialectical, vanree2013communistmanifesto
**Content**: Was "dialectical materialism" Plekhanov's systematization or Marx's own view? White questions unbroken continuity; Moore locates an unresolved materialism/dialectic tension already in Marx; van Ree documents a substantive strategic departure. Plekhanov as systematizer/reviser, not neutral transmitter.
**Connection**: Frames Section 5's question of whether Plekhanov "originated" or "distorted" the orthodox canon.

**Section Summary**: The foundations are more sophisticated (Spinozist, anti-mechanist) than the "vulgar" label implies, but the hieroglyph epistemology and the fidelity question remain genuinely contested.
**Word Target**: 900–1100 words

## Section 3: Aesthetics and the Sociological Theory of Art

**Section Purpose**: Present Plekhanov's aesthetics as an instance of the ideology level, and stage the "vulgar Marxism" coherence debate in miniature.

**Subsection 3.1: The genetic theory and the critique of "art for art's sake"**
**Papers**: plekhanov1957unaddressed, toma2025politicization, nascimento2014plekhanov
**Content**: Labor-priority derivation of art (contra/via Bücher and Spencer); "art for art's sake" historicized as the ideology of an estranged intelligentsia; the Belinsky/Chernyshevsky utilitarian lineage.
**Subsection 3.2: The reductionism debate**
**Papers**: baxandall1967marxism, truitt1970reply, sockol2023verso, sockol2011bridge, dellavolpe1978critique, luyan2015lefebvre
**Content**: The Baxandall/Truitt exchange (sociological "translation" as reduction vs. historical nuance); Della Volpe's and Lefebvre's rival Marxist critiques; Sockol's recent defense reframing the aesthetics as anti-Kantian, activist polemic rather than naive reflectionism; the line to Socialist Realism.
**Connection**: A concrete testing-ground for the master-tension; links back to 1.1's ideology tier.
**Section Summary**: Coherence contested; recent revisionism (Sockol) parallels the broader rehabilitation motif.
**Word Target**: 700–850 words

## Section 4: Political Trajectory and the Determinism/Voluntarism Problem

**Section Purpose**: Narrate Emancipation of Labour → 1903 split → 1905 two-stage schema → 1914 defencism as the practical counterpart of the deterministic philosophy of history, foregrounding the unresolved determinism/voluntarism tension.

**Papers**: gaido2015zasulich, gaido2017iskragroup, elwood2011brussels, baron1958russiaimpact, avila2017kautsky, day2009permanentrevolution, thatcher2021momentoftruth, baron1981warrevolution, mayer1997plekhanov, zhuravleva2019theoretical
**Content**: The Emancipation of Labour group's anti-terrorist, pro-organization founding; Plekhanov on the Iskra board then siding with Martov in 1903; the two-stage theory grounded in his account of Russian development (Baron 1958); his 1906 questionnaire and Kautsky's dissent exposing stagism from within (Avila/Gaido); permanent revolution as rival schema (Day); WWI defencism (Baron 1981). Throughout: no single work resolves how his determinism coexists with repeated decisive improvisation — reconstruct this as the section's problem. Mayer on the Plekhanov–Lenin difference over worker consciousness.
**Connection**: Provides the empirical stakes for the Section 5 "tragedy" narratives.
**Word Target**: 800–1000 words

## Section 5: Influence, Reception, and the Historiographical Verdict

**Section Purpose**: Trace the transmission and mutation of Plekhanov's materialism (Lenin, diamat, Labriola, Western Marxism) and map the meta-level debates that judge him — integrating Domains 5 and 6.

**Subsection 5.1: Lenin, reflection theory, and Soviet diamat**
**Papers**: bonilla2020reflexion, oittinen2018dialectician, pateman2019dogmatism, antonov2021matter, mareeva2016lifshits
**Content**: Lenin's 1908 adoption of Plekhanov's reflection theory; the live dispute over a post-1914 Hegelian "rupture" (Bonilla) versus continuity and non-Plekhanovian sourcing (Oittinen, who credits Herzen); fallibilist defenses of the Lenin/Plekhanov epistemology (Pateman) against the dogmatism charge.
**Subsection 5.2: Lateral and Western Marxist reception**
**Papers**: savino2018italian, colletti1971marxism, savin2020origins, leblanc2013spider, szabadossautkin2021lukacs, lukacs1971lenin, kolakowski1978maincurrents, acton1955illusion
**Content**: Labriola's praxis-philosophy via Gramsci (Savino); Colletti defining "Second International Marxism" as Kautsky+Plekhanov; the council-communist/Frankfurt critique (Pannekoek→Marcuse) via Savin; Lukács's Hegelian-praxis alternative; the Marxological syntheses (Kolakowski, Acton) placing Plekhanov at the origin of the tradition later judged scientistic.
**Subsection 5.3: The historiographical verdicts — "vulgar materialist," "father of Bolshevism," "tragic Menshevik"**
**Papers**: baron1974resurrection, baron1974trotskyhistoriography, ulam2024historiography, deutscher1964review, cliff1957plekhanov, northvolkov2016wsws, carr2021fatherrussianmarxism, berlin1951carrreview, mcneal1964review, jameswhite2023jacobin
**Content**: These labels as contested, politically shaped verdicts, not neutral descriptions. Soviet demotion then selective "resurrection" (Baron 1974; Ulam's "divorced father at the wedding"); the divergent Marxist tragedy-narratives (Deutscher's Erasmus figure; Cliff's organizational isolation; North & Volkov's two-stage-theory logic); the reflexive layer (Berlin vs. Carr on whether harsh verdicts track scholarship or pro-Lenin sympathy); Cold War vs. post-Soviet reassessment (White).
**Connection**: Returns the master-tension to the surface and sets up the conclusion.
**Word Target**: 1200–1500 words

## Conclusion

**Purpose**: Synthesize the current state of the literature and position the neglected questions worth pursuing.

**Content**:
- The consolidating finding: across foundations, aesthetics, politics, and reception, the "vulgar/mechanistic materialist" caricature is increasingly contested (Bassin, Sockol, Steinberg, Pateman, Hu), yet the deeper unresolved problem is the determinism/individual-agency tension diagnosed independently by Shaw and Hook and enacted in Plekhanov's own political life.
- Unresolved questions/tensions: the rigor of the five-fold formula and the "social psychology" tier is under-analyzed; the coherence of his compatibilism remains open; the Lenin-rupture dispute (Bonilla vs. Oittinen) is unsettled; post-Soviet and Russian-language reassessments are underrepresented in English.
- How the debates connect: the reception verdicts are inseparable from the political trajectory, so evaluating Plekhanov's philosophy of history requires disentangling philosophical assessment from Cold War and factional-Marxist judgment.
- Expected contribution: a philosophically focused re-examination of the five-fold formula and the freedom/necessity synthesis with contemporary agency–structure tools, reading Plekhanov as a qualified mediation-theorist rather than a crude determinist.
**Word Target**: 450–550 words
