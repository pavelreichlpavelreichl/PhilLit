# Literature Review Plan: Dussel's Critique of Habermas

## Research Idea Summary

This review maps the secondary scholarship on Enrique Dussel's critique of Jürgen Habermas. It covers how scholars have discussed, analyzed, contextualized, and responded to Dussel's engagement with Habermasian discourse ethics, communicative action, universal pragmatics, and the theory of modernity — including the Dussel-Apel-Habermas dialogue and Dussel's transmodern/decolonial reframing of Habermas's project.

## Key Research Questions

1. How does the secondary literature characterize the substance and stakes of Dussel's critique of Habermas?
2. How do scholars frame the opposition between liberation ethics and discourse ethics, and is the critique read as a rejection, a supplement, or an immanent correction of Habermas?
3. How is Dussel's transmodernity/Eurocentrism critique positioned against Habermas's account of modernity?
4. What counter-arguments, limitations, or defenses of Habermas appear in the scholarship?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: The Dussel-Apel-Habermas Dialogue (Historical and Textual Context)

**Focus**: Secondary literature reconstructing the actual encounter and exchange between Dussel, Karl-Otto Apel, and Habermas — the Frankfurt/Mainz dialogues, the philosophy of liberation's reception in German discourse ethics, and the textual record of mutual engagement.

**Key Questions**:
- How do scholars reconstruct the chronology and substance of the Dussel-Apel-Habermas exchange?
- What did each interlocutor concede or refuse, according to the secondary literature?
- How central is Apel as the mediating figure between Dussel and Habermas?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP/IEP on discourse ethics and Latin American philosophy; PhilPapers categories on Dussel, Apel, philosophy of liberation
- Key terms: "Dussel Apel dialogue", "Dussel Habermas exchange", "philosophy of liberation discourse ethics", "Dussel Frankfurt School", "Apel Dussel debate"
- Expected papers: 8-12

**Relevance to Project**: Establishes the documented basis of the critique that all other domains analyze.

---

### Domain 2: Liberation Ethics versus Discourse Ethics

**Focus**: Scholarship comparing Dussel's ethics of liberation with Habermasian (and Apelian) discourse ethics — the status of the victim/excluded, the principle of material life, and the critique of the ideal speech situation as formal/proceduralist.

**Key Questions**:
- How do scholars present Dussel's "material" ethics against Habermas's "formal/procedural" ethics?
- How is the figure of the excluded/the victim used to critique the ideal speech situation and the symmetry of communicative participants?
- Do scholars read this as complementary or fundamentally incompatible with discourse ethics?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP "Discourse Ethics", PhilPapers "Ethics of Liberation"
- Key terms: "ethics of liberation discourse ethics", "Dussel material ethics", "ideal speech situation exclusion", "Dussel victim discourse ethics", "liberation ethics critique Habermas"
- Expected papers: 10-14

**Relevance to Project**: Core normative content of Dussel's critique of Habermas's practical philosophy.

---

### Domain 3: Transmodernity, Eurocentrism, and the Critique of Habermasian Modernity

**Focus**: Secondary literature on Dussel's decolonial critique of Habermas's "Philosophical Discourse of Modernity" — Eurocentrism, the world-systemic origins of modernity, the 1492 thesis, and transmodernity/interculturality as alternatives to Habermas's account of modernity as an unfinished project.

**Key Questions**:
- How do scholars present Dussel's challenge to Habermas's intra-European genealogy of modernity?
- How is "transmodernity" positioned against Habermas's "unfinished modernity"?
- How does the decolonial/world-systems framing reshape the critique beyond ethics into philosophy of history?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: PhilPapers/OpenAlex decolonial theory, modernity/coloniality
- Key terms: "Dussel transmodernity", "Dussel Eurocentrism modernity", "underside of modernity Habermas", "decolonial critique communicative rationality", "Dussel 1492 modernity"
- Expected papers: 10-14

**Relevance to Project**: Captures the macro-philosophical and decolonial dimension of the critique.

---

### Domain 4: Communicative Rationality, Universal Pragmatics, and Intercultural Dialogue

**Focus**: Scholarship analyzing Dussel's engagement with the formal-pragmatic core of Habermas — communicative rationality, universal pragmatics, validity claims, the lifeworld — and Dussel's proposal of intercultural/analectical dialogue as a corrective to a putatively monocultural pragmatics.

**Key Questions**:
- How do scholars assess Dussel's claim that communicative rationality presupposes a Eurocentric or already-included community of communication?
- How is "analectics"/interculturality offered as an alternative or supplement to universal pragmatics?
- What do scholars say about the asymmetry between interlocutors that Dussel insists Habermas elides?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP "Habermas", "Communicative Action"; PhilPapers analectic/interculturality
- Key terms: "communicative rationality Dussel", "universal pragmatics liberation", "analectics intercultural dialogue", "Dussel community of communication", "validity claims excluded other"
- Expected papers: 8-12

**Relevance to Project**: Targets the theoretical/epistemological core of Habermas's program that Dussel contests.

---

### Domain 5: Reception, Critical Responses, and Comparative Scholarship

**Focus**: Critical and comparative secondary literature — defenses of Habermas against Dussel, assessments of the cogency of Dussel's critique, comparisons with other critics (postcolonial, Frankfurt School, Latin American), and meta-level evaluations of whether the critique succeeds.

**Key Questions**:
- What objections, limitations, or rebuttals to Dussel's critique appear in the literature?
- Do scholars defend Habermas's universalism against the charge of Eurocentrism?
- How does Dussel's critique compare with other (postcolonial, communitarian, feminist) critiques of Habermas?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: NDPR reviews, PhilPapers, Semantic Scholar with --recent flag
- Key terms: "critique of Dussel Habermas", "Habermas universalism Eurocentrism defense", "Dussel Habermas reassessment", "liberation philosophy critical assessment", "postcolonial critique Habermas comparison"
- Expected papers: 8-12

**Relevance to Project**: Ensures balanced, critical coverage rather than a one-sided endorsement of Dussel.

---

## Coverage Rationale

The five domains move from the documented exchange (D1), through the two substantive axes of critique — practical/ethical (D2) and historical/decolonial (D3) — into the theoretical core of Habermas's program (D4), and close with critical/comparative reception (D5). Together they span the textual, normative, world-historical, epistemological, and meta-critical dimensions of Dussel's engagement with Habermas without significant overlap.

## Expected Gaps

- Sparse English-language secondary coverage of the most technical pragmatics disputes (much is in Spanish/German); researchers should search OpenAlex/PhilPapers in multiple languages.
- Possible thin coverage of Habermas's own (limited) direct responses to Dussel.
- Under-representation of recent (post-2015) decolonial re-readings — prioritize --recent searches.

## Estimated Scope

- **Total domains**: 5
- **Estimated papers**: 44-64 total
- **Key positions**: (1) Dussel's liberation/material ethics as corrective to discourse ethics; (2) Habermasian proceduralist universalism; (3) decolonial/transmodern reframing of modernity; (4) critical/comparative assessments defending or relativizing both.

## Search Priorities

1. Foundational works: Dussel's *The Underside of Modernity*, the Apel-Dussel dialogue volumes, key reconstructions of the exchange.
2. Recent developments: post-2015 decolonial and transmodernity scholarship (use --recent flag).
3. Critical responses: defenses of Habermas and meta-assessments of the critique's success.

## Notes for Researchers

- Use the `philosophy-research` skill scripts extensively; start with `search_sep.py` (Habermas, Discourse Ethics) and `search_philpapers.py` (Dussel, philosophy of liberation) for foundational context.
- Many sources are Spanish or German — use `search_openalex.py` and `s2_search.py` with multilingual terms; verify all references via CrossRef before inclusion.
- Apply the `--recent` flag for Domains 3 and 5 to capture current decolonial scholarship.
- Distinguish key papers (sustained analysis of the Dussel-Habermas relation) from peripheral ones (passing mention) in BibTeX annotations.
- Deliberately seek out critical/dissenting literature for Domain 5 to avoid a confirmatory bias.
