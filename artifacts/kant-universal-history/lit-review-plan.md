# Literature Review Plan: Kant's "Idea for a Universal History with a Cosmopolitan Purpose" (1784)

## Research Idea Summary

A two-part review of Kant's 1784 essay (Idee zu einer allgemeinen Geschichte in weltbürgerlicher Absicht): (1) the reception history from Herder to the present, and (2) the main debates in contemporary English- and German-language scholarship on the essay's status, teleology, unsocial sociability, the relation of history to morality, cosmopolitanism, and critiques of its progressivism (Eurocentrism, race, colonialism).

## Key Research Questions

1. How has the essay been received and reinterpreted (Herder, Hegel, neo-Kantians, Cassirer, Arendt, Yovel, Habermas, Lyotard/Foucault, Frankfurt School, Wood, Kleingeld, Ypi)?
2. What is the epistemic status of the "Idea" and the Leitfaden: regulative or constitutive; how does it relate to the Critique of the Power of Judgment and to natural teleology?
3. What is unsocial sociability, and does it yield a coherent moral-psychological and political account?
4. How do nature's plan/providence, moral progress, the highest good, and practical hope fit together? Is the history compatible with Kant's moral philosophy and with freedom?
5. What is the cosmopolitan content (republican constitution, federation of states, cosmopolitan right), and how does it relate to Perpetual Peace and the Doctrine of Right?
6. Do race theory, Eurocentrism and colonialism undermine the philosophy of history, and did Kant change his mind (Kleingeld, Bernasconi, Mills, Eze, Flikschuh/Ypi, Allais, Huseyinzadegan)?
7. How does the essay relate to Kant's other historical writings (Herder reviews, Conjectural Beginning, Contest of Faculties, Anthropology, Religion)?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: Reception History, 1784-1945

**Focus**: Early and 19th/early 20th-century reception: Kant-Herder dispute (Kant's reviews of Herder's Ideen, 1785; Herder's Metakritik and Briefe zur Beförderung der Humanität), Schiller, Fichte, Hegel's Lectures on the Philosophy of History and critique of the "ought", Marx, neo-Kantians (Cohen, Natorp, Windelband, Rickert, Vorländer, Marburg socialism), Cassirer (Kants Leben und Lehre; Philosophy of Enlightenment).

**Key Questions**:
- What did Herder and Hegel take Kant's project to be, and what is distinctive in their objections (historicism, pluralism, immanent teleology vs. regulative Idea)?
- How did neo-Kantian and Marburg readings tie the essay to ethical socialism and the League of Nations idea?
- How reliable is the standard narrative of a Kant-to-Hegel trajectory?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Kant's Social and Political Philosophy; Kant's Philosophy of History if available; Herder; Hegel's Political Philosophy; Neo-Kantianism); IEP (Herder; Kant); PhilPapers categories on Kant's philosophy of history and Herder.
- Key terms: "Kant Herder controversy", "Herder Ideen Kant Rezension", "Hegel Kant philosophy of history", "Kant Geschichtsphilosophie Rezeption", "Neukantianismus Geschichtsphilosophie", "Cassirer Kant Enlightenment history", "Schiller Kant universal history".
- Expected papers: 10-14 (Zammito, *Kant, Herder, and the Birth of Anthropology*; Beiser, *Enlightenment, Revolution and Romanticism* and *German Historicism*; Sikka, *Herder on Humanity and Cultural Difference*; Forster's Herder work; Menze; Lehmann; Hegel-Kant studies; Zöller; Kleingeld on Herder; Wood on Hegel/Kant).

**Relevance to Project**: Provides the historical half of the brief and the interpretive lineage that contemporary debates inherit or contest.

### Domain 2: Twentieth-Century and Post-War Reappropriations

**Focus**: Arendt (Lectures on Kant's Political Philosophy; spectator and judgment vs. history), Yovel (*Kant and the Philosophy of History*), Habermas (cosmopolitan constitutionalism, "Kant's Idea of Perpetual Peace, with the Benefit of Two Hundred Years' Hindsight"), Rawls (Law of Peoples, Lectures on History of Moral Philosophy), Lyotard (the "sign of history", *Le différend*), Foucault ("What is Enlightenment?", Gouvernement de soi), Adorno/Horkheimer and the Frankfurt School (Dialectic of Enlightenment; Adorno's "Progress"), Benjamin and Koselleck (Kritik und Krise), Löwith, Rorty, Kuehn-style intellectual history, Reinhart Koselleck on historical time, German studies (Riedel, Kaulbach, Höffe, Bartuschat, Brandt, Kersting, Geismann).

**Key Questions**:
- How do these thinkers read the progress thesis and the "spectator" enthusiasm (Contest of Faculties) and the link to the 1784 essay?
- Which readings are faithful, and which instrumentalise the essay?
- How did German-language scholarship (Brandt, Höffe, Kersting, Horn, Geismann) frame it?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: PhilPapers (Kant: philosophy of history; Arendt on Kant); SEP (Arendt; Habermas; Adorno; Foucault); search_philpapers.py and s2_search.py.
- Key terms: "Arendt Kant political philosophy judgment history", "Yovel Kant philosophy of history", "Habermas Kant cosmopolitan", "Lyotard Kant sign of history", "Foucault Kant Aufklärung Revolution", "Adorno Kant progress universal history", "Kant Geschichtsphilosophie Forschungsbericht", "Koselleck Kant".
- Expected papers: 10-14.

**Relevance to Project**: Completes the reception history and shows the critical-theory and postmodern inflections.

### Domain 3: Status of the Idea, Teleology and the Guiding Thread

**Focus**: Regulative vs. constitutive reading of the "Leitfaden"; relation to Critique of Judgment (§§ 63-68, 83-84), the Appendix to the Dialectic, the "Idea of a purposiveness of nature"; natural teleology and the species vs. individual (the Anlagen "fully developed in the species, not the individual"); a priori vs. empirical history; whether the essay's "as if" (gleichsam) is compatible with its strong claims; also the role of "Idee" and "Plan der Natur"; lay vs. professional history; the relation to the Herder reviews.

**Key Questions**:
- Is the Idea a regulative maxim for historians, a postulate, or a quasi-empirical prediction?
- How coherent is the teleology with the first and third Critiques?
- Does the essay licence a "prophetic" history (Contest of Faculties, Part 2)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Kant's Aesthetics and Teleology; Kant's Philosophy of Biology? if available; Teleological Notions in Kant); PhilPapers.
- Key terms: "Kant regulative idea history teleology", "Kant guiding thread universal history", "Leitfaden Kant Geschichte", "Kant natural teleology history Critique of Judgment", "Kant Naturabsicht Geschichte", "Kant teleology species human history", "Kant a priori history", "Kant as if teleology".
- Expected papers: 10-14 (Wood, *Kant's Ethical Thought* and "Kant's Philosophy of History"; Guyer on Kant's teleology; Ginsborg; McLaughlin; Lindstedt; Kleingeld, *Kant and Cosmopolitanism*; Louden; Allison, "Teleology and History in Kant"; Fenves; Düsing; Rauscher; Zammito; Lütterfelds; Wike; Schönecker).

**Relevance to Project**: Core interpretive question of the essay.

### Domain 4: Unsocial Sociability, Moral Psychology and the Problem of History and Morality

**Focus**: Ungesellige Geselligkeit and antagonism as the means of nature; "crooked timber"; the relation to Anthropology, Religion (radical evil, ethical commonwealth), Conjectural Beginning; the paradox that moral progress is produced by non-moral motives; whether history can be a vehicle of moral progress, the highest good, and practical hope; rational faith in progress; freedom vs. natural mechanism; the role of the "devil's problem" of a republic of devils; Rousseauian background; Hirschman-style and Smithian "invisible hand" comparisons.

**Key Questions**:
- Is unsocial sociability a natural (empirical) or moral-practical trait; how does it relate to radical evil?
- Can antagonism produce a legal-moral order without undermining moral autonomy (history as external legality vs. inner moral progress)?
- Does hope for progress function as a duty or as a postulate (Kleingeld, Wood, Kleingeld's "Progress and Providence", Anderson-Gold, Louden, Rukgaber, Pinzani, Cohen, Ypi's "Avant-garde" reading)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Kant's Moral Philosophy; Kant's Philosophy of Religion; Kant's Anthropology); PhilPapers.
- Key terms: "unsocial sociability Kant", "ungesellige Geselligkeit", "Kant antagonism history moral progress", "Kant radical evil unsocial sociability", "Kant hope progress highest good history", "Kant providence moral progress", "Kant crooked timber", "Kant moral progress human species", "Kant ethical community history".
- Expected papers: 10-14 (Wood, *Kant's Ethical Thought* and "Unsociable sociability"; Kleingeld, *Fortschritt und Vernunft*; Kleingeld "Kant's Theory of Peace"; Anderson-Gold, *Unnecessary Evil*; Louden, *Kant's Impure Ethics*; Cohen, *Kant and the Human Sciences*; Ypi, *Emancipation*/*Avant-garde*; Cohen "Kant on the Ethical Commonwealth"; Muthu; Pinzani; Honneth; Deligiorgi; Kain; Kuehn; Surprenant; Sedgwick).

**Relevance to Project**: Addresses the central tension of the essay: how a mechanism of nature relates to moral freedom.

### Domain 5: Political and Cosmopolitan Dimensions

**Focus**: Propositions 5-8: perfect civil constitution, external relations of states, the federation of nations (Völkerbund), cosmopolitan right; link to Perpetual Peace (1795), "Theory and Practice" (1793) and Doctrine of Right (1797); statism vs. world-state vs. federalism; global justice and international order (Rawls, Habermas, Pogge, Höffe, Kleingeld, Flikschuh, Ypi, Niesen, Brown, Cavallar, Hirsch, Waldron, Byrd and Hruschka, Tan, Bohman); role of republicanism and the status of the cosmopolitan "purpose"; the Idea of "weltbürgerlich" (Kleingeld on the term "Weltbürger" 1784-1800); the "Enlightenment" in Beantwortung der Frage (1784).

**Key Questions**:
- What does the 1784 essay commit Kant to regarding a world federation, and how do commentators reconcile it with the "negative surrogate" of Perpetual Peace?
- Is Kant's cosmopolitanism moral, legal, political, or all three; how does war function as a means in history?
- How do contemporary global justice debates use the essay?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP (Kant's Social and Political Philosophy; Cosmopolitanism; Peace); PhilPapers; search_openalex.py for IR/political theory.
- Key terms: "Kant cosmopolitan right federation of states", "Kant perpetual peace Idea universal history", "Kant weltbürgerlich", "Kant world state federation", "Kant Völkerbund Weltrepublik", "Kant war nature's plan", "Kant international relations republicanism", "Kant global justice".
- Expected papers: 10-14 (Kleingeld, "Six Varieties of Cosmopolitanism in Late Eighteenth-Century Germany", *Kant and Cosmopolitanism*; Cavallar, *Kant and the Theory and Practice of International Right*; Flikschuh; Ypi, *Statist Cosmopolitanism*; Habermas; Höffe, *Königliche Völker*; Kersting; Bohman and Lutz-Bachmann (eds.), *Perpetual Peace*; Pauline Kleingeld; Brown; Hunter; Byrd and Hruschka).

**Relevance to Project**: Sets out the political content of the title's "cosmopolitan purpose".

### Domain 6: Critical Perspectives: Progress, Eurocentrism, Race and Colonialism

**Focus**: Critics of Kant's progressivism and of its Eurocentric structure: Bernasconi, Eze, Mills (racial contract), Mignolo, Said-style postcolonial critiques, Chakrabarty, Mehta, Muthu (Enlightenment against Empire), Kleingeld ("Kant's Second Thoughts on Race"; "Kant's Changing Cosmopolitanism"), Kleingeld on Kant's change of mind, Allais ("Kant's Racism"), Huseyinzadegan (*Kant's Nonideal Theory of Politics*), Flikschuh and Ypi (eds., *Kant and Colonialism*), Ypi, Lu-Adler (*Kant, Race, and Racism*), Mikkelsen, Larrimore, Hill and Boxill, Sandford; also feminist and decolonial critiques; critiques of teleology and stadial history; Adorno and Foucault's genealogies of progress; plus empirical or conceptual objections to progress claims (Amy Allen, *The End of Progress*; Koselleck; Rorty). Include scholarly defences of the essay (Kleingeld, Ypi, Wood) and sceptics, to be balanced.

**Key Questions**:
- Do the essay's claims about "Europe" lawgiving to the rest of the world (Proposition 9 and the ending) and its stadial structure entail Eurocentrism or colonial apologetics?
- Did Kant substantially revise his views (Kleingeld) or not (Mills, Eze, Lu-Adler)?
- Can the cosmopolitan project be rescued (Ypi, Flikschuh, Allen, Huseyinzadegan)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: PhilPapers (Kant: race; Kant: colonialism); SEP (Race; Kant's Social and Political Philosophy); s2_search.py with --recent for 2018-2026 literature.
- Key terms: "Kant race philosophy of history", "Kant Eurocentrism progress", "Kant colonialism cosmopolitan right", "Kant Herder race debate", "Kleingeld Kant second thoughts race", "decolonizing Kant progress", "Allen end of progress Kant", "Kant racism Enlightenment", "Kant anthropology race history teleology".
- Expected papers: 10-14 (Bernasconi 2001, 2002; Eze 1997; Mills 2005, 2014; Kleingeld 2007, 2012; Muthu 2003; Flikschuh and Ypi 2014; Allais 2016; Lu-Adler 2023; Huseyinzadegan 2019; Allen 2016; Mehta; McCarthy, *Race, Empire, and the Idea of Human Development*; Valdez; Sandford; Larrimore).

**Relevance to Project**: Provides sustained critical pressure on the proposal's neutral-seeming "progress" framework; ensures balance.

## Coverage Rationale

Domains 1-2 deliver the reception history chronologically (to 1945, then post-war). Domains 3-5 deliver the three core contemporary debates (status/teleology, moral psychology and morality-history relation, politics/cosmopolitanism). Domain 6 supplies sustained critical challenge and the race/colonialism literature. Domain 7 covers intertextual and methodological issues and helps with reference works. Overlaps are managed by assigning thinkers by primary focus: reception figures to 1-2; their systematic arguments to 3-6.

## Expected Gaps

- Few works trace the reception of the 1784 essay specifically (rather than Kant's philosophy of history generally); likely a gap in systematic reception history, especially for the neo-Kantians and for 20th-century French reception.
- Debates between teleological-regulative readings (Domain 3) and moral-psychological readings (Domain 4) are rarely integrated.
- Few works connect the race/colonialism critique with the details of Propositions 1-9 (most address Anthropology, Physical Geography or Perpetual Peace).
- German- and English-language scholarship often proceed in parallel with limited cross-citation.

## Estimated Scope

- **Total domains**: 7
- **Estimated papers**: 70-95 (some overlap; target roughly 60-80 unique after deduplication)
- **Key positions**: regulative/heuristic vs. quasi-constitutive teleology; moral-political (Kleingeld, Wood, Ypi) vs. historicist/Hegelian vs. critical-theoretic readings; statist/federalist vs. world-state cosmopolitanism; defenders vs. critics of Kant's progressivism (Kleingeld/Ypi vs. Mills/Eze/Lu-Adler/Allen).

## Search Priorities

1. Foundational works: Kant's primary text and reviews of Herder; Herder, Hegel; Yovel, Wood, Kleingeld, Cavallar, Arendt, Habermas.
2. Recent developments (2015-2026): Kant and colonialism, Lu-Adler, Huseyinzadegan, Allen, Ypi, new Kleingeld work; Kantian Review and Kant-Studien contributions.
3. Critical responses: Bernasconi, Mills, Eze, Mignolo, Allen, Lyotard/Foucault, Adorno; sceptics of the regulative reading.
