## Introduction (~450 words)

**Bib files**: 7, 1, 3

**Content**:
- The essay is short and appeared in 1784, the same year as *What is Enlightenment?*. Its central device is an "Idea" or guiding thread (Leitfaden) for writing universal history according to a "plan of nature", with a cosmopolitan aim. Use Kant's text via wood2007idea. The translator's introduction there frames the Idea as a theoretical idea anticipating the teleology of the third Critique, and marks the essay as the first statement of the federation of states.
- State the two questions of the review: (1) how the essay has been read from Herder to the present; (2) which interpretive disputes structure current scholarship.
- Ameriks (ameriks2009purposive) asks whether the essay shows Kant's philosophy to be "fundamentally historical". Use this to frame the stakes.
- Rorty and Schmidt (rorty2009critical, rorty2009introduction) record that the Critical Guide arose from renewed interest in progress narratives, international justice and globalisation. This explains why the reception remains active.
- Preview the structure and state the main gap: few works study the reception of *this essay*, as opposed to Kant's philosophy of history in general.

**Key Papers**: wood2007idea, rorty2009critical, rorty2009introduction, ameriks2009purposive, allison2009teleology

# PART I: RECEPTION HISTORY (~2300 words)

## Section 1: Contemporaries: Herder and the first quarrel over universal history (~700 words)

**Bib files**: 1, 7, 3, 6

**Section Purpose**: Show that the earliest reception was a dispute over method, happiness and politics, not only an episode in the "historicism versus Enlightenment" story.

**1.1 The Herder dispute: three readings**
- **Papers**: wood2007herder (Kant's reviews), wood2007conjectural (Kant's 1786 reply), wood1999history, sikka2007herder, sikka2011herder, piirimae2023herder, mcnulty2022speculations, nisbet2021natural, beiser2011historicist. Pointers only: ameriks2012fateful, reichl2021herderreview, sakabe1989freedom, vanderzande2004zammito (as a pointer to Zammito's book).
- **Content**: Organise the literature by the three axes on which commentators place the quarrel:
  - (a) *Methodological*: Kant's charge that Herder lets analogy and imagination stand in for rigour (translator's introduction in wood2007herder). McNulty adds that Kant rejected Herder's speculative developmental histories while relying on teleological speculation himself in the race essays (mcnulty2022speculations).
  - (b) *Ethical*: Sikka presents Herder's defence of plural, climate- and custom-bound happiness as a rival to the subordination of individual happiness to the progress of the species (Propositions 2-3) (sikka2007herder, sikka2011herder).
  - (c) *Political*: Piirimäe reads Herder as a critic of empire who developed his own account of commerce, patriotism and peace (piirimae2023herder).
- Beiser's account of historicism (beiser2011historicist) supplies the standard frame. The revisionist works challenge it.
- **Connection**: The three axes return in Part II as the teleology debate (a), the history-versus-morality debate (b) and the Eurocentrism debate (c).

**1.2 Other contemporaries**
- **Papers**: kleingeld2011cosmopolitanism, kleingeld1999six, mensch2025forster, martinson1988schiller
- **Content**: Kleingeld places Kant among Forster, Cloots, Hegewisch and Novalis, and distinguishes six varieties of cosmopolitanism, so Herder was not the only early interlocutor. Mensch treats Forster's 1786 critique of Kant's race theory against the background of Kant's geography lectures. Schiller's 1789 Jena lecture on universal history is an early reworking of the same project (martinson1988schiller).

**Section Summary**: The first reception already contains the objections later raised by historicists, eudaimonists and anti-imperialists.

## Section 2: From Idealism to neo-Kantianism and Cassirer (~600 words)

**Bib files**: 1, 2, 3, 4

**2.1 Fichte, Hegel and the "Kant-to-Hegel" narrative**
- **Papers**: rauscher2002regulative, pinkard2009norms, kleingeld2001nature, requejo2007somos, ypi2014revolution. Pointers only: picardi2013guiding (Fichte), marmasse2017list, bruns2017selbst, tenne2017copernican, albayrak2017empty.
- **Content**: Set out the two positions.
  - *Continuity/supersession*: Rauscher argues that Kant's separation of empirical history from reason's regulative role is unstable, and that Hegel's theory is the more consistent idealist one. This partly explains the move from Kant to Hegel (rauscher2002regulative).
  - *Resistance*: Pinkard, following Kleingeld, rejects the picture of Kant as a "Hegelian avant la lettre". For Kant a free social order is a regulative ideal; for Hegel it is achievable now (pinkard2009norms). Kleingeld (kleingeld2001nature) reads Kant's shift from "nature" to "providence" as a turn to a moral approach to history.
  - Requejo and Valls trace unsocial sociability into Hegel (requejo2007somos). Ypi's Kant-Marx comparison concerns revolution, not Marx's reading of the 1784 essay (ypi2014revolution).
- **Gap to flag**: No study of Hegel's *Lectures* or Marx with an abstract is in the corpus. The Hegel-Jahrbuch items are titles only.

**2.2 Neo-Kantian ethical socialism and Cassirer**
- **Papers**: vorlaender1900sozialismus, widmer2023stammler, widmer2024cohen, sieg2006natorp, ferrari2023cassirerhegel, aramayo2019cassirer (Tier B). Pointers only: medicus1902geschichte, krijnen2013geschichtsphilosophie.
- **Content**:
  - Vorländer's 1900 programme to ground socialism in Kant is a primary source.
  - Widmer argues against treating "Marburg socialism" as a single school. She proposes "left-Kantianism" as an umbrella for divergent views of law and history (widmer2023stammler), and reads Cohen as offering an ethical alternative to historical materialism (widmer2024cohen).
  - Sieg discusses Natorp's late political idealism.
  - Ferrari reads Cassirer as keeping the process of spirit open against Hegelian closure. Aramayo parallels Cassirer's defence of Weimar republicanism with Kant's response to the French Revolution.
- **Gap to flag**: Windelband and Rickert; Cassirer's own texts; how directly the 1784 essay (rather than Kant's ethics) figured in these debates.

## Section 3: Twentieth-century reappropriations (~1000 words)

**Bib files**: 2, 4, 6, 7, 5

**3.1 Secularisation and conceptual history**
- **Papers**: chorell2021unsecularizing, palti2018koselleck, lloyd2009providence, wittwer1996eschatologie (Tier B). Pointers only: loewith1983weltgeschichte, koselleck1987critique.
- **Content**: Chorell reports Löwith's thesis that philosophy of history is secularised eschatology. Lloyd reads the Idea as the point where Providence is transformed into the secular idea of progress (lloyd2009providence). Palti places philosophies of history within Koselleck's *Sattelzeit* and Foucault's "Age of History", and notes that Koselleck cites Herder's *Metakritik* on plural times (palti2018koselleck). This links back to Section 1.

**3.2 Judgment, spectatorship and the "sign of history" (Arendt, Lyotard, Foucault)**
- **Papers**: beiner1997rereading, degryse2011sensus, blumenthalbarby2021judgment, calcagno1995interface, dentreves2000critique, clewis2009enthusiasm. Pointers only (as the texts under discussion): lyotard1986enthousiasme, foucault1984lumieres, scheerlinck2017enthusiasmus.
- **Content**:
  - These readings shift attention away from the 1784 Leitfaden and toward the third Critique and the 1798 enthusiasm passage.
  - Beiner argues that Arendt reads Kant one-sidedly through the spectator. Degryse defends a single Arendtian theory of judgment.
  - Calcagno contrasts Kant's enthusiasm, a sign of a moral tendency, with Lyotard's denial that such a sign has political relevance.
  - D'Entrèves describes Foucault's "qualified defence" of Enlightenment as an ethos of critique of the present, which leaves aside the progress thesis.
  - Clewis supplies the Kantian account of enthusiasm as sublime.
- **Connection**: All of these bear on the question the plan poses about which readings are faithful to Kant and which instrumentalise him. Make explicit that they largely bypass the 1784 essay.

**3.3 Critical theory: progress after catastrophe**
- **Papers**: honneth2007irreducibility, frelek2025hope, mccarthy2009race, allen2016endofprogress. Pointers only: pensky2010storms, langthaler2002benjamin.
- **Content**:
  - Honneth distinguishes "system-conforming" from "system-bursting" readings of progress and keeps the second as a conflict-ridden learning process.
  - Frelek rereads Adorno's "Progress" (and Benjamin) as negative hope.
  - McCarthy holds that the idea of development is both "dangerous and necessary" and has underwritten global white supremacy.
  - Allen argues that Habermas and Honneth rely on a backward-looking, Eurocentric idea of progress.
  - Introduce McCarthy and Allen briefly here; Section 7 develops them.

**3.4 Cosmopolitan constitutionalism, IR, and systematic/German readings**
- **Papers**: fine2003habermas (Tier B), hurrell1990kant, doyle2017kant, hoeffe2007kosmopolitismus (Tier B), brandt1996origines, muglioni1996principe, pollmann2011kummer (Tier B), hoeffe2011schriften (Tier C, name as the commentary volume). Pointers only: habermas1992perpetual, habermas2008constitutionalization, rawls2000lectures, galston1983yovel, williams1981yovel, kaulbach1965zusammenhang, schroeder2011freiheit, kersting2007wohlgeordnete.
- **Content**:
  - Fine and Smith describe Habermas's post-1989 attempt to "think with Kant against Kant".
  - Hurrell lays out statist versus cosmopolitan IR readings. Doyle represents the democratic-peace use of Kant.
  - German and French systematic readings: Brandt derives the philosophy of history from pragmatic anthropology; Muglioni reads it as moral teleology; Pollmann reads it as "therapeutic" consolation against melancholy.
  - Yovel's 1980 book is attested only by reviews. Mention it without characterising its argument.

**Part I Summary**: The reception moves in three directions. Some readers push away from the Leitfaden (toward judgment and spectatorship). Others hold that the Leitfaden is superseded (Hegel) or secularised theology (Löwith). Others rebuild it in legal-political form (Habermas, IR). A reception history focused on the essay itself remains to be written.

# PART II: CONTEMPORARY DEBATES (~3400 words)

## Section 4: What kind of "Idea"? Regulative, biological, or more (~750 words)

**Bib files**: 3, 7, 4

- **Papers**: allison2009teleology, tredanaro2024purposiveness, kuehn2009reason, cohen2008biological, ginsborg2014organisms, chen2023nonconstitutive, zammito2008naturalistic, rauscher2002regulative, sargentis2015crisis (Tier B), vuillerod2024naturaliste, forster2009hidden, bittner2009philosophy, filieri2025aim, ostaric2023unity, kleingeld2001nature. Pointers only: kleingeld2008historiography, guyer2014moral, quarfood2006twolevel.
- **Content**: Set out the positions.
  - (1) *Regulative-systematic*: the Idea is grounded in the Appendix to the Dialectic (allison2009teleology, tredanaro2024purposiveness). On this view the essay is about historiography, not historical laws (kuehn2009reason).
  - (2) *Biological model*: history is modelled on organism teleology, which leaves room for scientific history (cohen2008biological). This connects to the CPJ debate about why teleology is "merely regulative" (ginsborg2014organisms, chen2023nonconstitutive).
  - (3) *Naturalist model*: Kant draws the model from his 1750s natural history, not from biology (vuillerod2024naturaliste).
  - (4) *Sceptical*: Zammito raises reservations about the reach of the Anlagen and about how much work providence has to do. Rauscher argues the regulative/empirical split is unstable.
  - (5) *Practical-teleological*: Kleingeld's nature-to-providence shift (kleingeld2001nature); Sargentis's double perspective of reflecting judgment; Filieri's "aims of freedom".
- Reflexive claims: Förster on the Eighth Proposition's "hidden plan"; Bittner on the Ninth Proposition's claim that philosophical history "helps" history.
- **Objection to flag**: Marwah (Section 7) argues that the teleology is not heuristically innocent.

## Section 5: Unsocial sociability, legality and moral progress, and hope (~850 words)

**Bib files**: 4, 3, 2

**5.1 What is unsocial sociability?**
- **Papers**: schneewind2009good, darwall2014social, grenberg2009anatomy, klein2026concept, charpenel2019insociable, beller2026seeds, muthu2014productive. Pointers only: wood1991unsociable, wood2009fourth, kim2010radikales, lerussi2018unsociable, ferguson2015unsocial.
- **Content**:
  - Schneewind places it in the Montaigne-Mandeville context. Darwall separates third-personal amour propre from second-personal sociability.
  - The radical-evil question: Wood's identification of radical evil with unsocial sociability should be reported *via Grenberg's account of it*, since Wood's own entries are Tier C. Grenberg asks whether we would be evil without society. Klein argues the concept is morally neutral and distinct from evil and war. Charpenel proposes corrections to Wood's reading.
  - Muthu extends the concept to global domination and resistance.

**5.2 Legality without morality? The paradox of non-moral means**
- **Papers**: guyer2009crooked, herman2009habitat, james2019practical, heisenberg2018wende, honneth2007irreducibility, williams2024progress
- **Content**:
  - Guyer asks why "crooked timber" makes the Fifth Proposition's problem insoluble, given that coercive right exists to deal with crookedness. Herman points to the essay's "effacement of the individual moral agent".
  - James argues the essay identifies no mechanism for moral change once legal conditions are in place.
  - Heisenberg argues that between 1784 and 1790 Kant adds ethical cooperation as a second source of progress.
  - Williams argues that the *Renewed Attempt* concedes that rightful progress is the most we can be sure of.

**5.3 Hope, the highest good and practical faith**
- **Papers**: woo2023hope, cureton2018reasonable, ypi2010natura, ypi2024moral, lenczewska2022rationality, louden2000impure, sweet2013practical, pollmann2011kummer. Pointers only: yovel1972highest, lindstedt1999progress, andersongold2000unnecessary.
- **Content**:
  - Woo argues that the de-emphasis of the highest good explains Kant's historical turn. Cureton reads the antagonism arguments as describing what a virtuous person reasonably hopes for.
  - Ypi analyses the coordination and continuity problems behind the Guarantee (ypi2010natura), and argues that constructivism needs purposive history to avoid circularity (ypi2024moral).
  - Louden and Sweet treat history as part of the practical philosophy rather than an add-on.

## Section 6: The cosmopolitan aim: federation, right and international order (~600 words)

**Bib files**: 5, 2

- **Papers**: kleingeld2009changing, kleingeld2004approaching (title-level only), kleingeld2016moral, kleingeld1999six, flikschuh2000kant, ripstein2009force, byrd2010kant, hoesch2012voelkerbund, mertens1995war, caranti2006perpetual, brown2024sieyes, molloy2017kant, brown2014european, ypi2011global, klein2022permissive. Pointers only: hoeffe1995volkerbund, hirsch2018weltstaat, pogge1988kant, poletti2021kant.
- **Content**:
  - Kleingeld argues that the Seventh Proposition calls for a federation *with coercive powers*, and that later texts change this in ways the literature usually overlooks. Connect this to the Völkerbund/Weltrepublik debate (Höffe and Hirsch as pointers).
  - Juridical readings (Flikschuh, Ripstein, Byrd and Hruschka) ground the cosmopolitan condition in right, without the plan of nature. That is a methodological alternative to the 1784 route.
  - Mertens finds an ambiguity in Kant's international writings that underlies statist and cosmopolitan readings, and partly places Kant in the just-war tradition. Caranti discusses the realist critique of Kant's peace project.
  - Brown (2024) reads *Perpetual Peace* as a curtailment of earlier cosmopolitan idealism, in response to Sieyès-era politics. Molloy reads it political-theologically.
  - Contemporary uses: the EU (brown2014european); statist cosmopolitanism (ypi2011global).
- **Gap to flag**: Propositions 5-8 are rarely studied in their own right outside the Critical Guide.

## Section 7: Race, Eurocentrism and the critique of progress (~800 words)

**Bib files**: 6, 3, 4, 2, 7

**7.1 Change of mind versus continuity**
- **Papers**: kleingeld2007second, kleingeld2014colonialism, flikschuh2014colonialism, eberl2019barbarism, mballo2024cosmopolitanism, govedarica2024assessment, lu-adler2022lazy, lu-adler2023race, luadler2023place, mosayebi2025luadler, huseyinzadegan2025observations. Pointers only: bernasconi2007unfamiliar, mills2005untermenschen, allais2016racism, storey2015empire, hill2000kantrace.
- **Content**:
  - Anchor the debate in the text. The Flikschuh-Ypi volume abstract notes that the 1784 essay states "without any critical distancing" that "our" part of the world will probably give laws to the others.
  - *Repair*: Kleingeld argues that Kant endorsed racial hierarchy and colonialism until the mid-1790s and then gave up both. Eberl and Mballo hold partial-rescue positions. Govedarica and Smajević Roljić take a deflationary position.
  - *Continuity*: Lu-Adler argues that racism is integral to the system and to the progress programme ("lazy savagery"; world-citizen pedagogy). Mosayebi and Huseyinzadegan respond to her.

**7.2 Is the teleology itself racialised?**
- **Papers**: marwah2022white, mcnulty2025culture, basevich2020reckoning, larrimore2008antinomies (Tier B), papish2024race, ramsauer2023problem, hahmann2024universalism
- **Content**: Marwah and McNulty argue that the teleological natural history and the concept of culture limit non-whites' moral capacity. This bears directly on Section 4. Basevich describes a racist pragmatic anthropology serving nonideal justice. Papish reads radical evil through natural history. Ramsauer argues that Kant's egalitarianism is "thin". Hahmann argues that the universalist framework itself generates pejorative judgments.

**7.3 Reconstruction or abandonment of progress?**
- **Papers**: huseyinzadegan2019nonideal, huseyinzadegan2022mills, basevich2022antiracist, mccarthy2009race, allen2016endofprogress, valdez2019transnational, gani2017erasure, inglis2025time, pascoe2024juridical. Pointers only: chakrabarty2000provincializing, mignolo2011darker.
- **Content**: Lay out the options:
  - nonideal theory (Huseyinzadegan 2019);
  - Black Radical Kantianism (Huseyinzadegan 2022);
  - Du Boisian race-conscious history (basevich2022antiracist);
  - chastened development (McCarthy);
  - contextualist abandonment of backward-looking progress (Allen);
  - transnational cosmopolitanism (Valdez);
  - decolonial critique (Gani);
  - defence of the cosmopolitan project (Inglis);
  - gender and juridical racism (Pascoe).

## Conclusion (~450 words)

**Bib files**: 7, 3, 6, 1

**Content**:
- Summarise the convergences:
  - Herder's three axes (method, happiness, politics) recur in today's debates over teleology, morality and Eurocentrism.
  - The regulative reading is now dominant but contested, from the Hegelian side (Rauscher) and from the race-critical side (Marwah, McNulty).
- Unresolved tensions:
  - whether the essay's teleology can be separated from its racial natural history;
  - whether legal progress can carry moral progress (James, Guyer, Herman);
  - whether the 1784 federation is Kant's early view or his considered one (Kleingeld 2009).
- Restate the gaps: a reception history of the essay itself, especially the 19th century and French reception; Propositions 5-9; integration of German- and English-language scholarship.

**Key Papers**: rorty2009critical, allison2009teleology, rauscher2002regulative, kleingeld2009changing, lu-adler2023race, honneth2007irreducibility
