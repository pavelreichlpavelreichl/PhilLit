## Section 3: The 2010s (2010-2019)

### 3.1 2010-2012

Several entries in these years connect Marx's value theory, crisis theory and law of the falling profit rate to the 2007-8 crisis (Resnick and Wolff 2010; Tabb 2010; Carchedi 2011; Giacché 2011; Micocci 2011; Smith and Butovsky 2012), while others treat human nature, alienation and species-being (Musto 2010; Sayers 2011; Czank 2012; Tabak 2012; Weyher 2012).

#### 2010

- Amariglio (2010), *Subjectivity, Class, and Marx's "Forms of the Commune"*: reads the Grundrisse's "forms that precede capitalist production" to draw out the forms of subjectivity implied by Marxian class concepts.
- Anderson (2010), *Marx at the Margins: On Nationalism, Ethnicity, and Non-Western Societies*: draws on notebooks and New York Tribune journalism to argue that Marx was sensitive to nationalism, race, ethnicity and non-Western societies, including Poland, Ireland and the proslavery movement (title-based description) [unverified].
- Best (2010), *Marx and the Dynamic of the Capital Formation*: examines Marx's dialectical method in *Capital* through debates in cultural studies, political economy and critical sociology, proposing an "aesthetics of political economy."
- Carchedi (2010; key carchedi2011behind, CrossRef gives 2011), *Behind the Crisis*: argues against claims of Marx's irrelevance, inconsistency and obsolescence by grouping debates on Marx's theory of value, method and crisis that bear on the current crisis (year disputed between CrossRef and Semantic Scholar).
- Clark and Foster (2010), *The Dialectic of Social and Ecological Metabolism: Marx, Mészáros, and the Absolute Limits of Capital*: draws on Marx's account of social and ecological metabolism and Mészáros's absolute limits of capital (Marx is one of two subjects).
- Comninel (2010), *Emancipation in Marx's Early Work*: examines the idea of human emancipation in Marx's early writings.
- Harvey (2010), *A Companion to Marx's Capital*: a guide to Volume 1 of *Capital* based on the author's CUNY lectures, aimed at first-time readers (title-based description) [unverified].
- Heller (2010), *Marx, the French Revolution, and the Spectre of the Bourgeoisie*: defends the class character of the bourgeoisie in Marx's account of the French Revolution against revisionist historians who deny its bourgeois and capitalist nature.
- Musto (2010), *Revisiting Marx's Concept of Alienation*: traces the development of Marx's concept of alienation and how the publication of previously unknown texts reshaped its interpretation over the twentieth century.
- Musto (2010), *The Formation of Marx's Critique of Political Economy: From the Studies of 1843 to the Grundrisse*: traces how the critique of political economy formed, presenting it as a basis for Marx's renewed relevance after the 2008 crisis.
- Resnick and Wolff (2010), *The Economic Crisis: A Marxian Interpretation*: interprets the 2008 crisis through Marxian class analysis, arguing that neoclassical and Keynesian approaches oscillate between two forms of capitalism and neither prevents crises.
- Tabb (2010), *Marxism, Crisis Theory and the Crisis of the Early 21st Century*: the title and note indicate an assessment of Marxist crisis theory in relation to the early twenty-first-century crisis (title-based description; names Marxism rather than Marx).

#### 2011

- Boer (2011), *Opium, Idols and Revolution: Marx and Engels on Religion*: a critical survey and reappraisal of Marx and Engels's work on religion (Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Brown (2011), *Marx for Medievalists: Rethinking Feudalism and Historicism in Capital*: assesses the value of *Capital* for medieval historians and literary historicists and argues against an anachronistic notion of ownership in interpretations of feudal exploitation.
- Caraway (2011), *Audience Labor in the New Media Environment: A Marxian Revisiting of the Audience Commodity*: revisits Dallas Smythe's audience commodity theory through a two-sided Marxian class analysis of audience labour (Marx is a framework; the main topic is Smythe).
- Foster (2011), *The Ecology of Marxian Political Economy*: argues that the environmental emergency is rooted in capitalism and that Marxian political economy supplies an ecological analysis often missing from critiques.
- Giacché (2011), *Marx, the Falling Rate of Profit, Financialization, and the Current Crisis*: argues that Marx sees crises as arising from overproduction of capital and commodities, and links the falling rate of profit to financialisation and the current crisis.
- Grollios (2011), *Marx and Engels' Critique of Democracy: The Materialist Character of their Concept of Autonomy*: argues that Marx and Engels's critique of democracy rests on a materialist concept of autonomy (Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Mezzadra (2011), *The Topicality of Prehistory: A New Reading of Marx's Analysis of "So-called Primitive Accumulation"*: reads Marx on primitive accumulation in light of contemporary new enclosures, arguing for its topicality for global capitalism.
- Micocci (2011), *Marx and the Crisis*: argues that capitalism's dialectical functioning constitutes a metaphysics of monetary denomination, with value-price transformation as continuous inaccurate iteration and finance more rewarding than production.
- Niemi (2011), *Karl Marx's Sociological Theory of Democracy: Civil Society and Political Rights*: presents Marx's sociological theory of democracy, focusing on civil society and political rights.
- Sayers (2011), *Marx and Alienation: Essays on Hegelian Themes*: a book collecting essays on Marx's theory of alienation and its Hegelian roots.
- Walker (2011), *Primitive Accumulation and the Formation of Difference: On Marx and Schmitt*: reads Marx's primitive accumulation with Schmitt, arguing that it produces difference rather than merely destroying a holistic community (Marx is one of two subjects).
- Weeks (2011), *Un-/Re-Productive Maternal Labor: Marxist Feminism and Chapter Fifteen of Marx's Capital*: examines Marx's language on productive labour in *Capital* chapter fifteen against feminist uses of it to explain women's domestic labour.
- Wilde (2011), *Marx, Morality, and the Global Justice Debate*: relates Marx's views on morality to contemporary global justice debates.

#### 2012

- Balibar (2012), *From Philosophical Anthropology to Social Ontology and Back: What to Do with Marx's Sixth Thesis on Feuerbach?*: considers how to read the Sixth Thesis on Feuerbach, discussing the move between philosophical anthropology and social ontology.
- Brown (2012), *Patriarchy, Women's Oppression and Resistance: Comparing Marx and Engels on Gender and the Family in Precapitalist Societies* (book chapter): the title and note indicate a comparison of Marx and Engels on gender, family, patriarchy and women's oppression and resistance (title-based description; Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Czank (2012), *On the Origin of Species-Being: Marx Redefined*: discusses the origin of Marx's concept of species-being and proposes a redefinition.
- Harvey (2012), *History versus Theory: A Commentary on Marx's Method in Capital*: argues that a gap between Marx's theoretical political economy and his historical writings arises from limits Marx set in the Grundrisse, and discusses the method of *Capital* in that light.
- Heinrich (2012), *An Introduction to the Three Volumes of Karl Marx's Capital*: an introduction to all three volumes of *Capital*, translated by Alex Locascio (title-based description) [unverified].
- Magun (2012), *Karl Marx and Hannah Arendt on the Jewish Question: Political Theology as a Critique*: the title indicates a comparison of Marx and Arendt on the Jewish question, treating political theology as a critique (title-based description; Marx is one of two subjects).
- Maidansky (2012), *The Logic of Marx's Theory of History*: offers a logical reconstruction of Marx's theory of history from the concept of labour, presenting four socioeconomic formations, and challenges the Marxian project of eliminating division of labour and private property.
- Rojek (2012), *Is Marx Still Relevant to the Study of Leisure?*: tagged critical-skeptical; the note reports that it argues Marxism remains relevant to leisure studies despite its late, short-lived influence and its eclipse by neoliberal perspectives.
- Smith and Butovsky (2012), *Profitability and the Roots of the Global Crisis: Marx's "Law of the Tendency of the Rate of Profit to Fall" and the US Economy, 1950-2007*: affirms the relevance of Marx's value theory and profit-rate law to the 2007-8 crisis, stressing the growth of unproductive constant capital and labour.
- Tabak (2012), *Dialectics of Human Nature in Marx's Philosophy*: a monograph on Marx's conception of human nature, opposing the compartmentalization of Marx's thought into competing doctrines.
- Weyher (2012), *Re-Reading Sociology via the Emotions: Karl Marx's Theory of Human Nature and Estrangement*: rereads Marx's theory of human nature and estrangement as a resource for the sociology of emotions.
