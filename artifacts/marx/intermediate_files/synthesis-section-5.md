### 2.2 2005-2009

Several entries in these years discuss Marx's theory of money and value (Moseley 2005; Hein 2006; Kristjanson-Gural 2008; Vasudevan 2009), the TSSI replication debate (Kliman and Freeman 2006; Kliman 2007), and the 2008 crisis (Rasmus 2008), while others revisit alienation (Cowling 2006; Archibald 2009) and the 1844 and Grundrisse manuscripts (Musto 2008; Musto 2009).

#### 2005

- Burkett (2005), *Marx's Vision of Sustainable Human Development*: argues that Marx's vision of communism as sustainable human development, rather than as a calculation problem, addresses the needs of anti-capitalist movements.
- de Brunhoff (2005), *Marx's Contribution to the Search for a Theory of Money* (book chapter): revisits Marx's theory of money as part of his theory of capitalism, asking whether it needs updating and whether it helps analyse nineteenth-century capitalism.
- Germer (2005), *The Commodity Nature of Money in Marx's Theory* (book chapter): examines whether money must be a commodity in Marx's theory, disputing contemporary Marxist authors who read Marx as compatible with non-commodity money.
- Gimenez (2005), *Capitalism and the Oppression of Women: Marx Revisited*: argues that Marx's methodology identifies the capitalist structural conditions underlying gender inequality and the limits of legal and political reform.
- Jossa (2005), *Marx, Marxism and the Cooperative Movement*: draws attention to passages where Marx praised cooperatives, to refute the view that he was hostile to the market or to cooperation as a mode of production in transition.
- Kennedy (2005), *G.A. Cohen's Karl Marx's Theory of History: A Defence*: discusses Cohen's book; the note gives only this topic (title-based description; concerns Cohen's defence of Marx).
- Kouvelakis (2005), *The Marxian Critique of Citizenship: For a Rereading of On the Jewish Question*: proposes a rereading of Marx's *On the Jewish Question* as a critique of citizenship and political emancipation.
- Moseley (2005), *Marx's Theory of Money* (edited volume): appraises Marx's theory of money, including its commodity-money basis, its critique of quantity and credit theories, and its relation to the transformation problem.
- Roberts (2005), *Marx in Hell: The Critique of Political Economy as Katabasis*: argues that Marx borrows features of Dante's *Inferno* in the 1859 Preface and the first-edition preface of *Capital*, presenting his critique as a descent.
- Tsoulfidis and Tsaliki (2005), *Marxian Theory of Competition and the Concept of Regulating Capital: Evidence from Greek Manufacturing*: discusses the classical-Marxian notion of competition as rivalry between firms and regulating capital, with empirical evidence (Marx's theory serves as a framework).
- Yuill (2005), *Marx: Capitalism, Alienation and Health*: the title indicates an application of Marx's theory of capitalism and alienation to health (title-based description).

#### 2006

- Burkett and Foster (2006), *Metabolism, Energy, and Entropy in Marx's Critique of Political Economy: Beyond the Podolinsky Myth*: the title indicates a treatment of Marx's critique of political economy in relation to metabolism, energy and entropy (title-based description).
- Carling (2006), *Karl Marx's Theory of History and the Recovery of the Marxian Tradition*: assesses Cohen's *Karl Marx's Theory of History* and its contribution to recovering the Marxian tradition.
- Chattopadhyay (2006), *Passage to Socialism: The Dialectic of Progress in Marx*: the note reports only that it discusses the dialectic of progress in Marx in the passage to socialism (title-based description).
- Cowling (2006), *Alienation in the Older Marx*: argues that alienation persists as a concept in the mature Marx, contesting the view that it is confined to the early writings (title-based description).
- Hein (2006), *Money, Interest and Capital Accumulation in Karl Marx's Economics*: shows that Marx's value theory, rejection of Say's Law, reproduction schemes and credit theory fall on the side of Schumpeter's "monetary analysis", and draws parallels to post-Keynesian approaches.
- Higonnet (2006), *Terror, Trauma and the 'Young Marx' Explanation of Jacobin Politics*: a critical examination of the "young Marx" explanation of Jacobin politics and the Terror.
- Kliman and Freeman (2006), *Replicating Marx: A Reply to Mohun*: replies to Mohun's critique of the TSSI, arguing that simultaneist interpretations contradict Marx's exploitation theory of profit while the TSSI conforms to it.
- Kliman (2007), *Reclaiming Marx's "Capital"*: book-length defence arguing that alleged inconsistencies in *Capital* (transformation problem, falling rate of profit) result from misinterpretation and vanish under the TSSI (year disputed between CrossRef, 2007, and Semantic Scholar, 2006).
- Klotz (2006), *Alienation, Labor, and Sexuality in Marx's 1844 Manuscripts*: discusses alienation, labour and sexuality in the 1844 Manuscripts.
- Palermo (2006), *The Ontology of Economic Power in Capitalism: Mainstream Economics and Marx*: argues, following Marx, that capitalism constantly reproduces asymmetrical constraints on classes independent of market form, contrasting this with mainstream conceptions of power (Marx serves as foil).
- Wetherly (2006), *The Theory of the State in Cohen's Functional Interpretation of Marx's Theory of History*: examines what Cohen's functional interpretation of Marx's theory of history implies for the theory of the state.

#### 2007

- Brophy (2007), *Recent Publications of the Marx-Engels Gesamtausgabe (MEGA)* (review essay): surveys recently published MEGA volumes (title-based description; concerns the edition of Marx and Engels).
- Clark, Foster and York (2007), *The Critique of Intelligent Design: Epicurus, Marx, Darwin, and Freud and the Materialist Defense of Science*: traces the materialist critique of intelligent design through four figures (title-based description; Marx is one of four subjects).
- Eidlin (2007), *Call to Destiny or Call to Action: Marx, Popper, and History* (book chapter): discusses Popper's criticism of Marx, which is skeptical of Marx's historical prophecy, and argues that Marx's writings do not on balance put forward a mechanical view of history.
- Hill (2007), *Adam Smith, Adam Ferguson and Karl Marx on the Division of Labour*: compares Smith, Ferguson and Marx on the effects of the division of labour and specialisation (Marx is one of three subjects).
- Jones (2007), *Marx's Critique of Political Economy: A Theory of History or a Theory of Communism?* (book chapter): examines the aim of Marx's theory, including Engels's graveside claim that Marx discovered "laws" of history, and whether Marx established what he set out to prove.
- Leopold (2007), *The Young Karl Marx*: a study of Marx's early writings that reads them through German philosophy, modern politics and a conception of human flourishing.
- Reichelt (2007), *Marx's Critique of Economic Categories: Reflections on the Problem of Validity in the Dialectical Method of Presentation in Capital*: argues that Marx's concept of validity, in which abstract labour is socially valid only within commodity exchange, exposes a tension in the concept of labour and underpins the value-form reading.
- Sayers (2007), *The Concept of Labor: Marx and His Critics*: defends Marx's conception of labour as form-giving activity against critics (Habermas, Benton, Arendt) who charge it with a productivist model.
- Screpanti (2007), *Libertarian Communism*: presents Marx and Engels as libertarian communists whose political economy is oriented to freedom and the abolition of alienation (Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Shuster (2007), *The Nature and Consequence of Karl Marx's Skin Disease*: establishes from Marx's correspondence that his skin disease was hidradenitis suppurativa, not boils, and discusses its psychological effect on him and his work (biographical rather than theoretical).
- Vercellone (2007), *From Formal Subsumption to General Intellect: Elements for a Marxist Reading of the Thesis of Cognitive Capitalism*: reads Marx's formal and real subsumption and general intellect to build a Marxist account of cognitive capitalism.

#### 2008

- Bellamy Foster and Burkett (2008), *Classical Marxism and the Second Law of Thermodynamics*: shows through textual analysis that Marx and Engels did not reject the second law of thermodynamics, correcting a claim that hindered understanding of their contribution to ecological economics (Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Buchwalter (2008), *Hegel, Marx, and the Concept of Immanent Critique*: examines immanent critique as a method shared by Hegel and Marx (BibTeX year 2008; the note and DOI give 1991; Hegel is a co-subject).
- Fracchia (2008), *The Capitalist Labour-Process and the Body in Pain: The Corporeal Depths of Marx's Concept of Immiseration*: answers the critique that Marx mistook birth pangs for death throes, reading the labour process and the body in pain as the corporeal basis of his concept of immiseration.
- Henry (2008), *The Theory of the State: The Position of Marx and Engels*: reviews the position of Marx and Engels on the theory of the state (Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Kristjanson-Gural (2008), *Money Is Time: The Monetary Expression of Value in Marx's Theory of Value*: derives the monetary expression of value for commodity money at the more developed stage of Marx's analysis, translating labour-time values into money.
- McIvor (2008), *The Young Marx and German Idealism: Revisiting the Doctoral Dissertation*: argues that recent work on German Idealism illuminates Marx's 1841 dissertation on Democritean and Epicurean atomism, which examines "reflective" thought and its link to individualism and alienation.
- Musto (2008), *Karl Marx's Grundrisse* (edited volume): marks the 150th anniversary of the *Grundrisse* (1857-58) with essays on the manuscripts, including on alienation and on Marx's life at the time of writing.
- Rasmus (2008), *The Deepening Global Financial Crisis: From Minsky to Marx and Beyond*: analyses the US subprime crisis, moving from Minsky's financial-instability theory to Marx's approach (Marx is one of two theoretical frames).
- Sardoni (2008), *The Marxian Schemes of Reproduction and the Theory of Effective Demand*: critically discusses Trigg's Keynesian development of Marx's reproduction schemes and argues that abandoning Marx's free-competition assumption is needed for a Marxian account of underemployment equilibria.
- Slater and McDonough (2008), *Marx on Nineteenth-Century Colonial Ireland: Analysing Colonialism as a Dynamic Social Process*: analyses Marx's writings on colonial Ireland as a dynamic social process, contra dependency-theory readings of colonialism.
- Starosta (2008), *The Commodity-Form and the Dialectical Method: On the Structure of Marx's Exposition in Chapter 1 of Capital*: argues that recent work on Marx's dialectical method under-thematises the analytical phase, and reads the structure of chapter 1 of *Capital* accordingly.

#### 2009

- Archibald (2009), *Marx, Globalization and Alienation: Received and Underappreciated Wisdoms*: argues that Marx's theory of alienation still applies to a global context in which factory work is said to have been humanised or exported.
- Bellofiore and Fineschi (2009), *Re-reading Marx* (edited volume): the note (abstract unavailable) indicates new perspectives on *Capital* after the critical (MEGA) edition (title-based description).
- Daremas (2009), *Marx's Theory of Democracy in His Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of the State* (book chapter): examines Marx's theory of democracy in his early critique of Hegel's philosophy of the state.
- Musto (2009), *Marx in Paris: Manuscripts and Notebooks of 1844*: takes up the philological problems of the 1844 Economic and Philosophical Manuscripts and the Paris notebooks, arguing they have been neglected in interpretation.
- Nimtz (2009), *A Return to Lenin, But Without Marx and Engels?*: argues against divorcing Lenin's project from that of Marx and Engels, responding to sympathetic re-readings of Lenin such as Lars Lih's (Marx and Engels are joint subjects alongside Lenin).
- Patterson (2009), *Karl Marx, Anthropologist*: a book-length study of Marx as an anthropologist; the table of contents shows it tracing Enlightenment and German anthropological antecedents before Marx.
- Ramirez (2009), *Marx's Theory of Ground Rent: A Critical Assessment*: reconstructs Marx's neglected theory of absolute ground rent from *Capital* III and *Theories of Surplus Value* and reviews the secondary literature on it.
- Vasudevan (2009), *From the Gold Standard to the Floating Dollar Standard: An Appraisal in the Light of Marx's Theory of Money*: uses Marx's analysis of "world money" to interpret the gold standard and the floating dollar standard, arguing that today's imperial hegemon is a net capital importer.
- Wendling (2009), *Karl Marx on Technology and Alienation*: examines how Marx's theory of alienation and his views on technology connect, drawing on archival materials including his notebooks on women, patriarchy and technology.
