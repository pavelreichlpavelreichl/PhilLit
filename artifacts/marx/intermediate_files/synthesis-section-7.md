### 3.2 2013-2014

Several entries in these years apply Marx's theories of the profit rate, ground rent and finance to the post-2007 crisis (Carchedi and Roberts 2013; Heinrich 2013; Corell and Herzog 2014; Smith 2014), while others treat Marx and gender or Marx and ecology (Wendling 2013; Brown 2014; Foster 2013; Foster and Holleman 2014).

#### 2013

- Bellofiore, Starosta and Thomas (2013), *In Marx's Laboratory*: an edited volume on the Grundrisse as Marx's laboratory for *Capital*, with chapters on the Grundrisse's method and levels of abstraction and its relation to the later *Capital* (edited volume).
- Blackledge (2013), *Marx, Engels and historical materialism*: outlines Marx and Engels's theory of history and its relation to their revolutionary practice, following Draper against Cohen and Rigby (book chapter; Marx and Engels are joint subjects).
- Carchedi and Roberts (2013), *The Long Roots of the Present Crisis: Keynesians, Austerians, and Marx's Law*: argues that falling profitability is the ultimate cause of capitalist crises and that Keynesian stimulus and austerity both fail to address it.
- Carver (2013), *Marx's Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte: Democracy, Dictatorship, and the Politics of Class Struggle*: reads the *Eighteenth Brumaire* as an account of democracy, dictatorship and Bonapartism (book chapter; year disputed between CrossRef and Semantic Scholar).
- Chitty (2013), *Recognition and Property in Hegel and the Early Marx*: argues that Hegel's account of property as expressing mutual recognition of free persons is carried into the 1844 Marx's exposition of communist society.
- Foster (2013), *Marx and the Rift in the Universal Metabolism of Nature*: presents Marx's theory of the rift in the universal metabolism of nature as a critique of the relation between nature and capitalist society and a basis for a unified ecological worldview.
- Heinrich (2013), *Crisis Theory, the Law of the Tendency of the Profit Rate to Fall, and Marx's Studies in the 1870s*: argues that Marx presented no final crisis theory and that his 1870s studies cast doubt on the falling profit rate as an explanatory law.
- Heinrich (2013), *The "Fragment on Machines": A Marxian Misconception in the Grundrisse and its Overcoming in Capital*: argues that the "Fragment on Machines" contains a misconception, a breakdown theory of capitalism, which *Capital* overcomes (book chapter).
- Musto (2013), *Marx for Today*: an edited volume on Marx's contemporary relevance; the retrieved text introduces Marx's worldwide reception and influence on philosophy, history and economics (edited volume; year disputed between CrossRef and Semantic Scholar).
- Pradella (2013), *Imperialism and Capitalist Development in Marx's Capital*: reads *Capital* together with Marx's writings on colonialism to argue that Marx analyses a world-polarising capitalist system rather than a national economy.
- Lacroix, Pranchère and Raillard (2013), *Was Karl Marx truly against human rights?*: responds to Leopold's revisionist claim that Marx is not opposed to human rights, arguing this does not invalidate Marx's early critiques of rights (English version of a French text; year disputed between CrossRef and Semantic Scholar).
- Sing and Younes (2013), *The Specters of Marx in Edward Said's Orientalism*: examines Said's charge in *Orientalism* that Marx was an Orientalist, and the book's ambivalent relation to Marxism.
- Smith (2013), *The "General Intellect" in the Grundrisse and Beyond*: examines Marx's "general intellect" in the Grundrisse against its use by Virno and Vercellone to describe post-Fordist capitalism.
- Sperber (2013), *Karl Marx the German*: places Marx in the intellectual and political world of nineteenth-century Germany, arguing that his theory paralleled other Young Hegelians but that his endorsement of English political economy set him apart (abstract-based).
- Sperber (2013), *Karl Marx: A Nineteenth-Century Life*: a biography placing Marx in the context of his own century and drawing on *MEGA* materials (title-based description) [unverified].
- Wendling (2013), *Second Nature: Gender in Marx's Grundrisse*: explores what the Grundrisse offers on gender, women's history and the family, and relates it to Marxist-feminist debates (book chapter).

#### 2014

- Bowring (2014), *Arendt after Marx: Rethinking the Dualism of Nature and World*: challenges the view that Arendt is unsympathetic to ecology, showing that she and Marx share more on nature and world than she presumed (Marx is one of two subjects).
- Brown (2014), *Marx on Gender and the Family: A Summary*: summarises Marx's writings on gender and the family, arguing they were once widely debated and were viewed as gender-blind by earlier feminist scholars.
- Chae (2014), *Marx on the Family and Class Consciousness*: examines Marx's account of the family in relation to class consciousness.
- Corell and Herzog (2014), *Subprime Crisis and Marx's Theory on Ground Rent*: applies Marx's theory of ground rent to the 2008-10 subprime crisis, treating mortgage-financed land and housing as a rent-bearing source of crisis.
- Crook and Short (2014), *Marx, Lemkin and the genocide-ecocide nexus*: links ecocide and genocide in extractive-industry contexts, taking Marx and Lemkin as theoretical starting points (Marx is one of two subjects; summary from title and opening of abstract).
- Foster and Holleman (2014), *The theory of unequal ecological exchange: a Marx-Odum dialectic*: reviews theories of unequal exchange from Ricardo and Marx onward and offers a Marx-Odum approach to unequal ecological exchange between centre and periphery (Marx is one of two foundations).
- Fuchs (2014), *Digital Labour and Karl Marx*: a book developing a critical theory of digital labour in the ICT industry using Marx's categories (year disputed between CrossRef and other indexes, which list 2013).
- Henning and Henninger (2014), *Philosophy after Marx*: gives a concise history of twentieth-century misreadings of Marx in German philosophy from Heidegger to Habermas, including the influence of Rawls and neopragmatism.
- Lange (2014), *Failed Abstraction: The Problem of Uno Kozo's Reading of Marx's Theory of the Value Form*: criticises Uno's rewriting of *Capital*, in particular his rejection of the early introduction of the value form in Volume I, and defends Marx's presentation.
- Moseley and Smith (2014), *Marx's Capital and Hegel's Logic*: an edited volume on the relation between Hegel's *Logic* and the method of *Capital* (edited volume).
- Pradella (2014), *Globalization and the Critique of Political Economy*: reconstructs Marx's critique of political economy within the history of political economy and reads it as a basis for analysing globalisation and the current crisis.
- Smith (2014), *Theories of Finance in Marx's Systematic Theory*: reconstructs Marx's theory of finance within his systematic dialectical theory of capitalism and compares it with Toporowski's typology of financial theories.
