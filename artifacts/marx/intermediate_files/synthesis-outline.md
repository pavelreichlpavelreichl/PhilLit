# Literature Review Outline (Chronological Catalogue)

**Research Project**: Marx scholarship since 1990: a chronological overview (by decade, then by year) of articles and books with Marx in the title and/or Marx's work as main topic
**Date**: 2026-09-30
**Total Literature Base**: 446 BibTeX entries (after removing one cross-file duplicate) across 6 files (literature-domain-1.bib to literature-domain-6.bib)
**Structure override**: at the user's explicit request this outline is chronological and catalogue-style, not thematic or debate-driven. The usual 800-1500 word outline and 3000-4000 word review targets do not apply. Every entry gets a one- to two-sentence overview.
**Full key lists**: `reviews/marx-since-1990/section-keys.md` (companion file; each writer works from the list for their section, sorted by year then author key).

---

## Introduction (one writer, ~400-600 words)

**Purpose**: state scope, inclusion criteria, method and limitations, and tell the reader how to use the catalogue.

**Content**:
- Scope: English-language articles, monographs and edited volumes published 1990-2026 in which Marx (his texts, concepts, or legacy) is in the title or is the primary subject (see lit-review-plan.md, inclusion and exclusion criteria). The catalogue is ordered by decade, then year, with alphabetical author order inside a year.
- Method: six search partitions (not thematic sections of the review): (1) philosophy, alienation, ethics; (2) Capital, value theory, political economy; (3) historical materialism, politics, class, state; (4) ecology, gender, colonialism, technology; (5) biography, reception, philosophical relations; (6) title-based decade sweep to close gaps. Sources: SEP/IEP for orientation, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, CORE, PhilPapers, CrossRef verification.
- Limitations to state plainly:
  - English only; non-English Marx scholarship (German, French, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish and others) is under-represented.
  - Coverage gaps in OpenAlex, CORE and PhilPapers; the list is a sample, not a complete census of 446 works, and per-year counts (10-17 entries) reflect search results, not publication volume.
  - Entries tagged `unverified-crossref` are DOI-less books; bibliographic details could not be confirmed by CrossRef.
  - Entries tagged `INCOMPLETE` have title-based notes only (no abstract); describe them cautiously (say what the title indicates) and never invent a thesis.
  - `YEAR NOTE` in an entry's note means CrossRef and Semantic Scholar disagreed; the BibTeX `year` field is authoritative and is the year used for ordering.
  - Keys whose embedded year differs from the BibTeX year are marked `(year!)` in section-keys.md (for example buchwalter1991hegel is placed in 2008; leopold2017greatness in 2019).
  - Note: derrida1994specters (the book) and derrida1994spectres (the 1994 New Left Review essay) are distinct works; describe each separately. Deduplication by DOI and normalized title across files was machine-verified by the orchestrator (one duplicate, Wendling 2009, already removed); writers should still report any further duplicates they notice and report them.
  - Some items are book reviews, review essays or encyclopedia-style entries; some treat Marx as one of several subjects.
- Preview: four decade sections, then a brief conclusion.

**Key Papers**: none needed; no theoretical argument is built. Optionally cite 2-3 landmark items in passing only if they fall in the introduction's framing (e.g., derrida1994spectres, postone1993criticaltheory, stedmanjones2016karl), described neutrally.

## Item-line conventions (apply in every section)

Each item gets one bullet or short paragraph line, in the order given in section-keys.md:

`Author (Year), *Title*: 1-2 sentences on topic and thesis, taken from the entry's note (CORE ARGUMENT) and abstract.`

Rules for writers:
1. Use the BibTeX entry's own `note` and `abstract`; do not add claims from memory.
2. Describe what the author argues; no evaluative superlatives ("seminal", "landmark", "most important") unless the note itself states a consensus or evidence.
3. Keywords contain `critical-skeptical`: identify the item as critical or skeptical of Marx or Marxism in its overview line (e.g., "a critical assessment of...").
4. Keywords contain `INCOMPLETE`: use hedged wording tied to the title ("the title indicates a treatment of..."); mark with "(title-based description)".
5. Keywords contain `unverified-crossref`: it is a DOI-less book; add a bracketed flag "[unverified]" after the item line.
6. Note mentions Marx as only one of several subjects, or the item is a book review or review essay: flag in the line, e.g., "(book review)" or "(Marx is one of several subjects)".
7. Note contains `YEAR NOTE`: keep the item in the BibTeX-year slot; optionally add "(year disputed between CrossRef and Semantic Scholar)".
8. If two keys are the same work, merge into one line with both keys.
9. Cite with keys in Chicago author-date form consistent with `../docs/conventions.md`; each year gets a short year heading (e.g., "1994"), and if a year has 10-17 items, keep item lines to about 30-45 words.
10. Give the year heading no interpretive text beyond, at most, one connecting sentence per decade chunk when the entries make a pattern evident (for example several items reacting to 1989).

**Word budget per writer**: about 45 words per item plus year headings, so roughly 2,300-2,800 words for a 52-57 item chunk; 1,300-1,500 for 27-32 item chunks.

## Conclusion (one writer, ~300-450 words, brief and descriptive)

**Purpose**: state observable trends across decades, using only patterns visible in the catalogue and counts from section-keys.md; no evaluation.

**Content**:
- Distribution: 104 entries in the 1990s, 111 in the 2000s, 134 in the 2010s, 98 in 2020-2026 (partial decade), noting these reflect the search sample.
- 1990s: reassessment after 1989 (justice and exploitation, value-theory debates, Derrida's *Specters of Marx* and responses).
- 2000s to 2010s: growth of ecological readings (burkett, foster, saito), postcolonial and Eurocentrism questions, gender and social reproduction, technology; the 2008 crisis and a visible rise in crisis, money and fictitious-capital titles; the 2013 peak and the 2017 and 2018 anniversary items (Capital at 150, bicentenary; biographies and notebook scholarship).
- 2020s: alienation and ethics revival, degrowth and Anthropocene, digital and AI capitalism, slavery and Marx.
- State any observation as a pattern in the sample, e.g., "the catalogue contains more X-titled entries after Y"; if counts by theme were not computed, do not quote figures.
- Gaps: repeat the limits stated in the Introduction in one sentence (English-only, database gaps, unverified or title-only items).
