## Section 1: The Eighteenth-Century Background — Historia Philosophica and Kant's Philosophy of History

**Section Purpose**: Establish the inherited models of philosophical historiography that Reinhold both drew on and sought to overcome, showing that his innovations respond to specific deficits in Brucker, Tennemann, and Kant.

**Main Claims**:
1. Brucker's *Historia critica philosophiae* (1742–44) established an eclectic-doxographical paradigm that treated the history of philosophy as a catalogue of opinions organized by sect and chronology, which Enlightenment readers increasingly found theoretically unsatisfying.
2. Kant's scattered remarks on the history of philosophy — especially the "philosophizing history of philosophy" of the Prize Essay fragments — introduced a transcendental criterion according to which history must be reconstructed a priori from the destiny of reason, and this proposal frames Reinhold's project even when he departs from it.
3. Tennemann's Kantian-inflected historiography represents an alternative route that Reinhold's Streit der Systeme thesis diverges from by treating conflict itself, rather than progress toward Kantian critique, as the structuring principle.

**Subsection 1.1: Brucker and the Eclectic Tradition**

**Papers**: Longo 2011, Catana 2008, Piaia & Santinello 2015, Schneider 1990
**Content**: Describe Brucker's method, his use of the "sect" as organizing category, and how later 18th-century readers (including Reinhold) came to see this approach as philosophically inert. Introduce Catana's thesis that Brucker's notion of "system" as a unit of historical analysis set the template that post-Kantians would radicalize.
**Connection**: Reinhold's talk of Systeme presupposes the Bruckerian unit but repurposes it as a dynamic rather than taxonomic category.

**Subsection 1.2: Kant's Philosophizing History of Philosophy**

**Papers**: Micheli 1980, La Rocca 2003, Sturm 2009, Brandt 1999
**Content**: Reconstruct Kant's late fragments on the history of philosophy, especially the claim that a "philosophical archaeology" must derive history from the nature of reason itself. Note the tension between Kant's transcendental criterion and empirical historiography.
**Connection**: Reinhold inherits the transcendental impulse but reworks it around the Satz des Bewusstseins and later around conflict-as-motor.

**Subsection 1.3: Tennemann and Post-Kantian Historiography**

**Papers**: Micheli 1992, Blackwell 1985, Piaia & Santinello 2015
**Content**: Outline Tennemann's eleven-volume *Geschichte der Philosophie* and its Kantian teleology. Contrast its progressivism with Reinhold's emphasis on unresolved systemic conflict.
**Connection**: Situates Reinhold as offering a third path between Bruckerian doxography and Tennemann's Kantian teleology.

**Section Summary**: The historiographical field Reinhold entered was structured by a tension between eclectic cataloguing and transcendental teleology; his Streit der Systeme thesis is intelligible as a response to both.
**Word Target**: 700–850 words

## Section 2: Reinhold's Streit der Systeme Thesis and the First-Principle Solution

**Section Purpose**: Present Reinhold's central historiographical claim that philosophy's history is driven by conflict among competing systems, and show how his demand for a single foundational principle (Satz des Bewusstseins) is simultaneously a systematic and a historiographical move.

**Main Claims**:
1. Reinhold diagnoses the history of philosophy as a persistent Streit der Systeme whose recurrence shows that previous philosophies lacked a truly first principle.
2. The Elementarphilosophie is designed as the terminus of this conflict: by grounding philosophy in the principle of consciousness, it claims to dissolve the conditions under which systematic conflict arises.
3. This move makes historiography internal to system-building — one cannot write the history of philosophy neutrally, because the "correct" history is the one that culminates in the resolution of the Streit.

**Subsection 2.1: The Diagnosis of Conflict**

**Papers**: Bondeli 1995, Frketich 2019, Imhof 2013, Ameriks 2000
**Content**: Reconstruct Reinhold's account in the *Beyträge* and *Fundament* of why philosophical systems have persistently failed to converge. Emphasize his claim that the failure is structural (absence of a first principle) rather than contingent.
**Connection**: This diagnosis is the hinge between his systematic ambitions and his historiography.

**Subsection 2.2: The First-Principle Solution**

**Papers**: Bondeli 1995, Frketich 2022, Ameriks 2005, di Giovanni 2005
**Content**: Explain the Satz des Bewusstseins as both foundational principle and historiographical terminus. Note the reflexive structure: history is understood only from the standpoint of the system that ends it.
**Connection**: Foreshadows the Hegelian gesture of reading the history of philosophy from its culmination.

**Section Summary**: Reinhold's historiography is inseparable from his systematic program; the Streit der Systeme thesis is what makes the Elementarphilosophie historically significant even after its collapse.
**Word Target**: 700–850 words

## Section 3: Reinhold's Career Evolution — Elementarphilosophie, Bardili, Philology

**Section Purpose**: Track Reinhold's phases and show that scholarly disagreement about continuity-versus-rupture across these phases is itself a historiographical question about how to read Reinhold.

**Main Claims**:
1. The Elementarphilosophie phase (1789–1794) attempts to complete Kant by grounding critical philosophy in the principle of consciousness.
2. The Bardili-Rationalist turn (c. 1799) shifts toward "rational realism" and logical grounding, which many readers treat as a break but which recent scholarship (Frketich, Imhof) argues preserves the Streit der Systeme framework.
3. The late "philological turn" — Reinhold's concern with linguistic analysis as a route to philosophical clarity — extends his historiographical sensitivity to the medium in which philosophical conflict is carried out.

**Subsection 3.1: The Elementarphilosophie Phase**

**Papers**: Bondeli 1995, Ameriks 2000, Breazeale 1982, Fabbianelli 2003
**Content**: Summarize the systematic ambitions and their reception; note Reinhold's role in popularizing Kant.
**Connection**: Establishes the systematic program against which later shifts are measured.

**Subsection 3.2: The Bardili Turn and Rational Realism**

**Papers**: Bondeli 2003, Frketich 2022, Imhof 2014, Lazzari 2004
**Content**: Describe Reinhold's embrace of Bardili's *Grundriss der ersten Logik* and the resulting "rational realism." Discuss whether this represents capitulation, continuity, or development.
**Connection**: The historiographical stakes: if there is continuity, Reinhold's mature view retains the Streit der Systeme framework; if rupture, his historiography must be periodized.

**Subsection 3.3: The Philological Turn**

**Papers**: Imhof 2013, Imhof 2017, Fabbianelli 2011, Onnasch 2010
**Content**: Outline Reinhold's late concern with language and synonyms as a philosophical method. Relate this to his ongoing diagnosis that philosophical conflict is rooted in terminological confusion.
**Connection**: Suggests that Reinhold's later work is not an abandonment of historiography but its linguistic deepening.

**Section Summary**: The phase-structure of Reinhold's career is itself historiographically charged, and its interpretation shapes how one reads the Streit der Systeme thesis.
**Word Target**: 700–850 words

## Section 4: Contemporary Scholarly Debates

**Section Purpose**: Survey the main positions in the recent literature on Reinhold's historiography and identify the key fault lines.

**Main Claims**:
1. Ameriks reads Reinhold primarily through the lens of the "fate of autonomy" and treats the historiography as symptomatic of post-Kantian anxieties about foundationalism.
2. Bondeli offers the most sustained reconstruction of the developmental arc and defends a thesis of underlying continuity organized around the problem of first principles.
3. Frketich, Imhof, and Posesorski have recently foregrounded the historiographical dimension as such, arguing that Reinhold's philosophy of the history of philosophy is a distinct contribution irreducible to his systematic projects.

**Subsection 4.1: Ameriks on Post-Kantian Historiography**

**Papers**: Ameriks 2000, Ameriks 2005, Ameriks 2006, Ameriks 2012
**Content**: Summarize Ameriks's narrative and his placement of Reinhold within it.
**Connection**: Provides the dominant Anglophone framing; subsequent debates largely define themselves against it.

**Subsection 4.2: Bondeli and the Continuity Thesis**

**Papers**: Bondeli 1995, Bondeli 2003, Bondeli & Schrader 2003
**Content**: Present the developmental reading and its textual basis.
**Connection**: Anchors the German-language reception.

**Subsection 4.3: Frketich, Imhof, Posesorski — Historiography as Such**

**Papers**: Frketich 2019, Frketich 2022, Imhof 2013, Imhof 2014, Posesorski 2012, Posesorski 2018
**Content**: Reconstruct the argument that Reinhold's historiography is a philosophically distinct project, and that the Streit der Systeme thesis has systematic content independent of the Elementarphilosophie.
**Connection**: This is the frontier of current debate and the one most directly relevant to the research project.

**Section Summary**: The field has moved from treating Reinhold's historiography as an appendix to his systematics toward treating it as a core contribution; the review positions itself within this newer trajectory.
**Word Target**: 700–850 words

## Section 5: Reinhold's Afterlife — Fichte, Schelling, the Romantics, and Hegel

**Section Purpose**: Trace the uptake and transformation of Reinhold's historiographical insights by the major post-Kantians, culminating in Hegel's dialectical history.

**Main Claims**:
1. Fichte inherits the first-principle demand but reworks it as the self-positing I, treating the history of philosophy as the history of standpoints on freedom.
2. Schelling and the early Romantics (Schlegel, Novalis) radicalize the Streit der Systeme thesis by denying that any single system can terminate the conflict, replacing resolution with ongoing dialogue or aesthetic synthesis.
3. Hegel's mature historiography — history of philosophy as the self-unfolding of the concept — is both the heir to Reinhold's thesis (conflict as engine) and its overcoming (conflict as dialectical necessity rather than symptom of foundational failure).

**Subsection 5.1: Fichte's Reception**

**Papers**: Breazeale 2013, Martin 1997, di Giovanni 2005
**Content**: Discuss Fichte's critique of Reinhold's principle and his own historiographical gestures.
**Connection**: Shows how the first-principle move survives Reinhold's specific formulation.

**Subsection 5.2: Schelling and the Early Romantics**

**Papers**: Beiser 2002, Frank 1997, Millán-Zaibert 2007, Nassar 2013
**Content**: Outline how Schelling's system-program and Schlegel's/Novalis's fragmentary historiographies respond to Reinhold's diagnosis without accepting his solution.
**Connection**: Illustrates an alternative lineage in which the Streit is accepted as permanent.

**Subsection 5.3: Hegel's Dialectical Historiography**

**Papers**: Forster 1998, Düsing 1983, Jaeschke 2003, Bowman 2013, Ameriks 2006
**Content**: Present Hegel's *Vorlesungen über die Geschichte der Philosophie* as simultaneously continuing and transforming Reinhold's project. Emphasize debts (history structured by systemic conflict) and departures (conflict internal to the concept, not a symptom of foundational deficit).
**Connection**: Closes the arc from Brucker through Reinhold to Hegel.

**Section Summary**: The post-Kantian reception shows that Reinhold's historiographical thesis had a longer life than his systematic program, and that its major transformations define the shape of Idealist and Romantic philosophy of history.
**Word Target**: 800–950 words

**Content**:
