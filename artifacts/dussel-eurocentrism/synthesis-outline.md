## Introduction: Why Dussel's Eurocentrism Critique, and Why Now (≈400 words)

**Purpose**: Frame the review's central question — what is distinctive in Dussel's account of Eurocentrism, and how has it been received and contested? Establish that the literature does not converge on a single reading: Dussel is read variably as a foundational decolonial thinker, a heterodox Marxist of the world-system, a Levinasian ethicist of alterity, and a liberation-theological critic of Western reason. The introduction should foreground three live questions that organize the review: (i) does the "myth of modernity" thesis succeed as historiography? (ii) does transmodernity offer a genuine alternative to both Eurocentric universalism and postmodern relativism? (iii) does Dussel's framework escape the Eurocentrism it diagnoses?

**Key Papers**: @dussel1995invention (the canonical primary text), @mendieta2000underside (anglophone framing), @maldonado2011dussel (decolonial reception), @timmenga2025critique (recent re-evaluation), @vargas2005eurocentrism (the self-application worry).

## Section 1: The Myth of Modernity and the 1492 Thesis (≈700 words)

**Section Purpose**: Reconstruct Dussel's core philosophical argument with analytic precision, distinguishing the historiographical claim (1492 as inception of modernity, Europe as pre-1492 periphery) from the ideological claim ("myth" as the rational self-justification of colonial violence).

**Main Claims**:
1. For Dussel, modernity has two birthdates and two faces: an emancipatory rationality and a sacrificial-Eurocentric apparatus that "justifies" violence as civilizational uplift.
2. The historiographical premise — that pre-1492 Europe was peripheral within an Afro-Eurasian inter-regional system — is load-bearing but under-examined in Anglophone reception.

**Subsection 1.1: The Two-Faced Modernity**
- Papers: @dussel1995invention, @dussel1997architectonic, @alcoff2013philosophy, @stromback2024ego.
- Content: Lay out Dussel's Eclipse-of-the-Other thesis and the ego conquiro as the paradigm preceding (and underwriting) the cogito. Stromback's reconstruction of the ego conquiro thesis sharpens the philosophical stakes; Alcoff's commentary on the Anti-Cartesian Meditations marks the Anglophone reception's pivot toward taking Dussel's genealogy seriously.

**Subsection 1.2: The World-System Reframing**
- Papers: @dussel2013ethics, @dussel2009newage, @pachon2012historiografia, @pimenta2025evolution.
- Content: Trace how Dussel's late work (especially the "Beyond Hellenocentrism" chapter of the 2013 Ethics) draws on Abu-Lughod and Frank to argue Europe was peripheral within a polycentric Afro-Eurasian system, and how the 2009 "New Age" essay extends this into a polycentric history of philosophy. Pachón Soto and Pimenta supply the close exegesis.

**Section Summary**: Establishes what Dussel actually argues, and flags that the historiographical premise — though central — receives less critical scrutiny than the ethical apparatus, anticipating critiques in Section 5.

## Section 2: Career Development — From Levinasian Alterity to Transmodern World-System (≈500 words)

**Section Purpose**: Show that Dussel's account of Eurocentrism is not a single doctrine but evolved across three phases: (1) the Argentine Levinasian–phenomenological liberation philosophy of the 1970s; (2) the Apel/Habermas dialogue of the 1990s, which forced an articulation of formal-discursive moments alongside material-ethical ones; (3) the late world-system/transmodern synthesis culminating in the 2013 Ethics and the Politics of Liberation.

**Main Claims**:
1. Each phase deepens, rather than abandons, the prior — the Levinasian alterity remains a substrate when world-system analysis is added.
2. The Apel encounter reshaped the form of the critique without softening its content (contra readings that see Dussel as concessive to discourse ethics).

**Papers**: @martin2019genealogia (early phase), @ball2019praxis (Levinas-Marx fusion), @dussel1997principles (self-narration of the Apel encounter), @dussel1997architectonic, @dussel2007fraternity, @dussel2012transmodernity, @dussel2003beyond, @maldonado2008dussel, @pimenta2025evolution.

**Content**: Use Pimenta and Maldonado-Torres for periodization scaffolding; use Dussel's own architectonic and "Principles, Mediations" essays as primary self-narration; use Martín and Ball to anchor the early phase; close with the 2007 "Fraternity to Solidarity" piece marking the late shift to politics.

**Section Summary**: Frames the rest of the review by establishing that "Dussel's account of Eurocentrism" is a moving target whose changes track shifting interlocutors (Levinas → Apel → Wallerstein/Quijano).

## Section 3: Transmodernity as Positive Program (≈450 words)

**Section Purpose**: Examine the constructive payoff of the critique. Transmodernity is not anti-modern rejection but an attempt to take up modernity's emancipatory rationality from "the underside" while affirming the alterity of the colonized Other. Distinguish it from postmodernism (which Dussel reads as Eurocentric self-deconstruction) and from anti-modernism.

**Main Claims**:
1. Transmodernity is meant as a pluriversal project, not a particularist alternative universalism.
2. Whether it succeeds in this depends on contested philosophical machinery — the analectic method and trans-ontology.

**Papers**: @dussel2012transmodernity, @mills2018dussel, @deere2017transmodern, @irvine2017ontological / @irvine2011ontological, @grosfoguel2012decolonizing.

**Content**: Use Mills for accessible reconstruction, Deere for a materialist revision of "the Other," Irvine for the ontological objection that the trans-ontology of alterity rests on the very metaphysics it claims to exceed. Grosfoguel's pluriversalism essay shows how decolonial allies appropriate transmodernity.

**Section Summary**: Sets up the question — is transmodernity a stable position or an unstable mediation between universalism and particularism? — that the critical literature (Section 5) presses.

## Section 4: Dussel and the Decolonial / Postcolonial Constellation (≈600 words)

**Section Purpose**: Locate Dussel within the modernity/coloniality/decoloniality (MCD) network and against Anglophone postcolonial theory. The defining axis is the postcolonial/decolonial distinction: postcolonialism centers nineteenth-century British/French empire and discourse-analytic methods; the decolonial school anchored by Dussel and Quijano centers 1492 and world-system analysis.

**Main Claims**:
1. Dussel's 1492 periodization, his refusal to grant primacy to the linguistic turn, and his theological-Marxian heritage mark the decolonial project's distinctiveness from postcolonialism.
2. Within the MCD network there is a real division of labor (Quijano: structural sociology; Mignolo: epistemic/semiotic; Dussel: philosophical/ethical) — and points of internal disagreement that the literature often elides.

**Subsection 4.1: Dussel's Place in the MCD Network**
- Papers: @maldonado2011dussel, @maldonado2011decolonial, @grosfoguel2009decolonial, @misoczky2019contributions, @mignolo2010globalization, @mignolo2000cosmopolis, @mignolo2020invisible.
- Content: Maldonado-Torres positions Dussel as foundational to the "decolonial turn"; Misoczky pairs Dussel with Quijano; Mignolo's border thinking reconfigures Dussel's geopolitical framing.

**Subsection 4.2: Against Postcolonialism**
- Papers: @grosfoguel2011decolonizing, @lindner2025provincializing, @bhambra2014connected, @morana2008coloniality, @mignolo2011epistemic, @dube2006unbecoming.
- Content: Grosfoguel's decolonial critique of postcolonial studies is the canonical statement; Lindner's "provincializing or delinking" is the most recent re-litigation. Bhambra's connected sociologies offer a convergence position. The Moraña–Dussel–Jáuregui volume stages the encounter directly.

**Subsection 4.3: African and Caribbean Inflections**
- Papers: @ndlovu2020globalcoloniality, @vellem2017unthinking, @grosfoguel2012decolonizing.
- Content: Ndlovu-Gatsheni and Vellem extend the 1492 thesis but raise the question whether African historical experience and African spirituality fit the Latin Americanist starting point.

**Section Summary**: Dussel's account of Eurocentrism is more philosophically ambitious than Quijano's sociology and more historically grounded than Mignolo's epistemic-turn version, but the network's internal disagreements are under-theorized in the secondary literature.

## Section 5: Reception and Criticism — Does Dussel Escape Eurocentrism? (≈600 words)

**Section Purpose**: Stage the strongest critical objections, organized by the kind of pressure they exert. The unifying question: does Dussel's framework reproduce, at the meta-philosophical or methodological level, the Eurocentrism it diagnoses?

**Main Claims**:
1. Internal-tension critiques (Vargas, Sharman) press the self-application worry: Dussel's anti-Eurocentric program relies on European theoretical apparatus.
2. Feminist critiques (Schutte, Lange) challenge the gendered construction of the indigenous Other.
3. Ontological critiques (Irvine) target the trans-ontology underwriting the analectic method.
4. Recent defenses (Timmenga et al., Stromback) argue the framework remains coherent under revision.

**Subsection 5.1: The Self-Application Worry**
- Papers: @vargas2005eurocentrism, @sharman2011latin, @vizcaino2021liberation.
- Content: Vargas argues liberation philosophy's heavy reliance on European categories sits uneasily with its anti-Eurocentric program; Sharman extends this to historiographical language; Vizcaíno argues Dussel's anti-fetishist method retains a Eurocentric anthropology of religion that misrepresents Afro-Atlantic traditions.

**Subsection 5.2: Feminist Critique**
- Papers: @schutte1991origins, @lange1998burnt, @velez2020decolonial.
- Content: Schutte's foundational critique of authoritarian/patriarchal residues; Lange's argument that Dussel's transmodern framework risks instrumentalizing indigenous peoples as a foil for European self-criticism and is closer to postmodernism than he allows; Vélez and Tuana on the male-dominated MCD canon.

**Subsection 5.3: Ontological and Methodological Objections**
- Papers: @irvine2011ontological, @sager2016liberation.
- Content: Irvine's charge that the trans-ontology is internally inconsistent; Sager and Spencer's call for a more fallibilist, Deweyan-pragmatist liberation method.

**Subsection 5.4: Defenses and Sympathetic Revisions**
- Papers: @timmenga2025critique, @stromback2024ego, @allen2021decolonizing, @maldonado2008against, @alcoff2013philosophy.
- Content: Timmenga et al. defend the coherence of Dussel's alterity-and-immanent-critique combination; Stromback elaborates ego conquiro toward explicit pluralism; Maldonado-Torres develops "coloniality of being" as a sympathetic extension.

**Section Summary**: The most persistent unresolved tension is between (a) Dussel's claim to ground critique in the alterity of the Other and (b) the European philosophical machinery (Levinas, Marx, Apel) by which that ground is articulated. Whether this is a fatal contradiction or a productive one is the live question.

## Conclusion: Unresolved Tensions and Open Questions (≈250 words)

**Purpose**: Synthesize the review's findings and identify what remains contested.

**Content**:
- Three positions on Dussel's Eurocentrism critique now stand in the literature: foundational-decolonial (Maldonado-Torres, Grosfoguel), internally-tensioned-but-revisable (Stromback, Vizcaíno, Timmenga et al.), and irrecoverably-Eurocentric-at-the-meta-level (Vargas, with Lange and Schutte adding gender).
- The historiographical premise of the 1492 thesis — that Europe was peripheral within a polycentric Afro-Eurasian system — has been less examined in Anglophone literature than the ethical superstructure built atop it (a gap noted by @pachon2012historiografia).
- Transmodernity's status as a pluriversal alternative remains contested, with the analectic and trans-ontological machinery the principal target.
- The relation between Dussel's decolonial Latin Americanism and African (Vellem, Ndlovu-Gatsheni) and Caribbean (Césaire via Grosfoguel) decolonial thought is a productive but under-elaborated frontier.
- Open question for future work: can Dussel's framework absorb the feminist and Afro-Atlantic critiques without collapsing into the postmodernism it rejects?
