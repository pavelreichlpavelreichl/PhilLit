# Literature Review Plan: Enrique Dussel's Account of Eurocentrism

## Research Idea Summary

This review examines Enrique Dussel's critique of Eurocentrism: his core claims (the "myth of modernity," the 1492 origin of modernity, Europe's peripheral status pre-conquest), the development of his thinking from early liberation philosophy through the Frankfurt-influenced ethics to the politics of liberation, his dialogue with decolonial and postcolonial thinkers, and the reception and criticism of his work.

## Key Research Questions

1. What are the substantive claims of Dussel's critique of Eurocentrism, and how do they cohere?
2. How did Dussel's account develop from the 1970s liberation philosophy to the transmodernity project?
3. How does Dussel's critique compare with and diverge from other decolonial/postcolonial critiques?
4. What major objections (philosophical, historiographical, political) have been raised against Dussel?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: Dussel's Core Critique of Eurocentrism

**Focus**: Dussel's substantive philosophical claims about Eurocentrism, including the "myth of modernity," 1492 as the origin of modernity, and the world-system reframing of Europe's role. Covers primary texts and close exegesis.

**Key Questions**:
- What does Dussel mean by the "myth of modernity" and its sacrificial logic?
- How does he argue Europe was peripheral before 1492?
- What normative conclusions follow from his historical reframing?

**Search Terms**: "Dussel myth of modernity", "Dussel Eurocentrism", "invention of the Americas Dussel", "1492 modernity Dussel", "Dussel transmodernity", "philosophy of liberation Eurocentrism", "Dussel world-system"

---

### Domain 2: Development of Dussel's Thought

**Focus**: The intellectual trajectory from early Latin American liberation philosophy (1970s) through the Apel/Habermas dialogue and Levinasian ethics to the three-volume Politics of Liberation. Tracks how the Eurocentrism critique evolved across these phases.

**Key Questions**:
- How did Dussel's early liberation philosophy frame Eurocentrism?
- What did the encounter with discourse ethics (Apel) change in his account?
- How does the late "Politics of Liberation" reformulate the critique historically?

**Search Terms**: "Dussel intellectual biography", "Dussel Apel debate", "Dussel Levinas liberation", "philosophy of liberation history", "Dussel ethics liberation", "Dussel politics of liberation", "analectic method Dussel"

---

### Domain 3: Dussel and Decolonial Thought

**Focus**: Dussel's relation to the modernity/coloniality/decoloniality network—Quijano, Mignolo, Maldonado-Torres, Escobar, Grosfoguel—and shared concepts like coloniality of power, geopolitics of knowledge, and transmodernity. Examines convergences, divisions of labor, and disputes within the group.

**Key Questions**:
- How do Dussel's and Quijano's accounts of coloniality/modernity interlock?
- What does Mignolo take from and modify in Dussel?
- Where do internal decolonial disagreements locate Dussel?

**Search Terms**: "modernity coloniality decoloniality", "Quijano coloniality power", "Mignolo darker side modernity", "Maldonado-Torres coloniality being", "decolonial turn Latin America", "transmodernity decolonial", "geopolitics of knowledge"

---

### Domain 4: Dussel and Postcolonial/Subaltern Critics

**Focus**: Comparison and dialogue with postcolonial theory (Said, Spivak, Bhabha) and South Asian subaltern studies, plus African critics (Mudimbe, Wynter, Mbembe). Maps where Dussel's Latin Americanist, theological, and Marxian commitments diverge from Anglophone postcolonialism.

**Key Questions**:
- How does Dussel's 1492-centered narrative differ from Said-style Orientalism critique?
- What is at stake in the postcolonial vs decolonial distinction for Eurocentrism?
- How do African and Caribbean critiques converge with or pressure Dussel?

**Search Terms**: "decolonial postcolonial difference", "Said Orientalism Latin America", "Spivak subaltern decolonial", "Mudimbe invention Africa", "Sylvia Wynter coloniality", "postcolonial Latin America debate", "subaltern studies Latin America"

---

### Domain 5: Reception, Criticism, and Limits

**Focus**: Critical reception of Dussel: charges of historiographical overreach, romanticizing the non-European, theological residues, gender blind spots, and tensions with Marxist and analytic critics. Includes sympathetic internal critique and external philosophical objections.

**Key Questions**:
- What historiographical objections target the 1492 thesis and world-system claims?
- What feminist and gender critiques have been raised against liberation philosophy?
- Are Dussel's normative grounds (Levinasian, theological) defensible to secular critics?

**Search Terms**: "Dussel critique objections", "liberation philosophy feminist critique", "Dussel Marxism critique", "Castro-Gomez Dussel", "decolonial critique limits", "Dussel theology critique", "Eurocentrism historiography critique"

---

## Coverage Rationale

Domains move from internal exegesis (1) through diachronic development (2) to lateral comparisons (3, 4) and finally critical reception (5), ensuring both sympathetic reconstruction and adversarial scrutiny.

## Expected Gaps

Limited Anglophone engagement with late Dussel; uneven gender analysis; sparse empirical-historiographical assessment of the 1492 thesis.

- **Total domains**: 5
- **Estimated papers**: 50-70
- **Key positions**: Dusselian decolonial, Mignolo/Quijano variants, postcolonial alternatives, Marxist/feminist/historiographical critics
