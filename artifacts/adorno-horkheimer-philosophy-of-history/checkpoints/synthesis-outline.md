# Literature Review Outline

**Research Project**: Adorno's and Horkheimer's Philosophy of History — a comprehensive survey of the major issues, questions, claims, and debates
**Date**: 2026-07-10
**Total Literature Base**: ~120 pre-dedup entries across 7 domains (target ~60-80 distinct works cited)
**Guiding meta-question** (threaded through all sections): Does early Frankfurt School critical theory offer *a philosophy of history*, or a *critique of philosophy of history as such*?

---

## Introduction

**Purpose**: Frame the research space and establish the organizing tension.

**Content**:
- Open with the problem: Adorno and Horkheimer inherit the German tradition of speculative philosophy of history (Kant, Hegel, Marx) yet appear to turn its categories — progress, universal history, totality, the world-historical subject — against themselves. Frame the review's central question: is the result a substantive philosophy of history, a meta-critique of the genre, or a "melancholy science" that suspends the question (O'Connor 2008; Sherman 2016)?
- State the four cross-cutting problems that recur across domains: (1) the entwinement of myth and enlightenment; (2) the dissolution of the nature/history antithesis; (3) progress/catastrophe and a totality without a subject; (4) the normative status of a totalizing historical critique.
- Note the internal differentiation the review will preserve: Adorno vs. Horkheimer; the Benjaminian source and foil; the Hegelian-Marxist baseline; and the reception (Habermas onward).
- Preview the seven-section structure and the descriptive, balanced approach (deliberately over-sampling critical/dissenting literature).

**Key Papers**: horkheimeradorno2002dialectic; oconnor2008adorno; adorno1984idea; allen2016endofprogress; habermas1987philosophical
**Word Target**: 500 words

## Section 1: The *Dialectic of Enlightenment* as Philosophy of History — Myth, Reason, and Self-Destruction

**Section Purpose**: Establish the canonical text and its central entwinement thesis, then open the debate over the *status* of its historical narrative — the anchor for every later section.

**Main Claims**:
1. The book advances a genealogical thesis that "myth is already enlightenment, and enlightenment reverts to mythology," linking the domination of outer and inner nature to the formation of the self.
2. Scholars disagree fundamentally over whether this narrative is fatalistic/totalizing, aporetic-by-design, disclosive, therapeutic, or preparatory.

**Subsection 1.1: The entwinement thesis and the domination of nature**
- Papers: horkheimeradorno2002dialectic; stone2014adorno (D1); nelson2011revisiting; hulatt2016reason
- Content: State the thesis; Hulatt's tripartite model (mimesis / self-preservation / rationality) complicating a two-term reading; Nelson on the double function of "nature" (ideology-critique vs. ethical register).

**Subsection 1.2: The Odyssey excursus as genealogy of the subject**
- Papers: comay2000adornos; allen2024odysseus; bermingham2018seductress
- Content: Odysseus as prototype of bourgeois subjectivity; Comay on self-binding/self-mutilation; W. S. Allen distinguishing cunning/*metis* as mimetic intelligence; Bermingham's feminist immanent critique (gendered subjectivity as structural gap).

**Subsection 1.3: What kind of "philosophy of history"? — scope and status of the narrative**
- Papers: foster2001dialectic; honneth2000possibility; noppen2012reflective; evans2020dialectic; kent2024semblance; setiya2023human
- Content: Competing genre-classifications — genealogy critique (Foster), disclosive critique (Honneth), preparation for a positive concept of enlightenment (Noppen), parody of anti-enlightenment thought (Evans), anti-fatalist recovery of myth (Kent), and immanent-critique-under-totality tested from analytic ethical naturalism (Setiya). Introduce the Habermasian performative-contradiction charge (habermas1987philosophical) here as the pivot that will be adjudicated in Section 7.

**Connection**: Frames the meta-question concretely: the same text is read as both a totalizing dead end and a deliberately aporetic critical resource.
**Section Summary**: The entwinement thesis is settled as the book's core; its *modality* (fatalist vs. emancipatory, argumentative vs. performative) remains the live dispute.
**Word Target**: 1000 words

## Section 2: Natural History (*Naturgeschichte*) — Dissolving the Nature/History Antithesis

**Section Purpose**: Reconstruct the conceptual hinge of Adorno's distinctive philosophy of history — the 1932 programme and its genealogy — and assess whether it is a stable doctrine or a shifting motif.

**Main Claims**:
1. Adorno's 1932 lecture dissolves the nature/history antithesis into a single category: nature appears as historically sedimented, history recurs with nature-like (mythic, "second-nature") compulsion.
2. The concept's genealogy (Lukács's second nature, Benjamin's allegory/transience) and its status (substantive doctrine vs. therapeutic device) are contested, as is its persistence into the mature work.

**Subsection 2.1: The 1932 programme and its sources**
- Papers: adorno1984idea; escuelacruz2014hacia; stahl2013lukacs; buckmorss1977origin
- Content: The anti-Heideggerian, anti-historicist polemic; Lukács's "second nature" (*Theory of the Novel*) and Benjamin's allegory as the two named antecedents; Buck-Morss on the biographical-intellectual formation.

**Subsection 2.2: Stable doctrine or shifting motif? — unity vs. therapy**
- Papers: pensky2004naturalhistory; whyman2016understanding; helmling2003constellation
- Content: Pensky's unificatory reading (three strands unified by transience) against Whyman's deflationary "therapeutic" reading; Helmling on natural history as bound to method (constellation vs. dialectical image).

**Subsection 2.3: Persistence and afterlife — aesthetics, Marx, ecology**
- Papers: pensky2021naturalhistory; stone2006disenchantment; breitenstein2021zerschlagen; cook2011adorno; wankhammer2024nature; friedlander2024walter
- Content: Continuity into *Aesthetic Theory* (Pensky 2021) and *Negative Dialectics*' second model via Marx's *Naturwüchsigkeit* (Breitenstein); disenchantment/re-enchantment (Stone); the ecological afterlife (Cook, Wankhammer's postcritique intervention); Friedlander's Benjamin-centered counterweight (natural history as Benjamin's own project). Note the plan's flagged gap: the Anthropocene/climate framing (developed further in Section 3).

**Connection**: *Naturgeschichte* is the theoretical precondition for treating myth and enlightenment as dialectically entangled (Section 1) and for the "world spirit and natural history" model (Section 3).
**Section Summary**: Consensus on the concept's centrality; disagreement on whether it is doctrine, method, or therapy — and on how much is Adorno's vs. Benjamin's.
**Word Target**: 900 words

## Section 3: Adorno's Negative Philosophy of History — Progress, Catastrophe, Universal History, Totality without a Subject

**Section Purpose**: Reconstruct Adorno's most explicit historical categories and confront the domain's sharpest paradoxes.

**Main Claims**:
1. Adorno reconstrues "progress" so that it is neither affirmed nor denied, holding progress and catastrophe together (the "slingshot to the megaton bomb").
2. He offers a *negative* universal history and a negative totality ("the whole is the false") while denying history a constituting subject — a construction whose coherence is itself debated.

**Subsection 3.1: Progress reconstrued — neither affirmed nor denied**
- Papers: frelek2025hope; hohendahl2013progressrevisited; cidam2020twotrack; weisbrod2020lowith
- Content: The 1962 "Progress" essay; its theological genealogy (Augustine, Kant, Benjamin — Hohendahl); the "two-track" reading (Çıdam: ethical imperative + politics of remembrance); the Löwith comparison (Weisbrod) on suffering as ontological vs. political.

**Subsection 3.2: Universal history after Auschwitz**
- Papers: burdman2020auschwitz; johnson2019anthropocene; sherman2016negativedialectics; lunajimenez2020naturalezaehistoria
- Content: The negative retention (not abandonment) of universal history; Adorno vs. Lyotard on writing history after injustice (Burdman); the extension to a "negative universal history" of the Anthropocene (Johnson); "qualified rejection of universal history" (Sherman).

**Subsection 3.3: Totality without a subject; the Hegelian reworking**
- Papers: stone2014adornohegel; baumann2011concreteuniversal; oconnor2011reconception; allenw2025kantadornoforms; bell2017liferuins
- Content: Whether a negative totality can obtain without a world-spirit-style subject; Stone on reshaped reconciliation (differences co-existing); Baumann on the concrete universal (leaving open whether Adorno escapes Hegel's aporias); W. S. Allen (2025) on thinking history "without the whole" via Kant's Third Critique; natural-history/catastrophe link (Bell).

**Connection**: Directly addresses the meta-question — is this substantive doctrine, meta-critique of the genre, or therapeutic suspension? Feeds the Hegel/Marx comparison (Section 4) and the reception debate (Section 7).
**Section Summary**: Adorno's categories are reconstructed with growing precision; their internal coherence (negative totality without a subject) remains the central unresolved puzzle.
**Word Target**: 1000 words

## Section 4: The Hegelian-Marxist Legacy — Continuities and Breaks

**Section Purpose**: Supply the genealogical baseline against which the originality and alleged aporias of the Frankfurt position are measured.

**Main Claims**:
1. Critical theory retains Hegelian determinate negation and immanent critique while refusing reconciliation and the "positive Absolute."
2. It inherits but renegotiates the Marxist-Lukácsian problematic of totality and the proletariat-as-subject once the revolutionary subject fails to materialize.

**Subsection 4.1: The transformation of Hegel's dialectic**
- Papers: marcuse1941reason; stone2014adorno (D4); finlayson2014hegel; finlayson2017hegel; rose2009hegelcontra; marasco2015highway
- Content: Marcuse's founding "positive" reading of Hegelian negativity as baseline; Finlayson's challenge to the "Continuity Thesis" of immanent criticism; Rose's speculative-Hegel test; Marasco's "highway of despair" (negativity without guaranteed reconciliation).

**Subsection 4.2: Marxism, totality, and the vanishing subject**
- Papers: jay1984marxism; jay1973dialectical; wiggershaus1994frankfurt; feenberg2014praxis; aratobreines1979younglukacs; lowy1979lukacsromanticism
- Content: Jay on totality's "adventures" from Lukács to Habermas; interdisciplinary materialism's departure from economism (Jay 1973, Wiggershaus); the philosophy-of-praxis lineage (Feenberg); the origin of the identical subject-object (Arato/Breines, Löwy).

**Subsection 4.3: What survives of Marxian political economy?**
- Papers: postone1993time; lichtenstein2022adorno; schmidt1971concept; gindner2023totality; bueno2024end; stahl2018lukacs; kavoulakos2017lukacs
- Content: Value-form/abstract-domination reading (Postone; Lichtenstein on Adorno's structural domination); Schmidt's dialectical concept of nature; reification's afterlife (Kavoulakos, Stahl); negative totality detached from the proletarian subject (Gindner; Bueno on Lukács-with-Adorno).

**Connection**: Establishes whether the break with Marxist teleology entails abandoning the philosophy of history or reconstituting it negatively — the pivot of the meta-question.
**Section Summary**: Critical theory is best read as an internal transformation, not a rupture, of the Hegel-Lukács-Marx lineage; the fate of the revolutionary subject is the decisive break.
**Word Target**: 900 words

## Section 5: Horkheimer's Trajectory — Materialism, the Critique of Reason, and the Theological Turn

**Section Purpose**: Treat Horkheimer as an independent theorist of history and surface the internal differentiation within the "Frankfurt" position.

**Main Claims**:
1. Horkheimer's philosophy of history develops across three stages — 1930s interdisciplinary materialism, the wartime critique of instrumental/subjective reason, and the late pessimistic-theological "longing for the wholly other."
2. Whether these stages form a continuity or a rupture — and how Horkheimer differs from Adorno — is contested.

**Subsection 5.1: The 1930s materialist programme**
- Papers: horkheimer1982traditional; horkheimer1993between; berendzen2008postmetaphysical; oneilluebel2004horkheimer; handelman2019trouble; abromeit2011maxhorkheimer
- Content: "Traditional and Critical Theory" as the pivot text; materialism as a postmetaphysical "stance" (Berendzen); the anti-positivist polemic and the Neurath exchange (O'Neill/Uebel, Handelman); Abromeit on the pre-1941 independence of Horkheimer's model.

**Subsection 5.2: The wartime critique of reason**
- Papers: horkheimer1947eclipse; freyenhagen2023linguistic; shea2021postmetaphysical
- Content: *Eclipse of Reason* on objective vs. subjective reason; Freyenhagen's Wittgensteinian defense against the Habermasian charge; Shea on a "problematic return to metaphysics."

**Subsection 5.3: Late pessimism and the theological turn — continuity or rupture?**
- Papers: horkheimer1978dawn; gunderson2012horkheimers; michaelis2022critical; siebert2005critical; nagldocekal2020nach; walentowicz2006max; stirk1992maxhorkheimer; crawford2025critical
- Content: The "wholly other"; continuity theses (Gunderson on compassion; Walentowicz's three-stage unity) vs. Crawford's critical-historicist account of the theological turn as a Cold-War reception construction; the Schopenhauer (Michaelis) and Kantian-practical-reason (Nagl-Docekal) genealogies; Stirk on the late conservatism.

**Connection**: The Horkheimer/Adorno difference (often collapsed — a gap flagged in the plan) sharpens what is genuinely co-authored in the *Dialectic* vs. individually held.
**Section Summary**: A developmental trajectory is well documented; continuity-vs-rupture and the Horkheimer-Adorno divergence remain open and under-theorized.
**Word Target**: 900 words

## Section 6: Benjamin's Messianic Historiography — Source and Foil

**Section Purpose**: Position Benjamin as the indispensable third figure whose messianic-materialist historiography both shapes and marks the limits of the Adorno-Horkheimer position.

**Main Claims**:
1. Benjamin's theses offer a redemptive, constellational alternative to progressive/historicist history ("empty homogeneous time," the angel of history, weak messianic power, *Jetztzeit*).
2. The Adorno-Benjamin methodological disputes (dialectical images, mediation, the theological) define both the debt and the departure — raising whether the Frankfurt philosophy of history is a "secularized" or disavowed Benjaminian messianism.

**Subsection 6.1: The theses and the critique of progress**
- Papers: lowy2005firealarm; pensky2010contributions; khatib2017wherethepast; wolin1994aesthetic; tiedemann1983historical
- Content: Löwy's line-by-line reading (weak messianic power as collective, this-worldly interruption); the angel of history against Kantian progress (Pensky 2010); "tradition of the oppressed" (Khatib); the framing dispute — "historical materialism or political messianism?" (Tiedemann vs. Wolin).

**Subsection 6.2: Constellation, dialectical image, and the mediation dispute**
- Papers: buckmorss1989dialectics; buckmorss1977origins; comay1999perverse; feldman2011notdialectical; recoder2019adornos
- Content: The Arcades method and Adorno's "Hornberg letter" objections; Feldman on the chiastic structure of Adorno's "not dialectical enough" charge; Recoder on Adorno's editorial "orthodoxy"; Comay on the "perverse" dialectic.

**Subsection 6.3: Messianism retained or expelled?**
- Papers: beardsworth2005benjamin; brittain2010politicaltheology; bohn2019historiography; moir2016walterbenjamin
- Content: Beardsworth's parallel-not-derivative reading (dialectical image vs. the fragmentary *Dialectic*); Adorno's cautious retention of messianic figures (Brittain, contra Agamben); Horkheimer's documented resistance to Benjamin's *Eingedenken* (Bohn); what "remains" of Benjamin's philosophy of history (Moir).

**Connection**: The secularization question directly informs Horkheimer's theological turn (Section 5) and Adorno's negative universal history (Section 3).
**Section Summary**: Benjamin is source and foil; the mediation dispute crystallizes the Frankfurt School's ambivalence toward the theological-redemptive register it cannot fully adopt or abandon.
**Word Target**: 900 words

## Section 7: Reception, Critique, and Contemporary Debates

**Section Purpose**: Provide the critical counterweight — objections, rescues, and reassessments — ensuring the review is not confirmatory.

**Main Claims**:
1. Habermas's "philosophy of consciousness" / performative-contradiction charge frames the entire reception; its force and the adequacy of rejoinders are contested.
2. Later Frankfurt theorists rescue, replace, or abandon the philosophy of history at contested cost; external (analytic, decolonial, feminist) critics press the totalization, normative-deficit, and Eurocentrism charges.

**Subsection 7.1: The Habermas debate and the *Historical Materialism* exchange**
- Papers: habermas1987philosophical; honneth1979communication; cook2000criticalstratagems; finlayson2003theory; vaki2005adornocontra; merawi2018limits
- Content: The performative-contradiction/normative-deficit charge; the Cook–Finlayson–Vaki cycle on whether immanent critique stands without discourse-theoretic foundations; the cost to aesthetic truth (Merawi). Cross-reference the anti-Habermasian rejoinders already introduced (freyenhagen2024why in D1; stone2014adorno).

**Subsection 7.2: Rescue, replacement, or abandonment — the generational transmission**
- Papers: wellmer2007adorno; honneth2009pathologies; bernstein2017unresolved; albert1985treatise; bottomore2002frankfurt
- Content: Wellmer's "rescue" strategy (decoupling method from totalizing pessimism); Honneth on "saving the sacred with a philosophy of history"; Bernstein's non-partisan stock-taking (neither generation resolved the grounding problem); the external critical-rationalist objection (Albert).

**Subsection 7.3: Eurocentrism, the universal-history residue, and contemporary reassessment**
- Papers: allen2016endofprogress; leeb2018liberating; baum2015decolonizing; bhambra2021decolonizing; olson2019decolonizing; mcarthur2021critical; biskamp2025provincializing; torres2025criticaltheory; deranty2022recognition; allen2025fromnormative
- Content: Allen's decolonial "fracturing" (progress as imperative, not accomplished fact) — positioning Adorno *against* Habermas/Honneth/Forst; the symposium critiques (Leeb/Nichols/Winter); Bhambra's more radical implication of critique's own categories + reparations; Biskamp "provincializing" Habermas (the residue relocated, not resolved); institutional uptake (Torres); the 2025 roundtable stock-taking (Allen et al.); Deranty turning the lens on Honneth's own historiography.

**Connection**: Adjudicates the meta-question at the level of reception — whether the negative philosophy of history is a normatively self-defeating dead end or a still-productive, anti-progressivist resource.
**Section Summary**: The reception is polarized between the normative-deficit charge and a contemporary anti-progressivist/decolonial recovery; both converge on the fate of "universal history" and progress.
**Word Target**: 1100 words

## Conclusion

**Purpose**: Synthesize the current state of the literature and return to the guiding meta-question.

**Content**:
- Restate the answer the literature collectively gestures toward: the Adorno-Horkheimer position is most defensibly read not as a rival substantive philosophy of history but as an immanent, negative *critique of the genre* that nonetheless cannot fully relinquish its categories (progress, universal history, totality) — retaining them "negatively" or under erasure.
- Identify the enduring tensions: (a) whether a negative totality without a subject is coherent; (b) whether the totalizing critique is self-undermining or deliberately aporetic; (c) the collapse of the Horkheimer-Adorno difference; (d) the secularization question (Benjaminian messianism retained or expelled); (e) the universal-history residue exposed by decolonial critique.
- Note the plan's flagged gaps as frontiers: *Naturgeschichte* and the Anthropocene; the negative philosophy of history and analytic moral-progress debates; the under-integration of postcolonial/feminist critique with mainstream Adorno scholarship.
- Close by observing the contemporary reversal of fortune: after decades of Habermasian eclipse, the negative, anti-progressivist philosophy of history is being reassessed as a resource (Allen, Freyenhagen, Whyman, Johnson) precisely under conditions of catastrophe, ecology, and decolonization.

**Word Target**: 500 words
