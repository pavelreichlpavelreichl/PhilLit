## Section 7: Critical Reception and the Politics of Reassessment

The critical fortunes of Kautsky's philosophy of history are themselves a philosophical problem. How a thinker is received, dismissed, and rehabilitated reflects not only the quality of his arguments but the political stakes that attach to them. In Kautsky's case, the reception history divides into two broad phases: a long period of condemnation that ran from roughly 1920 to 1980, anchored in Western Marxist and Leninist polemic; and a revisionist rehabilitation, beginning with Salvadori (1979) and intensifying since 2000, that challenges the fairness and precision of those earlier verdicts. Neither phase has produced consensus, and the unresolved tension between them is the central meta-level debate the present project must navigate.

### Subsection 7.1: The Western Marxist Indictment

The Western Marxist critique of Kautsky was less a textual engagement than a self-constituting gesture: "Kautskyanism" named the model of Marxism against which a tradition defined its own alternative. Anderson (1976) canonizes this narrative in *Considerations on Western Marxism*, where the Second International's economistic fatalism — with Kautsky as its dominant theorist — functions as the baseline departure point for the whole succession from Lukács and Korsch through the Frankfurt School and Althusser. Anderson's argument is structural: Western Marxism retreated from political economy and from the working class into philosophy and aesthetics precisely because the Kautskyan inheritance had made theory passive and contemplative, a reflection of objective processes rather than an instrument of transformation.

The most philosophically developed version of this charge appears in Kolakowski (1978). His treatment of Kautsky in volume two of *Main Currents of Marxism* argues that Kautsky's evolutionism collapses historical materialism into social Darwinism: history operates through necessary laws that function independently of human will, socialism is an inevitable natural outcome analogous to biological development, and revolutionary agency is absorbed into a fatalist waiting strategy. For Kolakowski, Kautsky's "scientism" empties Marxism of both its normative content and its dialectical complexity — what remains is a positivist natural science of society that cannot ground the political commitments it claims to support.

Yet a genealogical twist complicates any simple reception of this verdict. Baring (2023) argues that Lukács's foundational critique of "vulgar Marxism" in *History and Class Consciousness* is not an external critique of Kautsky but an internal working-out of Kautsky's own problematic. Kautsky had himself distinguished proletarian class instinct from class consciousness, assigning Social Democratic intellectuals the task of transmitting the conclusions of scientific socialism to a working class that could not independently arrive at method. Lukács's definition of "vulgar Marxism" — the forgetting of method in favor of conclusions — derives directly from this Kautskyan transmission model. The "vulgar Marxism" label thus turns out, on Baring's reconstruction, to be parasitic on the very framework it indicts. This does not exonerate Kautsky, but it does destabilize the clean opposition between sophisticated Western Marxism and mechanical Second International orthodoxy that Anderson's narrative requires.

### Subsection 7.2: The Rehabilitation and Its Qualifications

The revisionist tradition opens with Salvadori (1979), whose *Karl Kautsky and the Socialist Revolution* was the first sustained scholarly reassessment of Kautsky's trajectory across six decades. Salvadori's central claim is that Kautsky's apparent fatalism was not a theoretical confusion but a deliberate strategic choice: maintaining a maximalist socialist horizon while accepting the constraints of parliamentary reformism in Wilhelmine Germany. The concept of "passive radicalism" reframes what critics read as doctrinal rigidity as a conscious calculation about the conditions for socialist transformation. Salvadori (1989) later revisited and refined this interpretation, acknowledging greater complexity in the relationship between Kautsky's evolutionism and his political practice, but without abandoning the strategic-choice framework. A related contextualizing move appears in Salvadori's comparison of Kautsky with Weber (1989), where both thinkers are shown grappling with the same structural-historical forces — capitalism, rationalization, modernity — and both refusing pure voluntarism, even while reaching opposite political conclusions.

Steenson (1978) contributes a sympathetic intellectual biography arguing that Kautsky's synthesis of Darwinian naturalism and Marxist political economy was coherent on its own terms. The reputation for fatalism, Steenson contends, distorts a more nuanced position on necessity and political action. Shandro (1997) presses further on the theory-practice question, arguing that Kautsky maintained a more dialectical account of the relationship between socialist theory and working-class experience than the "injection from without" model attributed to him — and contested by Lenin — allows. If working-class experience plays a constitutive role in the formation of socialist consciousness, the mechanistic-determinist reading loses some of its force.

Blackledge (2006, 2006) offers what is perhaps the most textually grounded version of the rehabilitation, combining close reading of Kautsky's historical studies with a principled periodization argument. Blackledge argues in "Karl Kautsky and Marxist Historiography" that Kautsky's work on early Christianity, the Reformation, and the Russian and American social formations demonstrates a sophisticated, non-reductive historical materialism, especially before 1910. The dismissive caricature of mechanical evolutionism "obscures the very real and important contribution he made to historical materialism in the early years of the last century" (Blackledge 2006). In *Reflections on the Marxist Theory of History* (Blackledge 2006), the broader verdict is balanced: Kautsky's historiosophy shows systematic integration of evolution, class struggle, and historical agency alongside a tendency toward passive fatalism that becomes dominant in the later work. Gronow (2016) reinforces this conclusion at the level of political economy, demonstrating that Kautsky's theoretical framework was more internally coherent than the "vulgar Marxism" label implies, while tracing the precise ways in which his codification of Marx's categories diverged from Marx's own immanent-critical project.

Lewis (2017) maps the resulting "Kautsky revival," showing how Lenin's polemic and Cold War historiography had shaped the negative consensus that recent scholarship has begun to challenge. Rakhinsky et al. (2024) supply a philosophical taxonomy from Nowak that clarifies what is structurally at stake in the rehabilitation debate: Kautsky's historical materialism is the originating instance of the "nomological" interpretation — treating Marxism as a system of law-like historical regularities — which stands in systematic contrast to the Lukácsian "praxis" interpretation. Whether the nomological reading is a contribution or a deformation of Marx is, on this taxonomy, precisely the question that divides the dismissal and rehabilitation traditions.

The reception history thus leaves three questions unresolved. First, the periodization question: is the distinction between a sophisticated pre-1910 Kautsky and an ossified later one defensible, or does it serve mainly to rescue a preferred theoretical core? Second, the question of political inflection: the rehabilitation tradition is concentrated in the post-2000 Historical Materialism milieu, raising the question of whether revisionism reflects a reconsideration of the evidence or a changed political context — specifically, a renewed interest in electoral socialist strategy — that makes Kautsky's "passive radicalism" look prescient rather than deficient. Third, Baring's genealogical argument introduces a further complication: if the "vulgar Marxism" charge was Kautskyian in origin, what does it mean to rehabilitate Kautsky against it? Neither pure dismissal nor uncritical rehabilitation provides stable ground, and this is the horizon within which the present project must position its own reading of Kautsky's philosophy of history.
