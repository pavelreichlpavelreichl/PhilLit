# Literature Review Plan: G.A. Cohen's *Karl Marx's Theory of History: A Defence* (1978)

## Research Idea Summary

A comprehensive literature review on G.A. Cohen's *Karl Marx's Theory of History: A Defence* (Princeton University Press, 1978; expanded ed. 2000/2001), the foundational text of Analytical Marxism. The review covers the book's core theses (primacy thesis/technological determinism, functional explanation, development thesis, base/superstructure via rational-material relations, material vs. social properties of production), its critical reception from 1978 onward, and the major debates it provoked across analytical, Hegelian/critical-theory, historicist, and feminist traditions.

## Key Research Questions

1. What are Cohen's core theses and how does he argue for them (rational reconstruction, functional explanation, primacy of productive forces)?
2. How did contemporaries (Elster, Wood, Miller, Shaw, and others) receive and criticize the book upon publication?
3. What is the "functional explanation" debate, and how did Cohen defend consequence-explanation without a specified mechanism against charges of holism/teleology?
4. How did Analytical Marxism / the September Group develop, diverge, and internally debate historical materialism after 1978?
5. How did Cohen himself revise, qualify, or abandon aspects of the primacy thesis in later work, and how did this feed his turn to normative political philosophy?
6. What are the major critiques of technological/productive-force determinism from within (and adjacent to) Marxism, including the primacy of relations of production and class struggle?
7. How have Hegelian/critical-theory Marxists, historicist Marxist historians, and feminist theorists challenged Cohen's framework and historical materialism more broadly?
8. What has been the retrospective/legacy assessment of the book, especially around its 2000/2001 expanded edition and subsequent anniversaries?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: Textual Foundations — Cohen's Core Theses and Method

**Focus**: Close exposition and analysis of the book's central arguments: the primacy thesis, the development thesis, functional explanation as the logical form of historical materialism, the base/superstructure distinction via "rechtsfrei" rational-material relations of production, and the distinction between material and social properties of productive forces/relations. Includes Cohen's methodological commitments (analytical philosophy, rational reconstruction, "no-bullshit" clarity) and how these theses interlock.

**Key Questions**:
- What exactly is the primacy thesis and how does it differ from vulgar technological determinism?
- How does Cohen define and defend the development thesis (productive forces tend to develop)?
- How does the distinction between material and social (or "purely economic") properties of production do philosophical work in the argument?
- What is Cohen's account of the relation between forces and relations of production ("fettering")?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP entries on "Historical Materialism," "Karl Marx," "Analytical Marxism," "G.A. Cohen" (if exists) via `search_sep.py`; PhilPapers category on historical materialism via `search_philpapers.py`
- Key terms: "primacy thesis Cohen", "development thesis historical materialism", "technological determinism Marx", "material properties productive forces", "base superstructure rational reconstruction", "Karl Marx's Theory of History Defence"
- Expected papers: 10-12 (primarily secondary expository/analytical treatments, plus Cohen's own subsequent restatements)

**Relevance to Project**: Establishes the doctrinal baseline against which all critical literature (Domains 2-7) must be read; ensures accurate characterization of theses before surveying debates.

### Domain 2: Contemporary Reception — 1978 Reviews and Early Critical Responses

**Focus**: The wave of reviews and critical articles published in the years immediately following the 1978 publication (roughly 1978-1985), including philosophical, historical, and political-theory venues. Central figures include Jon Elster, Allen Wood, Richard Miller, William H. Shaw, Alan Carling, and others who reviewed or critically engaged the book on first appearance.

**Key Questions**:
- How did reviewers initially assess the book's rigor, novelty, and fidelity to Marx's texts?
- What were the earliest objections to functional explanation, and to the primacy thesis?
- Did reviewers dispute Cohen's textual/exegetical claims about Marx (e.g., 1859 Preface reading)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: `s2_search.py`, `search_openalex.py`, `search_core.py` for book reviews in journals (e.g., *Journal of Philosophy*, *Political Theory*, *History and Theory*, *Philosophy and Public Affairs*, *Ethics*); `crossref` verification via citation-checking script
- Key terms: "Cohen Karl Marx's Theory of History review", "Elster review Cohen 1978", "Allen Wood historical materialism review", "Richard Miller Cohen Marx", "William Shaw Cohen technological determinism"
- Expected papers: 8-12 (contemporary reviews and immediate response articles, 1978-1985)

**Relevance to Project**: Documents the book's initial impact and establishes the earliest terms of debate that later, more developed critiques (functional explanation, determinism) built upon.

---

### Domain 3: The Functional Explanation Debate

**Focus**: The sustained methodological controversy over Cohen's use of functional explanation to underwrite historical materialism without specifying a causal mechanism, and his defense against charges of holism, teleology, and unfalsifiability. Centers on the exchange between Cohen, Jon Elster, and John Roemer, plus the broader philosophy-of-social-science literature on functionalism vs. methodological individualism/rational choice explanation.

**Key Questions**:
- What is Cohen's account of "consequence explanation" and how does it differ from mechanism-requiring causal explanation?
- What are Elster's and Roemer's objections (teleology, lack of microfoundations, "black-box" functionalism)?
- How did Cohen respond (e.g., in later essays and the expanded edition's new material)?
- How does this debate connect to broader philosophy-of-science debates on functional explanation (biology/social science analogies)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP "Functionalism," "Methodological Individualism," "Analytical Marxism" via `search_sep.py`; PhilPapers functional explanation category
- Key terms: "functional explanation Cohen Elster", "consequence explanation Marxism", "microfoundations functional explanation", "Roemer functionalism Marxism", "teleology historical materialism", "holism vs methodological individualism Marxism"
- Expected papers: 10-14 (core exchange papers plus philosophy-of-social-science commentary)

**Relevance to Project**: This is one of the most cited and sustained debates provoked by the book; central to the review's "special emphasis" on critical exchanges.

---

### Domain 4: Analytical Marxism and the September Group — Internal Debates

**Focus**: The formation and development of Analytical Marxism (the "September Group"/Non-Bullshit Marxism Group), including Cohen, Elster, Roemer, Erik Olin Wright, Adam Przeworski, and Philippe van Parijs. Covers internal disagreements about whether historical materialism can/should be defended via functionalism vs. rational choice/game-theoretic microfoundations, and the movement's trajectory (including its eventual fragmentation and members' divergent paths).

**Key Questions**:
- How did Analytical Marxism as a school position itself relative to Cohen's founding text?
- What internal disputes arose between functionalist and rational-choice wings (Cohen vs. Elster/Roemer)?
- How did class-analysis theorists (Wright) and political economists (Przeworski, van Parijs) extend or diverge from Cohen's framework?
- What is the movement's assessed legacy in political philosophy and social theory?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP "Analytical Marxism"; PhilPapers Analytical Marxism category; `search_openalex.py` for edited volumes (e.g., Roemer's *Analytical Marxism* anthology)
- Key terms: "Analytical Marxism September Group", "rational choice Marxism", "Erik Olin Wright class analytical Marxism", "Roemer Analytical Marxism", "Przeworski van Parijs Marxism", "no-bullshit Marxism"
- Expected papers: 8-12

**Relevance to Project**: Situates Cohen's book as the founding document of a broader intellectual movement; needed to assess the book's influence beyond direct exegetical debate.

---

### Domain 5: Cohen's Self-Revisions and Later Trajectory

**Focus**: Cohen's own retreats, qualifications, and self-criticisms of the primacy thesis and functional explanation in the decade-plus after 1978, culminating in his turn toward normative political philosophy (equality, justice, Rawls-critique). Key texts: "Reconsidering Historical Materialism" (1983), "Restricted and Inclusive Historical Materialism" (1988), *History, Labour, and Freedom: Themes from Marx* (1988), and reflective essays/interviews on why he moved away from historical materialism.

**Key Questions**:
- What specific commitments did Cohen retract or weaken (e.g., strong primacy thesis, transhistorical rationality assumption)?
- What is the distinction between "restricted" and "inclusive" historical materialism, and why did Cohen prefer the restricted version?
- How does Cohen narrate his own intellectual trajectory from historical materialism to egalitarian political philosophy?
- How did the 2000/2001 expanded edition's new introduction/afterword address accumulated criticism?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: `search_philpapers.py` for Cohen's collected essays; `search_sep.py` "G.A. Cohen" entry (if exists) for biographical/intellectual trajectory
- Key terms: "Reconsidering Historical Materialism Cohen", "Restricted Inclusive Historical Materialism", "History Labour and Freedom Cohen", "Cohen expanded edition 2000 afterword Marx", "Cohen turn to political philosophy Marxism"
- Expected papers: 6-10

**Relevance to Project**: Essential for accurately representing that Cohen himself substantially revised his positions; prevents the review from treating the 1978 theses as static/unchallenged even by their author.

---

### Domain 6: Critiques of Technological/Productive-Force Determinism

**Focus**: Sustained critiques, from within and adjacent to Marxism, of the primacy thesis as technological determinism, especially arguments for the explanatory priority of relations of production and class struggle over productive forces. Central figures: Andrew Levine and Erik Olin Wright (joint critique of the primacy thesis), William H. Shaw (defender-critic), and other Marxist theorists arguing class struggle, not technology, is the "motor" of history.

**Key Questions**:
- What is the Levine-Wright argument that relations of production, not forces, should have explanatory primacy?
- How does Shaw's own work (*Marx's Theory of History*, 1978) compare and contrast with Cohen's?
- What role does class struggle play as an alternative or supplementary motor of historical change?
- How do these critiques bear on empirical/historical plausibility of the development thesis?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: `s2_search.py`, `search_openalex.py` for journal debates (e.g., *Theory and Society*, *Political Studies*, *Philosophy and Public Affairs*)
- Key terms: "Levine Wright rationality class struggle primacy", "primacy of production relations Marxism", "technological determinism critique Marx", "class struggle motor of history", "Shaw Marx's Theory of History"
- Expected papers: 8-10

**Relevance to Project**: Directly addresses one of the most philosophically significant lines of internal Marxist criticism explicitly requested for special emphasis.

---

### Domain 7: Hegelian/Historicist and Feminist Critiques; Reception Beyond Analytical Marxism

**Focus**: Critiques of Cohen's analytical/functionalist approach to historical materialism from Hegelian and critical-theory Marxists and historicist/agency-centered Marxist historians (e.g., E.P. Thompson and the broader "history from below"/agency tradition, checking directly for engagement with Cohen where it exists), and feminist critiques of historical materialism responding to or bearing on Cohen's framework (e.g., critiques of the gender-blindness of the forces/relations schema, domestic labor debates, social reproduction theory).

**Key Questions**:
- Does E.P. Thompson's "The Poverty of Theory" (or related historicist critiques) engage Cohen directly, or only Althusserian structuralism more broadly? What is the accurate scope of overlap?
- What do agency-centered/historicist Marxist historians object to in Cohen's rational-reconstruction, ahistorical method?
- How have feminist theorists (e.g., critiques of historical materialism's treatment of reproduction, domestic labor, and gender relations as outside "production") challenged or sought to extend Cohen's categories?
- Is there a distinct "Hegelian Marxism vs. Analytical Marxism" methodological debate in the secondary literature?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: `search_sep.py` "E.P. Thompson," "Feminist Political Philosophy," "Historical Materialism"; `s2_search.py`/`search_openalex.py` for feminist historical-materialism literature
- Key terms: "E.P. Thompson Poverty of Theory Cohen", "historicist critique analytical Marxism", "feminist critique historical materialism", "domestic labor social reproduction historical materialism", "gender and forces of production Marxism", "agency structure Marxist historiography Cohen"
- Expected papers: 8-12 (note: verify directness of Thompson-Cohen engagement empirically; include closely related historicist critiques of the general analytical-Marxist method if direct engagement is limited)

**Relevance to Project**: Ensures the review is not confined to intra-analytical-philosophy debate; captures explicitly requested critical perspectives from outside the analytical tradition, and guards against overrepresentation of sympathetic (analytical) voices.

## Coverage Rationale

The seven domains move from (1) accurate textual exposition, through (2) immediate reception, (3-4) the two most consequential internal debates (functional explanation; the Analytical Marxism movement), (5) the author's own retreat and revision, to (6-7) the two major families of external critique (determinism/class-struggle critiques from within Marxism; Hegelian/historicist/feminist critiques from outside analytical Marxism). This structure ensures: every named debate in the research idea is assigned an explicit home; contemporary (1978-85), mid-career (1980s-90s revisions), and retrospective (2000s+ legacy, folded into Domains 5 and 7) timeframes are all covered; and no domain is purely expository — each is oriented around live disagreement, satisfying the priority on rigorous, comprehensive, and critically balanced coverage rather than a summary of Cohen's views alone.

## Expected Gaps

- Direct engagement between Cohen and E.P. Thompson-style historicist Marxism may be sparser than assumed; Domain 7 researchers should verify and, if direct engagement is thin, substitute closely related historicist/agency-based critiques of analytical Marxism's method more broadly, flagging the substitution explicitly.
- Feminist engagement specifically with Cohen's 1978 text (as opposed to historical materialism generally) may be limited; researchers should distinguish direct critiques of Cohen from critiques of historical materialism that are readily connectable to his specific framework.
- Recent (post-2015) scholarship may be thin, since the book's most active debate period was the 1980s-1990s; Domain 5 and 7 researchers should specifically search for 2000s-2020s retrospective/anniversary pieces (e.g., around the book's 25th/40th anniversary or Cohen's 2009 death) to avoid recency gaps.
- Possible underrepresentation of non-English-language secondary literature (e.g., German/French receptions of Analytical Marxism); flag as a scope limitation rather than attempt exhaustive multilingual search.

## Estimated Scope

- **Total domains**: 7
- **Estimated papers**: 58-82 total across all domains
- **Key positions**: (1) Cohen's original functionalist/technological-primacy defense; (2) Elster/Roemer's rational-choice, microfoundations-demanding critique of functional explanation; (3) Levine-Wright's relations-of-production/class-struggle primacy alternative; (4) Cohen's own later "restricted" historical materialism and eventual abandonment of primacy in favor of normative theory; (5) Hegelian/historicist and feminist critiques rejecting the analytical-functionalist method itself

## Search Priorities

1. **Foundational works**: Cohen (1978/2000) itself (for context, not as a "found" source but as the anchor text), Elster's early critical reviews and *Making Sense of Marx* (1985), Roemer's *Analytical Foundations of Marxian Economic Theory* and edited *Analytical Marxism* (1986), Wright/Levine/Sober's *Reconstructing Marxism* (1992), Shaw's *Marx's Theory of History* (1978)
2. **Recent developments**: Post-2000 retrospectives, the 2000/2001 expanded edition's reception, any 2009-onward obituary/legacy assessments of Cohen, recent (2015-2025) philosophy-of-history treatments of functional explanation and technological determinism
3. **Critical responses**: All domains, but especially 3, 6, and 7 — prioritize papers that explicitly argue against Cohen's theses over purely expository summaries
