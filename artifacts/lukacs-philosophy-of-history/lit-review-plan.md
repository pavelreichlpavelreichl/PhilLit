# Literature Review Plan: Georg Lukács's Philosophy of History

## Research Idea Summary

This review surveys Georg Lukács's philosophy of history across two axes: (a) his critique of the historiographical/philosophical tradition (Hegelian idealism, vulgar and Second-International Marxism, neo-Kantian historicism, and the "contemplative"/reified stance of bourgeois thought), and (b) his constructive theory centered on totality, the standpoint of the proletariat, class consciousness, praxis, and the identical subject-object of history. It further maps the full arc of Lukács's intellectual development and the major secondary debates his work generated across Western Marxism, Critical Theory, structuralist and analytic Marxism, and late-Lukács ontology scholarship.

## Key Research Questions

1. What is the structure and philosophical grounding of Lukács's critique of the "contemplative" bourgeois and mechanistic-Marxist traditions, and how does the concept of reification unify it?
2. What is Lukács's positive theory of history (totality, class consciousness, praxis, subject-object identity, teleology), and how coherent/defensible is it?
3. How should Lukács's oeuvre be periodized — is there rupture or continuity between the pre-Marxist works, *History and Class Consciousness* (HCC), the 1967 self-criticism, and the late *Ontology of Social Being*?
4. How did the major critical traditions (Althusser/structuralism, analytic Marxism, Frankfurt School, later Western Marxism) receive, appropriate, and contest his philosophy of history?
5. How do Lukács's Hegelian and Marxian sources (Hegel's philosophy of history; Marx's 1844 Manuscripts and *Capital*) shape and constrain his position?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: Intellectual Development and the Periodization Debate

**Focus**: Lukács's biographical-intellectual trajectory and the meta-debate over how to periodize it: the pre-Marxist romantic-idealist works (*Soul and Form*, *The Theory of the Novel*, the Heidelberg aesthetics), the conversion to Bolshevism, HCC (1923), the 1967 self-critical preface, and the "young vs. mature/late Lukács" question as a framing device for the whole literature.

**Key Questions**:
- What are the decisive stages and turning points in Lukács's development, and what continuities (messianism, totality, anti-positivism) persist across them?
- How should the 1967 preface's self-criticism be read — as repudiation, tactical accommodation, or genuine philosophical revision?
- Is the "epistemological break" model (rupture) or a continuity/developmental model more defensible?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: SEP "Georg [György] Lukács" (Titus Stahl); IEP entry on Lukács; `search_sep.py` and `search_iep.py` for orientation and bibliography mining.
- Key secondary: Michael Löwy, *Georg Lukács: From Romanticism to Bolshevism*; Andrew Arato & Paul Breines, *The Young Lukács and the Origins of Western Marxism*; Mary Gluck, *Georg Lukács and His Generation*; Lee Congdon, *The Young Lukács*; Kadarkay biography.
- Key terms: `"Lukács intellectual development"`, `"young Lukács"`, `"Lukács 1967 preface"`, `"Lukács self-criticism"`, `"Theory of the Novel Lukács"`, `"Lukács romanticism Bolshevism"`.
- Journals: *New German Critique*, *Telos*, *Thesis Eleven*.

**Relevance to Project**: Supplies the biographical/periodization scaffolding the review requires and frames the continuity-vs-rupture debate that recurs in Domains 5-7.

### Domain 2: Reification and the Critique of Bourgeois and Traditional Thought

**Focus**: The critical/negative half of Lukács's philosophy of history — the "Reification and the Consciousness of the Proletariat" essay and its targets: Hegelian idealism, neo-Kantian historicism (Rickert, Windelband, Simmel, Weber), Second-International "scientistic"/evolutionary Marxism (Kautsky, Bernstein), vulgar/mechanistic materialism, and the "antinomies of bourgeois thought" (freedom/necessity, subject/object, form/content) arising from the contemplative stance.

**Key Questions**:
- How does reification (extended from Marx's commodity fetishism) function as a diagnostic that unifies the critique of philosophy, science, and social form?
- How do the "antinomies of bourgeois thought" reconstruct classical German philosophy as a symptom of capitalist social structure?
- What is the specific charge against Second-International determinism and neo-Kantian dualism?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: HCC "Reification" essay; SEP entries on "Georg Lukács", "Western Marxism" (if available), "Max Weber" and neo-Kantianism for context via `search_sep.py`.
- Key secondary: Andrew Feenberg, *The Philosophy of Praxis*; Konstantinos Kavoulakos, *Georg Lukács's Philosophy of Praxis: From Neo-Kantian Ethics to Marxist Ontology*; Richard Westerman, *Lukács's Phenomenology of Capitalism*; Timothy Bewes, *Reification, or The Anxiety of Late Capitalism*; Gillian Rose, *Hegel Contra Sociology*.
- Key terms: `"Lukács reification"`, `"antinomies of bourgeois thought"`, `"Lukács neo-Kantianism"`, `"contemplative stance Lukács"`, `"Second International Marxism critique"`, `"commodity fetishism reification"`, `"Lukács Weber rationalization"`.
- Journals: *Historical Materialism*, *Radical Philosophy*, *Continental Thought & Theory*.

**Relevance to Project**: Covers requirement (a) — the critique of the tradition — and establishes reification as the pivot connecting critique to construction.

### Domain 3: The Constructive Theory of History — Totality, Class Consciousness, Praxis, Subject-Object

**Focus**: The positive/constructive half — Lukács's category of totality, the "standpoint of the proletariat," imputed (*zugerechnetes*) class consciousness, the primacy of praxis, the proletariat as "identical subject-object of history," and the associated questions of historical teleology and the resolution of the antinomies. Includes the internal *Tailism* debate (Lukács's own 1925-26 defense).

**Key Questions**:
- How does "totality" function methodologically as "the bearer of the principle of revolution in science"?
- What is the logical structure of the identical subject-object claim, and what work does it do in overcoming reification?
- What is "imputed" class consciousness and how does it relate to empirical proletarian consciousness?
- Does Lukács's account entail teleology, and of what kind?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: HCC essays ("Class Consciousness," "Reification," "Towards a Methodology of the Problem of Organisation"); *A Defence of History and Class Consciousness: Tailism and the Dialectic* (with John Rees intro and Žižek postface).
- Key secondary: Martin Jay, *Marxism and Totality*; Feenberg, *Lukács, Marx and the Sources of Critical Theory*; Tom Rockmore, *Irrationalism* and edited *Lukács Today*; Kavoulakos; essays in *Georg Lukács Reconsidered* (ed. Thompson).
- Key terms: `"Lukács totality"`, `"identical subject-object"`, `"imputed class consciousness"`, `"standpoint of the proletariat"`, `"Lukács praxis"`, `"Lukács Tailism dialectic"`, `"Lukács historical teleology"`.
- Journals: *Historical Materialism*, *Thesis Eleven*, *Rethinking Marxism*.

**Relevance to Project**: Covers requirement (b) — the core constructive doctrines and the internal tensions (teleology, subject-object) that later critics target.

### Domain 4: Hegelian and Marxian Sources and Methodology

**Focus**: Lukács's philosophical genealogy — his appropriation of Hegel's philosophy of history and dialectic, and his reading of Marx's method. Includes the interpretive puzzle that HCC anticipated themes of Marx's *1844 Manuscripts* before their 1932 publication, the relation to *Capital*'s method (abstraction, fetishism), and Lukács's later *The Young Hegel*.

**Key Questions**:
- Which elements of Hegel's *Philosophy of History* and *Phenomenology* does Lukács mobilize, and where does he claim to "materialize" them?
- How does Lukács read Marx's dialectical method, and is his reading a legitimate reconstruction or a Hegelianization?
- What is the significance of the convergence/divergence with the *1844 Manuscripts* (alienation vs. reification)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: HCC "What is Orthodox Marxism?"; Lukács, *The Young Hegel*; SEP entries on "Hegel's Philosophy of History", "Karl Marx", "Alienation" via `search_sep.py`.
- Key secondary: Tom Rockmore, *Marxism after Marx* and Hegel-Marx studies; Gillian Rose, *Hegel Contra Sociology*; Chris Arthur on Hegel-Marx; Roberto Finelli; Feenberg on Marx's sources.
- Key terms: `"Lukács Hegel dialectic"`, `"Lukács Marx method"`, `"1844 Manuscripts alienation reification"`, `"Lukács Young Hegel"`, `"Hegel philosophy of history Marxism"`, `"dialectics of nature Lukács Engels"`.
- Journals: *Hegel Bulletin*, *Historical Materialism*, *Science & Society*.

**Relevance to Project**: Grounds both the critique and the construction in their sources; addresses the explicit requirement on Hegel's philosophy of history and Marx's methodology.

### Domain 5: The Late Ontology of Social Being and the Continuity Question

**Focus**: Lukács's late *Zur Ontologie des gesellschaftlichen Seins* (and the *Prolegomena*), its categories (labor as ontological model, teleological positing vs. causality, social being, the critique of his own earlier "messianic utopianism"), and the scholarly debate over its relation to the philosophy of history in HCC — rupture, correction, or deepening.

**Key Questions**:
- What is the ontological reframing of history in terms of labor, teleological positing, and the causal nexus?
- Does the *Ontology* abandon, revise, or fulfill the subject-object problematic of HCC?
- How does the late Lukács handle teleology and historicism differently from the young Lukács?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: *The Ontology of Social Being* (Labour; Marx; Hegel volumes); *Prolegomena to the Ontology of Social Being*.
- Key secondary: Nicolas Tertulian's essays on the Ontology; Feenberg, *The Philosophy of Praxis* (late chapters); Kavoulakos ("neo-Kantian ethics to Marxist ontology"); Guido Oldrini; essays in *Studies in East European Thought* special issues; Michael Thompson (ed.) chapters on the late work.
- Key terms: `"Lukács Ontology of Social Being"`, `"Lukács teleological positing labour"`, `"Lukács late ontology"`, `"Tertulian Lukács ontology"`, `"Lukács social being category"`, `"Prolegomena ontology Lukács"`.
- Journals: *Studies in East European Thought*, *Historical Materialism*, *Monthly Review*.

**Relevance to Project**: Covers the explicit requirement on the late ontology and its relation to the earlier philosophy of history; empirically tests the continuity/rupture thesis from Domain 1.

### Domain 6: Structuralist and Analytic Critiques

**Focus**: The rationalist/anti-humanist and analytic lines of critique. Althusser and structuralist Marxism's charge of "expressive totality," historicism, and theoretical humanism; Merleau-Ponty's critique in *Adventures of the Dialectic*; and analytic-Marxist objections to the identical subject-object and to teleological/holist historical explanation (methodological individualism, functional explanation).

**Key Questions**:
- What precisely is Althusser's objection to Lukács's "expressive totality" and historicism, and is it a fair reading?
- How does Merleau-Ponty diagnose the impasse of the "Western Marxism" Lukács inaugurates?
- How do analytic Marxists (Elster, Cohen-adjacent critiques) challenge the subject-object identity and holistic teleology?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: Althusser, *Reading Capital* and *For Marx* (historicism/humanism sections); Merleau-Ponty, *Adventures of the Dialectic* ("Western Marxism"); Jon Elster, *Making Sense of Marx*.
- Key secondary: Perry Anderson, *Considerations on Western Marxism* and *Arguments within English Marxism*; Gregory Elliott on Althusser; Alex Callinicos on Althusser/Lukács; secondary work on Merleau-Ponty and Marxism.
- Key terms: `"Althusser expressive totality"`, `"Althusser historicism Lukács"`, `"anti-humanism Marxism"`, `"Merleau-Ponty Western Marxism Lukács"`, `"analytic Marxism subject-object"`, `"methodological individualism Marx teleology"`.
- Journals: *New Left Review*, *Radical Philosophy*, *Historical Materialism*.

**Relevance to Project**: Delivers the critical/objection-heavy coverage (structuralist anti-humanism, analytic critiques, Merleau-Ponty) mandated by the proposal — the primary counterweight to Domains 2-3.

---

### Domain 7: Frankfurt School, Western Marxism, and Later Reception

**Focus**: The appropriative/critical reception in Critical Theory and beyond. The Frankfurt School engagement (Adorno's negative-dialectical critique of the subject-object identity; Horkheimer; Benjamin) and the "afterlife" of reification (up to Honneth's *Reification*); the placement of Lukács within Western Marxism alongside Korsch and Gramsci; and later reception — Goldmann, Jameson, Feenberg, and Postone's critique of "traditional Marxism"/labor.

**Key Questions**:
- How do Adorno and the Frankfurt School inherit reification while rejecting the proletarian subject-object and the affirmative totality?
- What are the affinities and differences among the founding Western Marxists (Lukács, Korsch, Gramsci)?
- How do Goldmann, Jameson, Feenberg, and especially Postone reconstruct or contest Lukács's philosophy of history?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: Adorno, *Negative Dialectics* and *History and Freedom* lectures; Korsch, *Marxism and Philosophy*; Gramsci, *Prison Notebooks* (selections); Honneth, *Reification: A New Look at an Old Idea*; Postone, *Time, Labor, and Social Domination*; Jameson, *Marxism and Form* / *The Antinomies of Realism*.
- Key secondary: Lucien Goldmann, *Lukács and Heidegger*; Feenberg (critical-theory lineage); Martin Jay, *The Dialectical Imagination* and *Marxism and Totality*; Andrew Arato; Timothy Hall and other essays in *Georg Lukács Reconsidered*.
- Key terms: `"Lukács reification Frankfurt School"`, `"Adorno subject-object Lukács"`, `"Honneth reification"`, `"Korsch Gramsci Western Marxism"`, `"Postone Lukács traditional Marxism"`, `"Jameson Lukács totality"`, `"Goldmann Lukács"`, `"Feenberg philosophy of praxis"`.
- Journals: *New German Critique*, *Telos*, *Constellations*, *Historical Materialism*, *Thesis Eleven*.

**Relevance to Project**: Covers the Frankfurt School, Western Marxism (Korsch/Gramsci), and the full later-reception roster (Goldmann, Jameson, Feenberg, Postone) — mixing appropriation and critique.

## Coverage Rationale

The seven domains partition the material by function rather than by author, minimizing overlap. Domains 2-3 cover Lukács's own critique and construction (requirements a and b); Domain 1 supplies development/periodization; Domain 4 supplies philosophical sources; Domain 5 covers the late ontology; Domains 6-7 partition the secondary critical literature into (6) rationalist/analytic opposition and (7) Critical-Theory/Western-Marxist appropriation-and-critique. Every named debate and figure in the proposal maps to exactly one primary domain, with cross-references handling boundary cases (e.g., the continuity question is framed in Domain 1 and tested in Domain 5; teleology is stated in Domain 3 and contested in Domains 6-7).

## Expected Gaps

- Anglophone secondary literature may underweight the Hungarian- and German-language reception (Budapest School: Heller, Fehér, Márkus; Italian reception via Tertulian/Oldrini) — flag for targeted searching.
- The *Ontology* is comparatively under-studied in English; expect thinner returns in Domain 5 and rely on *Studies in East European Thought*.
- Potential synthesis gap the project could fill: a unified assessment of whether the teleology/subject-object problem is dissolved or merely displaced across the young-to-late trajectory.

## Estimated Scope

- **Total domains**: 7

- **Key positions**: (1) Lukács's Hegelian-Marxist philosophy of praxis; (2) structuralist anti-humanist rejection (Althusser); (3) Frankfurt-School negative-dialectical inheritance-with-rejection (Adorno/Honneth); (4) analytic-Marxist and Merleau-Pontian critique of subject-object teleology; (5) value-theoretic reconstruction (Postone) and continuity-oriented late-ontology readings.
