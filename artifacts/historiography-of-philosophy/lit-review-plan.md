# Literature Review Plan: Method and Meaning in the Historiography of Philosophy

## Research Idea Summary

This review examines the methodological and meta-philosophical debates governing how the history of philosophy ought to be practiced. Its center of gravity is the dispute between **appropriationists** (rational reconstructivists), who read past texts for their philosophical merits and arguments usable today, and **contextualists**, who insist on recovering historical meaning within the author's own intellectual setting. The anchor primary source is *The Methodology of the History of Philosophy* (Smith, Schliesser, and Laerke, eds.), and the review extends to adjacent sub-debates about interpretive charity, anachronism, the canon, and the very aims of doing history of philosophy.

## Key Research Questions

1. What are the core commitments, arguments, and figures of appropriationism vs. contextualism, and is the dichotomy itself defensible?
2. How do related distinctions (history of philosophy vs. history of ideas; synchronic vs. diachronic; analytic vs. continental) map onto or cut across this central debate?
3. Why do the history of philosophy at all — what functions (philosophical, self-understanding, scholarly, dialectical, therapeutic) does it serve, and which methodology each function recommends?
4. How do practical-methodological problems (anachronism, charity, primary vs. secondary sources, translation, canon formation) shape and complicate these positions?

## Literature Review Domains

### Domain 1: The Central Debate — Appropriationism vs. Contextualism

**Focus**: The foundational dispute between rational reconstruction / appropriation and historical contextualism, including attempts to transcend or complicate the binary (e.g., "contextualist appropriation," collegial reading).

**Key Questions**:
- What distinguishes rational reconstruction (Bennett, Rorty) from historical/contextual reconstruction?
- What are Skinner's contextualist arguments (against the "mythology of doctrines/coherence/prolepsis") and how do they transfer from intellectual history to philosophy?
- Is the dichotomy a false one? What hybrid or third-way positions exist (Garber, Laerke, Schliesser, Della Rocca)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: *The Methodology of the History of Philosophy* (Smith, Schliesser, Laerke, eds., OUP); SEP entry on "Philosophy of History" and relevant historiography sections; PhilPapers category "Historiography of Philosophy."
- Key terms: "rational reconstruction history of philosophy", "contextualism history of philosophy", "appropriationism", "Quentin Skinner meaning and understanding", "Jonathan Bennett history of philosophy", "Garber contextualism"
- Expected papers: 12-15 key papers/chapters

**Relevance to Project**: This is the spine of the review; all other domains orient around it.

---

### Domain 2: Adjacent Distinctions and Sub-Debates

**Focus**: Conceptual distinctions that structure or cross-cut the central debate — history of philosophy vs. history of ideas; synchronic vs. diachronic reading; analytic vs. continental approaches; collegiality and the "philosopher's" vs. "historian's" stance.

**Key Questions**:
- How does the history of philosophy differ from the history of ideas (Lovejoy) and intellectual history (Cambridge School)?
- What is at stake in synchronic vs. diachronic reconstruction?
- How do analytic and continental traditions theorize their own historiography differently?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: Lovejoy *The Great Chain of Being* (methodological preface); SEP "Hermeneutics"; Cambridge School literature (Skinner, Pocock, Dunn); PhilPapers "History of Philosophy, Misc."
- Key terms: "history of ideas Lovejoy", "intellectual history Cambridge School", "synchronic diachronic history of philosophy", "analytic continental history of philosophy", "Gadamer effective history philosophy"
- Expected papers: 10-12 key papers

**Relevance to Project**: Maps the conceptual terrain surrounding the appropriationism/contextualism axis and prevents treating the central debate in isolation.

---

### Domain 3: The Aims and Function of the History of Philosophy

**Focus**: Meta-philosophical justifications for doing history of philosophy at all — as philosophical method, self-understanding, scholarship for its own sake, dialectical resource, or therapy.

**Key Questions**:
- Why should philosophers engage the past? Is history constitutive of philosophy or a dispensable adjunct?
- What functions does historiography serve (problem-solving, defamiliarization, critique of the present, therapeutic dissolution)?
- How do positions on aims dictate positions on method?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: Rorty, Schneewind, Skinner *Philosophy in History*; relevant chapters in the Smith/Schliesser/Laerke volume; SEP "Pragmatism" and metaphilosophy entries; NDPR reviews of the anchor volume.
- Key terms: "why study history of philosophy", "philosophy and its past", "history of philosophy as method", "therapeutic history of philosophy", "philosophy in history Rorty Schneewind Skinner"
- Expected papers: 10-12 key papers

**Relevance to Project**: Addresses research question 3 directly; links the "point" of the enterprise to methodological choices.

---

### Domain 4: Methodological Practice — Anachronism, Charity, Canon, Sources, Translation

**Focus**: Concrete interpretive problems and craft issues: the principle of charity in interpretation, the problem of anachronism, primary vs. secondary sources, translation/commensurability, and canon formation and revision.

**Key Questions**:
- What role should charity (Davidson, Grandy) play in interpreting historical figures, and when does it become distortive?
- How is anachronism diagnosed and avoided, and is some anachronism unavoidable or even productive?
- How are philosophical canons formed, justified, and contested (including non-Western and excluded figures)?

**Search Strategy**:
- Primary sources: Davidson/Grandy on charity; relevant chapters of the anchor volume on sources and translation; SEP "Charity, Principle of" coverage within interpretation entries; literature on the philosophy canon and its expansion.
- Key terms: "principle of charity interpretation history", "anachronism history of philosophy", "philosophical canon formation", "primary secondary sources history of philosophy", "translation history of philosophy method"
- Expected papers: 10-12 key papers

**Relevance to Project**: Covers research question 4; grounds the abstract debate in the practical decisions historians of philosophy actually make.

---

## Coverage Rationale

The four domains move from the core theoretical dichotomy (Domain 1), outward to neighboring conceptual distinctions (Domain 2), up to meta-philosophical justification (Domain 3), and down to concrete interpretive practice (Domain 4). Together they cover theoretical foundations, methodological approaches, and critical perspectives, ensuring both the "method" and "meaning" halves of the topic are addressed without excessive overlap.

## Expected Gaps

- Non-Western and global historiography of philosophy is comparatively under-theorized in the canonical (largely Anglo-European) literature; the review can flag this and the canon debate as a live frontier.
- The volume and surrounding literature may under-represent feminist and decolonial critiques of canon and method — worth actively searching for as a critical counterweight.
- Empirical/sociological accounts of how historiographical norms actually function in the discipline are sparse.

## Estimated Scope

- **Total domains**: 4
- **Estimated papers**: 42-50 total
- **Key positions**: (1) Rational reconstruction / appropriationism (Bennett, Rorty); (2) Historical contextualism (Skinner, Garber, Ayers); (3) Third-way / hybrid views (Laerke, Schliesser, Smith, Della Rocca); (4) Pragmatist/therapeutic and dialectical justifications for engaging the past.

## Search Priorities

1. **Foundational works**: The anchor volume (Smith/Schliesser/Laerke); Rorty/Schneewind/Skinner *Philosophy in History*; Skinner "Meaning and Understanding"; Bennett's reconstructive methodology.
2. **Recent developments**: Post-2010 work refining or rejecting the binary; canon-expansion and decolonial methodology; use `s2_search.py` / `search_openalex.py` with recent filters.
3. **Critical responses**: Objections to contextualism's anti-philosophical tendency; objections to reconstruction's anachronism; critiques of the charity principle; canon critiques.
