# Literature Review Outline

**Research Project**: Method and Meaning in the Historiography of Philosophy — the Appropriationism/Rational Reconstruction vs. Contextualism Debate and Its Satellites
**Date**: 2026-06-01
**Total Literature Base**: ~50 usable papers across 4 domains (several INCOMPLETE entries excluded from citation; see notes)

---

## Overall Narrative Arc

The review is organized by **insight and debate**, not by domain. The spine is the appropriationism/contextualism axis (Domain 1). Around it the review weaves three further threads: (a) the meta-philosophical question of *why* do history of philosophy at all (Domain 3), treated as integral because positions on function drive positions on method; (b) the conceptual distinctions that structure the field (Domain 2: history of ideas, Cambridge School, analytic–continental); and (c) the concrete craft problems where the abstract debate becomes operational (Domain 4: charity, anachronism, canon). The argument builds toward a recurring claim made by recent literature — that the binary is unstable and that "function is prior to method" (van Ackeren) — and culminates in canon-critical (feminist, decolonial) perspectives that reframe the entire debate as also a question about *what* the history of philosophy is a history *of*.

A note on excluded sources: `skinner1969meaning`, `garber/della rocca` are usable, but several anchor and foundational entries are marked INCOMPLETE/no-abstract (Skinner 1969 in some files, Frede 1988, Cohen 1986, Mercer 2016, Van Norden 2017, Grandy 1973, the 2022 Methodology anchor volume). Where these are central, cite them via their verified note content but flag the need to attribute claims carefully and lean on the verified summarizing sources (Mercer 2019, Smith 2024) where possible.

## Introduction

**Purpose**: Frame the core dispute and establish why methodology in the history of philosophy is a live philosophical problem, not a parochial scholarly housekeeping matter.

**Content**:
- Open with the structuring dilemma stated in Rorty's own terms (from the abstract): either we anachronistically impose our problems on the dead to make them conversation partners, or we confine ourselves to making their views look less silly in context. This is the dilemma the whole literature negotiates.
- Name the two poles: appropriationism / rational reconstruction (treating past figures as colleagues whose arguments are assessed for validity) vs. contextualism / historical reconstruction (recovering meaning within the author's linguistic and intellectual setting).
- State the central scholarly fact: the debate has a canonical origin (Rorty 1984; Skinner 1969), a sharp recent flashpoint (the 2015 Garber–Della Rocca Spinoza exchange), and a retrospective assessment (Mercer 2019 on the "contextualist revolution").
- Preview the four threads: the central binary; the question of aims; the surrounding conceptual map; and concrete craft problems plus canon critique.
- Note the anchor volumes (Laerke/Smith/Schliesser 2013; Smith/Schliesser/Laerke 2022) as the gravitational center of contemporary discussion.

**Key Papers**: rorty1984historiography, skinner1969meaningUnderstanding, dellaRocca2015interpretingSpinoza, mercer2019contextualistRevolution, laerkeSmithSchliesser2013philosophyHistory
**Word Target**: 450-550 words

## Section 1: The Central Debate — Rational Reconstruction, Contextualism, and the Instability of the Binary

**Section Purpose**: Establish the two poles in their canonical and contemporary forms, then show that the most active recent literature argues the dichotomy is unsustainable.

**Main Claims**:
1. The poles are well-defined and each has a coherent rationale grounded in a different conception of what understanding a past text consists in.
2. The recent center of gravity is a cluster of "third-way" positions that reject the binary while disagreeing about what replaces it.

**Subsection 1.1: The Appropriationist Pole**
**Papers**: rorty1984historiography, dellaRocca2015interpretingSpinoza, frede2021philosophicalHistory, pickave2020defence
**Content**: Rorty's four-genre typology and his defense of rational reconstruction as legitimate-though-distorting. Della Rocca's PSR-driven Spinoza as the unapologetic contemporary instance ("the real is the rational"; context can mislead — what matters is argument validity). Frede's "philosophical history of philosophy" as a sophisticated appropriationism that still reads past figures as addressing genuine problems. Pickavé's defense that engaging contemporary tools need not entail anachronism.
**Connection**: Supplies the position the project must evaluate; note the internal tension (Rorty concedes distortion; Frede insists on selectivity's "dangers and limitations").

**Subsection 1.2: The Contextualist Challenge**
**Papers**: skinner1969meaningUnderstanding, skinner2002visions, garber2005whatPhilosophical
**Content**: Skinner's three mythologies (doctrines, coherence, prolepsis) and the speech-act account of meaning as illocutionary act recoverable only in linguistic context. Garber's distinction between "philosophy done historically" and "history done philosophically," and his claim that contextual understanding is itself philosophical because it reveals the contingency of present assumptions.
**Connection**: This is the case the appropriationist must answer; the Skinner→Garber transfer is the hinge by which a thesis about intellectual history becomes a thesis about the history of philosophy.

**Subsection 1.3: Critiques of Both Poles and the Third Way**
**Papers**: lamb2009skinnerContextualism, blau2020meaningsUnderstandings, hangai2023internalHistory, frede2021historiographyPhilosophy, antognazza2015benefit, corkum2023philosophyPast, ferreiraDaSilva2022historian, beaney2020twoDogmas, laerke2019frenchHistoriographical
**Content**: Lamb's charge that Skinner's anti-perennialism is unjustified and structurally repeats the errors it diagnoses. Blau's claim that Skinner conflated distinct senses of "meaning," leaving room for "extended" (philosophical) meaning. The "internal history" alternative (Frede/Mandelbaum via Hangai): prioritize philosophical reasons, admit context only in a principled, supplementary way. Antognazza's both/and (history as innovative and subversive for philosophy). Corkum's argument for the noncontingent, nonreplicable cognitive role of history. Ferreira da Silva's Brentanian "portrait painter" and the "Getting Things Right Constraint" (GTRC) as the principle the binary turns on. Beaney's "two dogmas of analytic historiography" rejecting both pure reconstruction and pure contextualism. Laerke's French "historiographical Spinozism" as a distinct living tradition.
**Connection**: Demonstrates that the field's frontier is post-binary; the project should position itself relative to *which* third way (GTRC-based, internal-history, integrative, pluralist).

**Section Summary**: The poles are clear but the binary is contested; what remains unresolved is whether a stable principled middle exists or whether "third way" names a family of incompatible compromises. Mercer's claim that "rational reconstructionism is a thing of the past" (1.4 below) is both a data point and a contestable verdict.

**Word Target**: 1300-1500 words

## Section 2: Why Do It At All — Aims as Prior to Method

**Section Purpose**: Show that disagreements about method are downstream of disagreements about function, and survey the functional positions. This is integral, not an afterthought: it explains *why* the binary resists resolution.

**Main Claims**:
1. Van Ackeren's thesis — function is prior to method — reframes the whole debate; the appropriationism/contextualism dispute is partly a proxy for disagreement about aims.
2. The functional positions form a spectrum (dispensable / advantageous / constitutive) crosscut by distinctive roles (defamiliarization, genealogy, therapy).

**Subsection 2.1: The Spectrum from Dispensable to Constitutive**
**Papers**: vanAckeren2018introduction, glock2008analyticHistory, nichols2006worthStudying, watson2002whatIsHistory, cohen1986doingPhilosophy, williams2000humanistic
**Content**: Van Ackeren's three positions and the priority-of-function thesis. Glock's "pragmatic historicism" (history is advantageous, not indispensable, and analytic engagement can be good history). Nichols's skepticism toward standard instrumentalist justifications. Williams's distinction between philosophy as humanistic vs. scientific discipline — the deepest argument that history is *constitutive* because philosophy's concepts are historically constituted. Cohen's strong constitutivism (flag INCOMPLETE — attribute cautiously). Watson on the autonomous-discipline view.
**Connection**: Maps the functional commitments that silently determine where a scholar lands on method.

**Subsection 2.2: Distinctive Critical Functions — Defamiliarization, Genealogy, Therapy**
**Papers**: perler2018alienation, geuss2017changingSubject, williams2000humanistic, fischer2011therapy, piercey2009usesOfPast, ooi2024permissivism
**Content**: Perler's Brechtian "alienation effect" — history denaturalizes present problems (and note: this is a *contextualist* method serving an *appropriationist*-adjacent critical aim, complicating the binary). Geuss's and Williams's genealogy as critique of the present. Fischer's therapeutic dissolution of pseudo-problems. Piercey's comparative map (Heidegger/Gadamer/MacIntyre/Rorty) of "uses of the past." Ooi's recent permissivism: multiple aims may be equally rational, motivating hermeneutic pluralism.
**Connection**: These functions cut across the binary — defamiliarization needs contextual accuracy but serves present philosophical self-understanding — supporting the review's thesis that aims, not the pole-labels, do the real work.

**Section Summary**: If function is prior to method and functions are plural (Ooi, van Ackeren), the appropriationism/contextualism question may be mis-posed as a single binary; what remains open is whether permissivism is satisfying or merely defers the normative question.

**Word Target**: 900-1100 words

## Section 3: The Conceptual Map — History of Ideas, Cambridge School, and the Analytic–Continental Divide

**Section Purpose**: Situate the central debate within neighboring distinctions, showing it is not an isolated intra-philosophical quarrel. Keep this tighter than Sections 1–2.

**Main Claims**:
1. The history of philosophy is demarcated from, but entangled with, the history of ideas and intellectual history; the demarcations are less clean than their founders claimed.
2. The analytic–continental divide and hermeneutics supply differing self-understandings that shape historiographical commitments.

**Subsection 3.1: History of Ideas vs. History of Philosophy**
**Papers**: mandelbaum1965history, catana2010lovejoy, knight2012unit, shogimen2016elusiveness
**Content**: Mandelbaum's three-way taxonomy (history of ideas / intellectual history / history of philosophy). Catana's claim that Lovejoy's demarcation was strategic rather than real. Knight's neo-Lovejovian rehabilitation of unit-ideas as a middle position. Shogimen's "elusiveness of context" — the contextualist pole faces its own underdetermination problem (which context?).
**Connection**: Shows the contextualist pole is methodologically underdetermined, strengthening Section 1's instability claim.

**Subsection 3.2: Analytic, Continental, and Hermeneutic Self-Understandings**
**Papers**: beaney2016historiography, simons2001fault, harrelson2014inferentialist, peden2019truth, frazer2018ethics
**Content**: Beaney on the "historical turn" in analytic philosophy and the early analytics' dismissive self-image. Simons on the contingency of the analytic–continental rift (so the historiographical difference may be sociological, not principled). Harrelson's inferentialist (Brandom) mediation; Peden's Davidsonian dissolution of ambiguities in Skinner. Frazer's reframing of the historical/presentist dispute as *ethical* rather than methodological — a disagreement about scholarly responsibility, not technique.
**Connection**: Frazer's ethical reframing and the philosophy-of-language mediations (Harrelson, Peden) give the project tools to argue the binary is partly a category mistake.

**Section Summary**: The surrounding distinctions reveal both poles as underdetermined and partly artifacts of disciplinary sociology; the demarcation questions feed back into the central debate.

**Word Target**: 700-850 words

## Section 4: Methodology in Practice — Charity, Anachronism, and the Politics of the Canon

**Section Purpose**: Ground the abstract debate in craft decisions, then escalate to canon critique as the point where method becomes politics — the project's required critical (feminist, decolonial) dimension.

**Main Claims**:
1. The interpretive tools that supposedly arbitrate the debate (charity, anachronism) are themselves indeterminate and conceal the very choices they claim to settle.
2. Canon formation is a historiographical act with epistemic and political consequences; feminist and decolonial critiques reframe "the history of philosophy" itself.

**Subsection 4.1: Charity and Anachronism**
**Papers**: davidson1984inquiries, grandy1973reference, stern2016nietzsche, jardine2000uses, pickave2020defence, harari2025historical, kaukua2010subjectivity, schumacher2012adam
**Content**: Davidson's charity as constitutive prerequisite vs. Grandy's "principle of humanity" (minimize psychological strangeness rather than maximize truth) — note both INCOMPLETE; attribute via notes. Stern's demonstration that charity is multiply ambiguous (unit/mode/strength) and so cannot bridge "right meaning" and "best philosophy." Jardine's taxonomy of productive vs. distorting anachronism. Pickavé's defense of moderate anachronism (cross-ref Section 1). Harari's distinction between contextualization-for-understanding and contextualization-for-judgment. Kaukua & Lähteenmäki's "non-textual standards" as a third class beyond text and context. Schumacher's history-of-economics case study of doxographic ("Whig") distortion as a concrete illustration.
**Connection**: The arbitrating tools do not arbitrate; they relocate the disagreement — reinforcing the priority-of-aims thesis.

**Subsection 4.2: Canon Formation and Its Critics**
**Papers**: shapiro2016revisitingCanon, lapointe2023historiography, smith2024historiography, mercer2016descartes, tyson2014exclusion, dotson2012how, vannorden2017taking, ganeri2025blueprint
**Content**: Shapiro on how appropriationist canon-fixing entrenched seven core figures and excluded women; Lapointe & Reck debunking analytic philosophy's ahistorical self-image and showing canons are constructions; Smith 2024 tracing the post-Kantian (Tennemann) formation of the "standard story" and its joint exclusion of women (O'Neill) and non-European thought (Park). Feminist reclamation: Mercer 2016 (Teresa→Descartes; exclusion has *epistemic* not merely equity costs — flag INCOMPLETE); Tyson's critique that additive inclusion can reinforce exclusion, only structural transformation corrects it; Dotson's "culture of justification." Decolonial/cosmopolitan: Van Norden's multicultural manifesto (flag INCOMPLETE) and Ganeri 2025's cosmopolitan post-Eurocentric blueprint.
**Connection**: This is where the project's critical mandate lands: the appropriationism/contextualism debate presupposes a canon whose composition is itself contestable; choosing a method does not settle *whose* texts get methodized.

**Section Summary**: Craft problems are not neutral solvents for the debate; canon critique shows the debate has been conducted over a pre-selected, contestable object. Open question: can contextualism's own historical rigor be turned against the Eurocentric canon (Smith 2024 suggests yes), making canon-expansion a contextualist rather than appropriationist project?

**Word Target**: 1000-1200 words

## Conclusion

**Purpose**: Synthesize the state of debate and position the project.

**Content**:
- Restate the trajectory: a once-sharp binary (Rorty/Skinner → Garber/Della Rocca) has given way to a consensus that the binary is unstable, with multiple incompatible "third ways" (internal history, integrative, GTRC-based, permissivist).
- Foreground the project's organizing insight: van Ackeren's priority of function over method, combined with the finding (Stern, Shogimen, Frazer) that the arbitrating tools — charity, context, anachronism — are themselves indeterminate or ethical rather than technical. This explains *why* the debate resists resolution.
- Identify open tensions: (1) Is methodological permissivism (Ooi) a genuine resolution or an evasion of the normative question? (2) Does Mercer's verdict that "rational reconstructionism is a thing of the past" hold, given live appropriationist defenses (Della Rocca, Frede, Pickavé, Corkum)? (3) Can the contextualists' own commitments be turned to canon expansion (Smith 2024, Shapiro), reframing the decolonial/feminist challenge as internal to rather than opposed to rigorous historiography?
- State the contribution: the project's analytical value lies in showing that the appropriationism/contextualism axis, the aims debate, and the canon debate are not three topics but one — each is a way of asking what the history of philosophy is *for* and *of*.

**Key Papers**: vanAckeren2018introduction, mercer2019contextualistRevolution, stern2016nietzsche, smith2024historiography, ganeri2025blueprint
**Word Target**: 450-550 words
