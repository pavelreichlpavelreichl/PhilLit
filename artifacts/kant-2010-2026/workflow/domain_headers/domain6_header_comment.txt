DOMAIN_OVERVIEW:
2025 and 2026 contain few works with accumulated citations, so significance in this domain rests on venue, publisher, series, author standing, reviews and early citations, all recorded as data in each entry. The largest visible outputs are Cambridge University Press Elements and monographs (about two fifths of the entries), Oxford, Princeton and Bloomsbury books, and articles in Nous, Philosophy and Phenomenological Research, the Journal of the History of Philosophy, the European Journal of Philosophy, Kant-Studien and Mind. Three features stand out in the 2025 entries: continued attention to the origin and structure of the first Critique (Anderson on Hume and the Antinomy, Stang on Kant's critique of metaphysics and contemporary meta-metaphysics, Hogan on the Nutshell argument), applied and contextual work on the Metaphysics of Morals (Timmermann on the right to lie, Sabourin on marriage, Williams on corporations and Kant), and the Kant-and-race debate (Kleingeld's Mind critical notice of Lu-Adler's book).

The 2026 entries (through 2026-09-30) add a historicist and religion-centred reading of the whole critical project (Hunter, The Kantian Religion, Princeton), a collected-essays volume on transcendental idealism and method by Guyer, a Kant-and-Freud study of the mind (Longuenesse), a reassessment of the Prolegomena-Groundwork parallel (Kleingeld, Kant-Studien), pre-critical metaphysics read with grounding theory (Schnieder), a Kantian challenge to predictive processing (Schlicht), the law-morality question in the Doctrine of Right (Brecher and Hirsch, an open-access CUP volume), moral feeling (Noller), autonomy and life in Kant and Hegel (Khurana), art criticism (Tuna) and Kant's relation to colonial slavery and racial capitalism (Lu-Adler, online first). Trends named in the plan are only partly visible: Kant and technology appears mainly in articles screened out for significance (for example Sticker on the Formula of Humanity and AI use, Philosophy and Technology 2026), and consolidation work on Kant and the Aristotelian, Stoic and Hegelian traditions appears in CUP volumes (Gobsch and Land, The Aristotelian Kant; Merritt, Kant's Transformation of Stoic Philosophy; both not included for the quota).

Few works respond explicitly to the dominant readings of the 2010s; the clearest cases are Hunter (against the systematic-philosophical reading), Khurana (against the self-legislation model of autonomy), Guyer (criticism of Kant's argument for transcendental idealism while defending the transcendental method) and Schlicht (against predictive-processing realism).
