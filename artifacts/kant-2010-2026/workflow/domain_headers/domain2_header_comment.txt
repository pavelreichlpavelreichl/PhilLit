DOMAIN_OVERVIEW:
Three debates in theoretical philosophy run through the block. The two-aspect (one-world) versus two-world readings of transcendental idealism are represented by Marshall (2013), Stang (2014) and Allais's Manifest Reality (2015); the conceptualism and nonconceptualism question, extended by naive-realist readings of perception, by Tolley (2013), Gomes (2014), Onof and Schulting (2015) and Stephenson (2015); and the reading of the Deduction and the Paralogisms in historical context by Dyck (2014) and Allison (2015). The empirical side of Kant's thought is prominent: Friedman's Construction of Nature (2013), Frierson's empirical psychology (2014) and the anthropology and biology volumes of Cohen and of Goy and Watkins (2014), together with Mensch on epigenesis (2013), Watkins on the early Kant and Newton (2013), McNulty on chemical laws (2015) and Hall on the Opus postumum (2014).

In practical philosophy the entries move between moral psychology and motivation (Grenberg 2013, Wuerth 2013 and 2014, Ware 2014), the Lectures on Ethics (Denis and Sensen, dated 2014 by CrossRef), commentary (Trampota et al. on the Doctrine of Virtue, 2013; Schönecker and Wood on the Groundwork, 2015) and the constructivism and realism question (Wuerth's moral realism, O'Neill's constructivist reading of reason). Political and critical work is led by Maliks (2014) and by Flikschuh and Ypi's Kant and Colonialism (2014), with Mikkelsen's translation of the race essays (2013), Mills on race (2014) and Hay on Kantian feminism (2013). Reception is covered by Golob (Heidegger on Kant, 2013), Beiser (neo-Kantianism, 2014) and Gardner and Grist (2015), and reference work by the Pisa Kant-Kongress proceedings (2013), the Kant-Lexikon (2015) and Reading Kant's Lectures (2015).

Year dates follow the CrossRef print date. Denis and Sensen (print 2014-12-31) and Ginsborg (print 2014-11-27), which are conventionally cited as 2015, therefore fall in 2014; Maliks (SEP 2015) and Palmquist (SEP 2016) also differ from their CrossRef print dates, and articles that were online-first in an earlier year (for example Gomes, Stang, Matherne, Wuerth) are dated by their print volume. Each case is explained in the entry's YEAR NOTE.

RELEVANCE_TO_PROJECT:
The block records the empirical-science and moral-psychology strand, the post-2012 perception debates and a volume-length treatment of Kant and colonialism (Flikschuh and Ypi). It also contains the monographs of the period on transcendental idealism and the Deduction (Allais, Allison) and on Kant's mind (Dyck, Frierson, Wuerth) that carry NDPR or multiple journal reviews and SEP listings.
