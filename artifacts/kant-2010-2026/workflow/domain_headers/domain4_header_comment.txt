DOMAIN_OVERVIEW:
Theoretical philosophy in 2019-2021 moves from commentary on the Critiques toward book-length reconstructions of single doctrines and systematic structure. Watkins's Kant on Laws (2019) argues for one generic concept of law instantiated in nature and morality; Abaci's modality book (2019) and Fugate's volume on the metaphysics lectures (2019) treat the precritical and critical metaphysics; de Boer (2020) reads the first Critique as a reform of Wolffian metaphysics and Moller (2020) reads its legal metaphor as a source of normativity; Posy and Rechter's first volume (2020) and Sutherland's Kant's Mathematical World (2021) give the philosophy of mathematics book-length treatments in the block. The interpretation of transcendental idealism is addressed by Jauernig's The World According to Kant (2021), which defends a two-level ontological reading and is cited in the IEP entry on transcendental idealism and the SEP entry on transcendental arguments; Proops reads the Dialectic as a metallurgical assay of metaphysics (2021); Hogan (2021) ties the argument from incongruent counterparts to freedom; Ypi (2021) reads the Architectonic through purposiveness; Schafer (2021) offers a capacities-first reading of transcendental philosophy. Self-knowledge and the mind are covered by Kraus (2019 article, 2020 book), Cohen on emotions as feelings (2020), van Mazijk on Kant, Husserl and McDowell (2020) and Schulting on apperception (2021).

In practical philosophy the block turns on autonomy and normativity (Kleingeld and Willaschek 2019 on autonomy without paradox, Schafer 2019 on constitutivism, Ware 2021 on the justification of ethics), on demandingness and the formula of humanity (Formosa and Sticker 2019, Kleingeld 2020), and on the Yale Groundwork edition with essays by Schneewind, Baron, Kagan and Wood (2019). Allison's Kant's Conception of Freedom (2020) traces the doctrine from the 1750s to the 1790s. Religion is covered by Insole on free belief (2019), Wood's Kant and Religion (2020) and Merritt on Stoic sources of radical evil (2021). Political and legal work includes Huseyinzadegan's Nonideal Theory (2019), Valdez on Du Bois and cosmopolitanism (2019), Loriaux on global distributive justice (2020) and Ripstein's Kant and the Law of War (2021). The widening of the field that the plan hypothesised is visible: Kleingeld on how to read Kant's sexism and racism (2019), Valdez (2019), Varden's Sex, Love, and Gender (2020), Allais and Callanan's Kant and Animals (2020), Basevich on race and nonideal theory (2020), Sabourin on women and enlightenment (2021) and Yab on race and cosmopolitan right (2021). Aesthetics and teleology are represented by Fisher on organisms (2019), Halper on art and natural beauty (2020) and Makkai (2021); reception by Ameriks (2019), Biagioli on Cassirer (2020), van Mazijk (2020) and Schulting (2021); reference work by the Cambridge Kant Lexicon and the 13th Kant Congress proceedings (both 2021).

Year dates follow the CrossRef print date. Online-first items whose print year falls in the block (Allison, Cohen, Schafer 2021, Merritt 2021, among others) are dated by the print volume or print date and the difference is explained in the entry's YEAR NOTE; their DOIs are carried in the url field because the metadata cleaner would otherwise overwrite the year with the earlier online year. Sutherland's Kant's Mathematical World has CrossRef print date 2021-10-31 and is dated 2021. The plan's seeds were confirmed as follows: Watkins 2019, Varden 2020, Allais and Callanan 2020, Posy and Rechter 2020 (volume 1), Jauernig 2021 and Sutherland 2021 are in the file; Schafer's Philosophers' Imprint seed ("Realism and Constructivism in Kant's Metaphysics of Morals") was not found by title search in CrossRef; Brandom's A Spirit of Trust (2019) was found but is a book on Hegel and was not included; Baiasu and Timmons (eds.), The Kantian Mind, appears in S2 with year 2023 and was not verified for this block.

RELEVANCE_TO_PROJECT:
The block records the consolidation of systematic, metaphysically oriented readings of transcendental idealism and the critical system (Jauernig, Proops, de Boer, Watkins), the first book-length treatments of Kant's philosophy of mathematics, and the entry of sex and gender, animals, race and nonideal theory into the monograph literature, while ethics work centres on autonomy, normativity and demandingness.
