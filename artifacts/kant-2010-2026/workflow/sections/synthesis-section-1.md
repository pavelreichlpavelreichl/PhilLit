## Introduction

This review lists selected scholarship on Kant published from 2010 through 30 September 2026: books, edited volumes, editions and translations, and journal articles, mainly English-language, with a few German-language reference works, proceedings and editions. Entries are organised by year, each with a short summary.

An entry had to be verified in tool output (Semantic Scholar, CrossRef, PhilPapers via Brave search, CORE, NDPR, SEP and IEP), be centred on Kant (or, for reception works, have him as a principal interlocutor), carry a publication year within the period (CrossRef print year preferred), not duplicate another entry, and meet at least one recorded significance indicator. The indicators are data, not verdicts: citation counts relative to year and subfield (retrieved 30 September to 1 October 2026), publisher and venue, NDPR and journal reviews, prizes confirmed by a source page, mention in SEP or IEP, status as a new edition or translation, and a documented role in a debate. The selection is not exhaustive: 251 entries (13 in 2010 and 2026, 14 in 2016, 16 in 2017, 15 in every other year); other works were screened out on indicators or the per-year cap.

Each year opens with a short overview, then lists entries by subfield; "continues the debate of" notes appear only where the annotations support them.

The list has limits. Twenty-six entries lack a retrievable abstract, so their summaries rest on NDPR reviews, publisher or journal pages, or snippets. For 2025 and 2026 most citation counts lie between 0 and 6, so significance rests on publisher, venue, reviews and author record; no NDPR review dated 2026 was found. OpenAlex was unavailable and not used for discovery. Education and disability have no entry; animals have three; the Opus postumum has eight, none in 2010-2013, 2015, 2018, 2020, 2021 or 2026; editions and translations number 13, none in 2010, 2014, 2020, 2021, 2023 or 2026. Dates follow the CrossRef print date, so some works, such as Denis and Sensen (eds.) (2014), appear a year earlier than conventionally cited.
